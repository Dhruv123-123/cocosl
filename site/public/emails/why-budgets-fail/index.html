Subject: Why your last budget died in week three
Preview text: It wasn't willpower. It was the design.

Hi,

If you've tried budgeting before and quit, here's something that might help: it probably wasn't you.

Most budgets ask you to manage a dozen categories every single day. Groceries, eating out, fun, household, transport, "misc". Every coffee is a decision. By the third week of the month, the categories that felt huge on the 1st are empty, you can't tell where it went, and opening the spreadsheet just feels bad. So you stop.

Three things make a budget stick instead:

  1. Fewer decisions. One number to watch, not twelve.
  2. A short feedback loop. Weekly, so a bad week is a small problem, not a ruined month.
  3. Automation for everything that doesn't change. Bills and savings move on their own.

That's the idea behind Day 7 of your reset: split your pay into bills, funds, goals and one weekly spending number. The first three run on autopilot. You only watch the fourth.

Example (fictional): Maya takes home $3,050 a month. After bills, funds and goals, she has $832 left for spending. $832 × 12 ÷ 52 = $192 a week. Groceries, takeout, a concert: all of it comes from $192. When it's gone, the week's done.

The full method, with the maths: How to make a budget you'll actually keep.

If you're on schedule, today is Day 3: turn on a low-balance alert, a large-transaction alert, and a payment-due alert on every card. Ten minutes, free, and your accounts start warning you instead of surprising you.

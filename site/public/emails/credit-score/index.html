Subject: Paid in full, score still dropped?
Preview text: Your card has two dates. Only one of them gets reported.

Hi,

Here's a confusing one: you pay your card in full, never pay a cent of interest, and your credit score still dips.

The reason is two dates.

  1. Statement closing date: when the month's statement is generated. The balance on this date is usually what gets reported to the credit bureaus.
  2. Payment due date: usually a few weeks later.

Spend $1,500 on a $2,000 limit and pay it all on the due date, and the bureaus may already have seen 75% utilization. Utilization (how much of your limits you're using) is a big part of the "amounts owed" category, about 30% of a FICO score.

The fix: in a big-spending month, make a payment before the statement closing date. The dip isn't permanent either; once a lower balance is reported, it usually bounces back.

Two more myths while we're here:

  • You don't need to carry a balance to build credit. Paying interest doesn't help your score. Pay in full.
  • Closing an old, no-fee card can hurt. It shrinks your total available credit, so utilization rises. Often better to keep it and use it for one small purchase every few months on autopay.

How to find (and cancel) forgotten subscriptions in 10 minutes

Search your statements for a handful of words, check your app stores, and use the cancel-or-keep test. A 10-minute audit that usually finds money leaking.

Subscriptions are designed to be easy to start and easy to forget. A free trial converts, a price goes up a dollar, a yearly renewal lands on a card you rarely check. Individually they're small. Together they're often one of the easiest monthly savings you'll find.

Here's a 10-minute audit.

Step 1: Search your statements (4 minutes)

Open your bank and card apps (all of them, including the old card in the drawer) and search the last 90 days for:

  • "monthly", "subscription", "renewal", "membership"
  • "recurring", "auto-pay", "premium", "plus", "pro"
  • The names of app stores: "Apple", "Google", "PayPal"

Then scroll the last 12 months for any charge that appears once a year. Annual renewals are the ones people miss.

Step 2: Check the app stores (2 minutes)

Many subscriptions bill through your phone, so they show up on statements as "Apple" or "Google" with no detail.

  • iPhone: Settings > [your name] > Subscriptions.
  • Android: Google Play Store > profile icon > Payments & subscriptions > Subscriptions.
  • PayPal: Settings > Payments > Manage automatic payments.

Step 3: Make a list (2 minutes)

Write down each subscription, its cost, and convert yearly ones to monthly (divide by 12). Total it.

Step 4: The cancel-or-keep test (2 minutes)

For each one, ask: "If this weren't already running, would I sign up for it today at this price?"

  • No: cancel it now, while you're looking at it.
  • Maybe: set a calendar reminder for one week before the next charge, and decide then.
  • Yes: keep it, guilt-free. Move it into your fixed bills.

Bonus: for keepers, check whether an annual plan, a student discount or a family plan is cheaper, and whether you're paying for the same thing twice (two music services, a phone plan that already includes a streaming service).

Hard to cancel?

US rules require sellers to clearly disclose the terms of recurring charges and, under the federal Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, to give you a simple way to stop them. If a company makes it difficult:

  1. Try cancelling the same way you signed up (online, in-app).
  2. Keep a screenshot or confirmation email.
  3. If charges continue, dispute them with your card issuer and consider reporting it to the FTC.

Make it stick

Add a 5-minute subscription scan to your monthly money check-in, and set a bank alert for any charge over a set amount so new ones can't slip by. If you're building a budget at the same time, put the subscriptions you kept in the "Bills" line of the four-line budget.

Common questions

What's the fastest way to see all subscriptions?

Your card and bank statements plus your app store subscription page cover nearly everything. Some banking apps also flag recurring charges automatically.

Should I use a subscription-cancelling app?

Be careful. Some charge a monthly fee or a share of your "savings" and need access to your accounts. The manual audit above is free and takes ten minutes.

Will cancelling a subscription affect my credit score?

No. Ordinary subscriptions aren't reported to credit bureaus (unless an unpaid bill goes to collections).

How to move your savings to a high-yield account in 15 minutes

Step by step: what to check, how to link and transfer, how long it takes, and the setup that keeps your emergency fund growing.

November 12, 2026 · 6 min read · Edited and fact-checked by The Money Made Simple editor

If your savings sit in the account your bank gave you by default, they may be earning close to nothing. Moving them to a high-yield savings account (HYSA) takes about 15 minutes, and you don't have to close anything.

Still choosing an account? Start with our high-yield savings comparison, which covers what to compare and lists five well-known options side by side. This guide is the next step: actually making the switch.

Before you start: three quick checks

  1. It's insured. Look for "Member FDIC" (or NCUA for a credit union) and find the bank in the FDIC's BankFind tool. If it's an app, check who really holds the money.
  2. You'll get the advertised rate. Some rates need a minimum balance or direct deposit. Read the rate table, not the banner.
  3. No monthly fee at your balance.

Step by step

  1. Have these ready: your ID, Social Security number, and your current bank's routing and account numbers (in your banking app, or on a check).
  2. Open the account online. It usually takes 5–10 minutes. Opening a deposit account typically doesn't involve a hard credit check, though some banks use a check of your banking history.
  3. Link your current checking account. Most banks verify it instantly through your bank login, or with two tiny test deposits that take a day or two.
  4. Start the transfer from the new bank. Pulling money in from the new bank is often faster and has higher limits than pushing it from the old one. Expect 1–3 business days.
  5. Leave a buffer in checking. Keep enough for a week or two of bills, so a late transfer never causes an overdraft.
  6. Automate a small transfer. Even $25 a week, set for the day after payday, so saving happens without a decision. Building an emergency fund.
  7. Name the account. "Emergency fund" or "Car repairs" makes you less likely to dip into it. Some banks let you split one account into goals, like sinking funds.

How much difference does it make?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Closing the old account too early. Wait until the money has arrived and any automatic payments have moved.
  • Chasing every rate change. Moving accounts for 0.1% isn't worth your time. Check the rate twice a year.
  • Keeping the HYSA one tap away from spending. A separate bank from your checking adds a helpful day of friction.

Common questions

How long does it take to open a high-yield savings account?

Usually 5–10 minutes online. Your first transfer typically arrives in 1–3 business days.

Does opening a savings account affect my credit score?

Usually not. Banks often check your banking history instead, which isn't a credit inquiry. Some may do a soft check.

Is the interest taxed?

Yes. Interest is taxable income, and banks send a 1099-INT if you earn $10 or more in a year.

How much should I keep in checking?

Enough to cover a week or two of bills and spending, so transfers never leave you short.

High-yield savings vs money market account vs CD: which one for your savings?

Three insured places for cash, three trade-offs. What each is, when each wins, and why a money market fund is different.

You've got savings and you want it earning something. The three usual choices are a high-yield savings account, a money market account and a CD. All three can be insured bank deposits. They differ in how easy the money is to reach and how the rate works.

High-yield savings accounts

The simplest option. Money in, interest paid monthly, transfer out when you need it. The rate floats with the market, so it can fall as well as rise. For an emergency fund, this is the default choice for most people. How to pick one.

Money market accounts

A money market account (MMA) is a bank deposit, insured like savings. Many come with checks or a debit card, which makes them handy for big, irregular payments. Some pay more on bigger balances and charge a fee below a minimum. If you'd never use the checks, a HYSA is usually simpler.

Don't mix it up with a money market fund. A money market fund is an investment you buy through a brokerage. It aims to hold a stable value and often pays a competitive yield, but it isn't a bank deposit and isn't FDIC-insured. It can be a fine place for cash inside an investment account; just know which one you have.

CDs (certificates of deposit)

You agree to leave the money for a set term (say 6 months to 5 years) and the bank fixes your rate for that time. If rates drop, you keep your rate. If you need the money early, you usually pay an early-withdrawal penalty, often several months of interest.

CDs suit money with a known date: a house deposit in 18 months, tuition next fall. A "CD ladder" (several CDs ending at different times) gives you a fixed rate with some money coming free regularly.

Which one should you use?

  • Emergency fund: high-yield savings. You need to reach it quickly, and a penalty in an emergency hurts.
  • Saving for something in 1–3 years: a HYSA, or a CD if you're sure of the date and the CD rate is higher.
  • Large cash balance and you write checks: a money market account can be convenient. Compare its rate with a HYSA.
  • Cash waiting to be invested inside a brokerage: a money market fund, understanding it's not insured.

Many people end up with two: a HYSA for the emergency fund and a CD or two for dated goals.

Accounts to compare

Most online banks offer all three. Ally, CIT Bank and Capital One 360 all publish savings, money market and CD rates on their sites, so you can compare like for like in one place. We haven't opened these accounts; check live rates and terms before choosing.

Common questions

Is a money market account better than a high-yield savings account?

Not automatically. Compare the rates and the minimums. If the MMA pays the same or less and you won't use the checks, the HYSA is simpler.

Can I lose money in a CD?

Not on the principal at an insured bank, but an early withdrawal can cost you some interest, and on a very short holding period the penalty can dip into principal at some banks. Read the penalty terms.

Are money market funds safe?

They're designed to be low-risk and keep a stable value, but they're investments, not deposits, and aren't FDIC-insured.

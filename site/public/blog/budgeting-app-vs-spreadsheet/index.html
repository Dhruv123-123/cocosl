Budgeting apps

Pros

  • Transactions arrive automatically, so nothing is missed.
  • Good charts, alerts and goal tracking.
  • Great for spotting where money actually goes, especially in the first month.

Cons

  • Many good ones charge a subscription, and prices can rise.
  • Bank connections break and need re-linking.
  • Automatic categorising makes it easy to never actually look. A budget you don't look at isn't doing anything.
  • You're sharing financial data with another company. Check what they collect and whether they sell it.

Best for: people with lots of transactions across several accounts, couples sharing finances, and anyone who loves dashboards and will check them.

Spreadsheets

Pros

  • Free or cheap, and fully yours. No bank access needed.
  • As simple or detailed as you want.
  • Typing the numbers forces a moment of attention, which is where behaviour actually changes.

Cons

  • Setup takes time if you start from a blank sheet.
  • No automatic transactions.
  • Easy to over-engineer into something you dread opening.

Best for: people who want control and privacy, and who prefer a few minutes a week over a daily feed.

Notebook

Pros

  • Zero tech, zero cost, zero distractions.
  • Writing by hand is a surprisingly strong habit trigger for some people.

Cons

  • Maths is manual; no history or charts without effort.
  • Harder to plan debt payoff or long-term goals.

Best for: people who already journal, or anyone who's bounced off apps and spreadsheets.

How to choose in two minutes

  1. How many transactions do you have a week? Under ~25 on one or two cards: any option works. Lots across many accounts: an app saves time.
  2. What made you quit last time? Too much effort: use the weekly-number method with any tool, so you track one number. Ignored the app: try a spreadsheet or notebook.
  3. Are you comfortable linking your bank? If not, it's a spreadsheet or notebook.

Whatever you choose, the method matters more than the tool. A simple system you check for 20 minutes a week beats a perfect app you never open.

Common questions

Are free budgeting apps safe?

Many use read-only connections through data aggregators, but "free" apps may make money from your data or by showing you financial offers. Read the privacy policy before linking accounts.

Can I use Google Sheets for budgeting?

Yes. Google Sheets is free and works on your phone. Most Excel templates open in Sheets.

How often should I update my budget?

Weekly for spending, monthly for the overall plan.

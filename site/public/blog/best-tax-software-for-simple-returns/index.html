The cheapest ways to file a simple tax return (start with the free ones)

For a simple return you may pay nothing. VITA, IRS Free File, FreeTaxUSA and TaxAct compared, and how to avoid surprise fees.

A "simple" return usually means W-2 income, maybe some interest, student loan interest and the standard deduction. If that's you, filing should cost little or nothing. The trick is starting in the right place, because many "free" products charge as soon as your return has one extra form.

How to avoid paying by accident

  1. Check what "free" covers before you start. Some free tiers stop at student loan interest, HSAs or 1099 income, and ask you to upgrade halfway through.
  2. Start from IRS.gov for Free File. Going straight to a company's homepage can land you on a paid product. The FTC found in 2024 that Intuit's "free" TurboTax ads were deceptive.
  3. Decline add-ons like audit defence and refund-transfer fees (paying your prep fee from your refund often costs extra).
  4. Have your documents ready: W-2s, 1099s, 1098-E for student loans, last year's return.

When to pay for help

If you started freelancing, sold investments, moved states or bought a home, a paid tier or a tax professional can be worth it. Freelancers: also think about quarterly estimated taxes for next year.

Common questions

What is the cheapest way to file taxes?

If you qualify, VITA or IRS Free File is free. Otherwise, a low-cost product like FreeTaxUSA usually costs only the state fee.

Can I file my taxes for free if I have 1099 income?

Often, yes. FreeTaxUSA's free federal return includes self-employment income, and VITA can help with some simple self-employment returns.

When does tax season start?

The IRS usually starts accepting returns in late January. The deadline for most people is April 15.

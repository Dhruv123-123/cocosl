How to budget on a $55,000 salary

$55,000 a year is $4,583 a month before tax and about $3,864 after federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare. Here's a plan for that money, how much rent it supports, and how much your state changes it.

$55,000 is enough to cover the basics with room for goals, if fixed bills stay around half of take-home. The biggest lever is rent: at $1,375 a month (30% of gross) you keep the plan below balanced. Paying more than that is common, but every $100 over comes straight out of the goals or weekly lines.

For comparison, the 50/30/20 rule would give $1,932 for needs, $1,159 for wants and $773 for savings.

Emergency fund target

Three months of the bills line is $5,797; six months is $11,593. Putting the whole goals line toward it, the three-month version takes about 10 months. Keep it in a high-yield savings account so it earns something while it waits. More in how to build an emergency fund.

What a 401(k) costs you at this salary

Contributing 5% ($2,750 a year) lowers your take-home by only about $2,420 a year, $93 a paycheck, because traditional 401(k) money goes in before federal income tax. If your employer matches 50% of the first 6%, contributing 6% costs you about $2,904 a year in take-home and adds $1,650 of employer money. See how a 401(k) match works.

Your tax bracket on $55,000

Your taxable income (salary minus the $16,100 standard deduction) is $38,900, which puts your top dollars in the 12% federal bracket. Your average federal rate is only 8.0%, because each bracket only applies to the dollars inside it. You could earn $11,500 more before any dollar is taxed at 22%, and even then only the dollars above that line are. A raise never lowers your take-home pay.

Common questions

How much is $55,000 a year after taxes?

About $46,372 a year, or $3,864 a month, for a single filer in a state with no income tax in 2026, after $4,420 of federal income tax and $4,208 of Social Security and Medicare. In California it's about $3,752 a month; in New York City about $3,525.

How much is $55,000 a year per hour?

About $26 an hour before tax, based on 40 hours a week for 52 weeks (2,080 hours). After federal tax and FICA it's about $22 an hour.

How much rent can I afford on $55,000?

The common 30%-of-gross rule gives $1,375 a month. Measured against take-home pay, a 35% cap gives about $1,353 with no state income tax. Either way, rent plus your other fixed bills should stay near 50% of take-home for the plan on this page to work.

How much should I save on $55,000?

A starting point is 15% of take-home pay, about $580 a month, split between an emergency fund of $5,797 to $11,593 (three to six months of bills) and retirement. If your employer matches 401(k) contributions, contribute enough to get the full match first.

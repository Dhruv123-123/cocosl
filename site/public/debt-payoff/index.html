Debt payoff calculator: snowball vs avalanche

Enter your debts and what you can pay each month. You'll see when you'd be debt-free with each method and what it costs in interest. Below it, three worked examples show how the numbers play out.

How it works: you pay a fixed total each month (all the minimums plus your extra). The extra goes to one target debt; when a debt is paid off, its minimum rolls on to the next one. Avalanche targets the highest rate first, snowball the smallest balance. More in debt avalanche vs snowball.

Worked example: $5,000 of debt

DebtBalanceAPRMinimum
Card A$2,75024.99%$82
Card B$1,50019.99%$45
Store card$75029.99%$25

The situation: three cards, $5,000 in total, and $152 a month of minimums. Paying just those (and rolling each one into the next as cards clear) takes 4 years 7 months and costs $3,352 in interest: two-thirds of the original debt again.

Say this person cancels two subscriptions and moves to a cheaper phone plan, which frees $150 a month. Paying $302 a month instead, the debt is gone in 21 months with $1,079 of interest. The extra $150 a month saves about $2,270 and nearly three years.

The method barely matters here. The store card is both the smallest balance and the highest rate (29.99%), so avalanche and snowball both start with it, and it's cleared by month 5. After that, avalanche saves only about $58.

Worked example: $15,000 of debt

DebtBalanceAPRMinimum
Card A$4,50024.99%$135
Card B$3,00021.99%$90
Store card$75029.99%$25
Personal loan$6,75012.99%$181

The situation: $8,250 on three cards plus a $6,750 personal loan at 12.99%, with $431 a month of minimums. Minimums alone take 4 years 5 months and cost $7,454 in interest.

Adding $300 a month ($731 in total) clears it in 25 months for $2,931 of interest. Avalanche and snowball are only $74 apart, because the personal loan is both the biggest balance and the lowest rate, so both methods leave it until last.

The bigger lever here is the cards' rates. $8,250 at 22% to 30% is where nearly all the interest comes from. A 0% balance transfer for even 12 to 15 months (after a typical 3% to 5% fee) would cut the interest on those cards for most of the payoff. Asking each card company for a lower rate is a free first step.

Worked example: $30,000 of debt

DebtBalanceAPRMinimum
Card A$7,50024.99%$225
Card B$4,50021.99%$135
Personal loan$6,00012.99%$161
Car loan$12,0007.49%$240

The situation: $12,000 on two cards, a $6,000 personal loan at 12.99% and a $12,000 car loan at 7.49%, with $761 a month of minimums. This is the example where the method makes a real difference.

Paying $1,261 a month ($500 extra), both methods finish in 28 months, but avalanche costs $4,735 in interest and snowball $5,257: $522 more. Snowball's first win comes in month 8 (Card B) against month 12 for avalanche (Card A). If that early win is what keeps you paying, it can be worth the $522. If you'll stick with it either way, avalanche is cheaper.

One thing not to do: put the extra $500 on the car loan. At 7.49% it's the cheapest debt here. Every extra dollar does three times as much work on a 24.99% card.

Ways to speed it up

The fastest lever is usually the rate on your cards: a 0% balance transfer (watch the fee and the rate after the intro) or simply asking your card company for a lower APR. Then find the extra payment in the budget for your salary.

Common questions

How long does it take to pay off $5,000 of debt?

In this page's example (3 debts, $152 of minimums), paying $302 a month clears it in about 1 year 9 months with the avalanche method and $1,079 of interest (snowball: 1 year 9 months, $1,137). Your own rates change this; the calculator above uses them.

How long does it take to pay off $15,000 of debt?

In this page's example (4 debts, $431 of minimums), paying $731 a month clears it in about 2 years 1 month with the avalanche method and $2,931 of interest (snowball: 2 years 1 month, $3,005). Your own rates change this; the calculator above uses them.

How long does it take to pay off $30,000 of debt?

In this page's example (4 debts, $761 of minimums), paying $1,261 a month clears it in about 2 years 4 months with the avalanche method and $4,735 of interest (snowball: 2 years 4 months, $5,257). Your own rates change this; the calculator above uses them.

Snowball or avalanche: which is better?

Avalanche (highest rate first) always costs the same or less interest. Snowball (smallest balance first) gives you an earlier win. The gap depends on your mix: in the examples here it ranges from about $58 to $522. If the early win keeps you going, snowball is worth it.

Does the calculator store my numbers?

No. It runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Cost of living budget in Houston

A modest one-bedroom in Houston costs about $1,261 a month with utilities, the 22nd highest of the 25 largest US cities. To keep it at 30% of your pay you'd earn about $51,000 a year. Here's what different salaries leave you with after TX taxes and rent.

Check your take-home pay in Houston

Rent in Houston

HUD's fair market rent for the Houston-The Woodlands-Sugar Land, TX area is $1,261 for a one-bedroom and $1,497 for a two-bedroom ($748 each if you split it). Fair market rent includes utilities and sits at the 40th percentile of rents for a standard unit: a realistic "modest apartment" number, not a luxury listing and not a bargain.

Getting around Houston

Houston METRO doesn't sell a monthly pass, but local bus and rail fares are $1.25 and cap at $3 a day, so even daily riding stays under about $90 a month. If transit covers your commute, that's what goes in the "other bills" line instead of a car payment, insurance and fuel, which together usually cost several times more. (Checked November 7, 2026; fares change, so confirm on the agency's site.)

Common questions

How much do you need to make to live in Houston?

To keep a fair-market one-bedroom ($1,261 a month) at 30% of gross pay, about $51,000 a year. Sharing a two-bedroom brings that down to about $30,000. Those are rent-based rules of thumb; your other costs decide the rest.

What is the fair market rent in Houston?

HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for the Houston-The Woodlands-Sugar Land, TX area is $1,261 a month for a one-bedroom and $1,497 for a two-bedroom, including utilities. It's set at the 40th percentile of rents for a standard unit, so many listings will be higher, especially in popular neighbourhoods.

Does Houston have a state or city income tax?

Texas has no state income tax on wages.

How much is $60,000 after taxes in Houston?

About $50,390 a year, or $4,199 a month, for a single filer in 2026. After a fair-market one-bedroom that leaves $2,938 a month for everything else.

Cost of living budget in Boston

A modest one-bedroom in Boston costs about $2,518 a month with utilities, the 4th highest of the 25 largest US cities. To keep it at 30% of your pay you'd earn about $101,000 a year. Here's what different salaries leave you with after MA taxes and rent.

Check your take-home pay in Boston

Rent in Boston

HUD's fair market rent for the Boston-Cambridge-Quincy, MA-NH area is $2,518 for a one-bedroom and $3,008 for a two-bedroom ($1,504 each if you split it). Fair market rent includes utilities and sits at the 40th percentile of rents for a standard unit: a realistic "modest apartment" number, not a luxury listing and not a bargain.

Getting around Boston

An MBTA Monthly LinkPass (subway and local bus) is $90. If transit covers your commute, that's what goes in the "other bills" line instead of a car payment, insurance and fuel, which together usually cost several times more. Common questions

How much do you need to make to live in Boston?

To keep a fair-market one-bedroom ($2,518 a month) at 30% of gross pay, about $101,000 a year. Sharing a two-bedroom brings that down to about $61,000. Those are rent-based rules of thumb; your other costs decide the rest.

What is the fair market rent in Boston?

HUD's FY2027 fair market rent for the Boston-Cambridge-Quincy, MA-NH area is $2,518 a month for a one-bedroom and $3,008 for a two-bedroom, including utilities. It's set at the 40th percentile of rents for a standard unit, so many listings will be higher, especially in popular neighbourhoods.

Does Boston have a state or city income tax?

Yes. On a $60,000 salary, Massachusetts income tax is about $2,780 a year.

How much is $60,000 after taxes in Boston?

About $47,610 a year, or $3,968 a month, for a single filer in 2026. After a fair-market one-bedroom that leaves $1,450 a month for everything else.

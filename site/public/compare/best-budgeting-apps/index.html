How to choose in two minutes

  1. Will you pay? If not: Rocket Money's free tier, Goodbudget's free tier, EveryDollar's free version, or a spreadsheet.
  2. Do you want a method or a mirror? YNAB and EveryDollar teach a method (zero-based). Monarch, Copilot and Rocket Money mostly show you where money went.
  3. Do you share money with someone? Monarch and Goodbudget are built with shared use in mind; check how each handles a second user.
  4. Are you comfortable linking your bank? If not, Goodbudget or a spreadsheet.

Whatever you pick, use it with one weekly spending number: it works inside any app and stops the app becoming a guilt dashboard.

Before you link your bank

Most apps connect through a data aggregator using read-only access. Before linking, check the privacy policy for whether your data is sold or used for advertising, and whether you can delete it. US rules on personal financial data rights (the CFPB's open banking rule) are still being phased in and contested, so read each app's own terms rather than assuming.

Common questions

Is a paid budgeting app worth it?

If it gets you to check your money weekly and saves you more than it costs, yes. If you'd stop opening it after a month, no.

What's the best free budgeting app?

It depends on your style: Rocket Money's free tier for tracking, Goodbudget's free tier for envelopes, EveryDollar's free version for zero-based. Free tiers change; check what's included.

Are budgeting apps safe?

Reputable apps use read-only connections and encryption, but you're still sharing financial data with another company. Use a strong unique password and two-factor authentication.

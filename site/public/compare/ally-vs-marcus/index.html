Ally vs Marcus: which online savings account suits you?

Two popular no-fee online banks compared: savings features, CDs, checking, transfers and who each suits, with a worked example of what a rate gap is worth.

Ally Bank and Marcus by Goldman Sachs are two of the best-known online savings accounts in the US. Both are FDIC-insured, neither charges monthly fees, and both pay rates that have generally been far above the national average for savings. The differences are in what surrounds the savings account: Ally is a full online bank with checking and goal-tracking tools; Marcus is focused on savings and CDs.

Short answer:

  • Want checking and savings in one place, or like splitting savings into goals: Ally.
  • Want a simple, separate savings account you'll rarely touch: Marcus.
  • Rate-sensitive: compare both banks' current rates on the day you open. The gap between them changes and is usually small.

A worked example: what a rate gap is worth

Ellis (a fictional example) has a $10,000 emergency fund and is choosing between the two. On the day Ellis checks, one bank pays 0.20 percentage points more than the other (a hypothetical gap, for illustration).

On $10,000, a 0.20-point difference is about $20 a year, or less than $2 a month.

Ellis also wants to save for three goals alongside the emergency fund: a car repair fund, a holiday fund and a new laptop. With Ally's buckets, all four goals can live inside one savings account with one login and one transfer. With Marcus, Ellis would need a separate account for each goal, if the bank allows that, or a spreadsheet to track one combined balance.

For Ellis, the convenience of buckets is worth more than $20 a year, so Ellis chooses whichever has the tools Ellis will actually use. If Ellis only wanted one untouched emergency fund, the simpler choice would be the account with the higher rate on the day of opening.

The bigger lesson: moving money from a traditional savings account paying close to nothing to either of these banks is worth far more than the gap between them. On $10,000, going from a near-zero rate to a typical online savings rate is worth hundreds of dollars a year. (How to open a high-yield savings account.)

Ally

Good at: being a full online bank. Checking, savings, CDs and a money market account in one place, with buckets that make sinking funds easy and no minimums. (Sinking funds, explained.)

Watch out for: no cash deposits, and its rate isn't always the highest available. External transfers usually take one to three business days.

Skip it if: you deposit cash often or want a bank branch nearby.

More detail: our Ally Bank overview.

Marcus

Good at: simplicity. A savings account and CDs, from a large bank, without the distractions of a full banking app.

Watch out for: no checking account from the same bank, so money has to move between two banks to spend it. That can be a feature (savings out of sight) or a nuisance.

Skip it if: you want everything in one app, or you like organising savings by goal.

Which one for which job?

  • Emergency fund only: either. Pick the higher rate on the day, and keep it there.
  • Several savings goals: Ally's buckets make this simpler.
  • Checking and savings together: Ally.
  • Savings kept deliberately separate from your everyday bank: Marcus.
  • A dated goal you want to lock in: compare both banks' CD rates for your term. (High-yield savings vs CDs.)

Common questions

Is Ally or Marcus safer?

Both are FDIC-insured banks. Deposits are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category, so within those limits your money is equally protected at either.

Can I have accounts at both Ally and Marcus?

Yes. Some people keep checking at one bank and savings at another. Because they're separate banks, each has its own FDIC coverage, which matters only if your balances are above the insurance limits.

How long do transfers take?

Transfers between an online bank and an external account usually take one to three business days. Plan ahead for big payments, and check each bank's current transfer limits.

Do Ally and Marcus change their savings rates?

Yes. Savings rates at both banks are variable and generally move with market interest rates. That's normal for savings accounts; if you want a fixed rate, look at a CD.

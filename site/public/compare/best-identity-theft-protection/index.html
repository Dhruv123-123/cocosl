Identity theft protection services: what you get for the money (and the free version)

What paid identity theft protection actually does, what it can't do, and how Aura, LifeLock, Identity Guard and Experian compare with the free steps that prevent most new-account fraud.

Paid identity theft protection services monitor your personal information and credit, alert you to suspicious activity, and help you recover if something happens. Some bundle insurance, a password manager or a VPN.

What they can't do is stop someone from trying. And the single most effective prevention step, a credit freeze, is free. So the honest question isn't "which service is best" but "what does a service add on top of the free steps, and is that worth it to you?"

The free version (do this first)

  1. Freeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. Free by law, about 15 minutes total. Step by step.
  2. Check your credit reports free, weekly, at AnnualCreditReport.com.
  3. Turn on alerts for every bank and card account (large transactions, new payees).
  4. Use a password manager and two-factor authentication on email and banking.
  5. If it happens: IdentityTheft.gov (the FTC's official site) builds a free recovery plan.

That covers most new-account fraud. What it doesn't give you: someone to call, monitoring of places like data-breach dumps, and insurance for expenses.

Who should consider paying

  • People who've already been victims, or whose SSN was in a major breach, and want help monitoring and recovering.
  • Families who want one bundle covering several people, including children (who can't easily check their own credit).
  • People who value a phone number to call more than they value the yearly cost.

Who probably shouldn't

  • Anyone who has frozen their credit, checks reports a few times a year and uses a password manager. You already have most of the protection.

Common questions

Does identity theft protection prevent identity theft?

No service can guarantee that. Monitoring alerts you faster; a credit freeze is what blocks most new-account fraud.

Is the insurance worth it?

It covers certain recovery costs up to a limit, not necessarily money stolen from accounts (banks have their own fraud protections). Read what's covered.

Can I use a freeze and a paid service together?

Yes. Many people do: freeze for prevention, service for monitoring and help.

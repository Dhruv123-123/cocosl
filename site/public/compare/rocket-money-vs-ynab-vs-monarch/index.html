Rocket Money vs YNAB vs Monarch: which budgeting app fits you?

Three popular budgeting apps compared head to head: how they budget, what's free, what they cost, how they handle subscriptions, couples and net worth, and who should skip each one.

Rocket Money, YNAB and Monarch get compared constantly, but they're built for different people. YNAB is a budgeting method with an app attached. Monarch is a full household money dashboard. Rocket Money started as a subscription-cancelling tool and grew budgeting features around it.

Short answer:

  • Want to change how you budget, and willing to learn a method? YNAB.
  • Want everything (accounts, net worth, a shared household budget) in one place? Monarch.
  • Want to find subscriptions and see spending, preferably free? Rocket Money.

Rocket Money

Good at: spotting subscriptions and recurring charges fast, simple spending views, and a usable free tier.

Watch out for: the bill negotiation service takes a share of the savings (see bill negotiation vs doing it yourself), and you can find subscriptions yourself in 10 minutes for free.

Skip it if: you want a strict budgeting method.

YNAB

Good at: changing behaviour. Zero-based budgeting means you only plan with money you already have, and the app is built around that method, with plenty of free guides and workshops.

Watch out for: it takes a few weeks to click, and it's a paid subscription after the trial.

Skip it if: you want an app that runs itself in the background.

Monarch

Good at: the full picture. Accounts, investments, net worth, goals and a shared household view, with a flexible budgeting style.

Watch out for: no free tier, and the breadth can be more than a beginner needs.

Skip it if: you mainly want to cut subscriptions or need something free.

Or: none of them

If you'd stop opening any app after a month, a spreadsheet with one weekly spending number is free and works with any bank. More options: best budgeting apps and app vs spreadsheet vs notebook.

Common questions

Which is cheapest?

Rocket Money, because it has a free tier. YNAB and Monarch are paid after a trial; compare annual pricing, which is usually cheaper than monthly.

Which is best for couples?

Monarch is built around shared households. YNAB lets you share one budget. Check Rocket Money's current sharing options.

Can I switch later?

Yes. Most let you export transactions to CSV. Switching mid-month is messier; switch at the start of a month.

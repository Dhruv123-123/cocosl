Balance transfer cards: how to compare them and do the maths first

A balance transfer can save hundreds or cost you more. How to compare the intro period, transfer fee and go-to APR, a worked example, and the mistakes that cancel out the savings.

A balance transfer card lets you move high-interest credit card debt onto a new card with a low or 0% introductory APR for a set period. Done right, nearly every payment goes to the balance instead of interest. Done wrong, you pay a fee, run up the old card again, and end up with more debt.

We don't publish a ranked list of specific cards: balance transfer offers change constantly and depend on your credit. Use the criteria and the maths below, then compare current offers from your own bank, card issuers you already use, and the comparison pages of major issuers.

The maths (worked example)

$5,000 on a card at 24% APR (hypothetical), paying about $287 a month:

PlanMonths to pay offCost
Stay on the 24% cardabout 22 monthsabout $1,214 in interest
Transfer to an 18-month 0% intro card, 3% fee ($150), pay $287/monthabout 18 months$150 fee, no interest
Same transfer, but only pay $200/month18 months at 0%, then $1,550 left at the go-to APR (24% assumed): about 9 more monthsabout $301 (fee + interest)

Simulations with monthly compounding, no new purchases, hypothetical rates. Your offer will differ.

The transfer saves about $1,000 in the first plan, and still saves money in the third, but the real lesson is the monthly payment: divide the balance plus fee by the number of intro months, and pay at least that.

Monthly payment needed = (balance + transfer fee) ÷ intro months

Mistakes that cancel out the savings

  1. Spending on the new card. New purchases may not get the 0% rate, and payments may go to the 0% balance first, leaving purchases growing at full APR. Use the card for transfers only.
  2. Running the old card back up. The freed-up limit is the trap. Take the old card out of your phone's wallet (keep the account open if it has no annual fee: why).
  3. Missing a payment. Some cards can end the intro rate after a late payment. Set autopay for at least the minimum.
  4. Paying only the minimum. The intro period ends either way; whatever's left starts accruing interest at the go-to rate.
  5. Missing the transfer deadline. Start the transfer the day you're approved.

Common questions

Will a balance transfer hurt my credit score?

Applying usually means a hard inquiry and a new account, which can cause a small temporary dip. Paying the balance down lowers utilization, which tends to help over time.

Can I transfer a balance to a card from the same bank?

Usually not. Most issuers only allow transfers from other issuers' cards.

Is the balance transfer fee worth it?

Compare the fee with the interest you'd pay otherwise over the same period. In the example above, a $150 fee replaced over $1,000 of interest.

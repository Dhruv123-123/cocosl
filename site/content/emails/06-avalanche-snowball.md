Hi,

Avalanche vs snowball gets argued online like a sport. So I ran the actual numbers.

Three hypothetical debts: a $900 store card at 18%, a $4,200 credit card at 27%, and a $6,500 personal loan at 9%. Minimums total $350, plus **$200 extra** a month.

- **Avalanche** (highest rate first): about 25 months, about **$1,850** in interest. First debt gone in **month 17**.
- **Snowball** (smallest balance first): about 25 months, about **$1,950** in interest. First debt gone in **month 5**.
- **Minimums only (no extra)**: about 89 months and about $6,700 in interest.

So avalanche saved roughly **$106**. Snowball delivered the first win **a full year sooner**.

My take: the best method is the one you're still following in month 12. If rate gaps are big or you're motivated by spreadsheets, avalanche. If you've started and quit before, snowball's small extra cost buys momentum. And either one crushes minimum payments.

One more number that surprises people: a **$3,000 card balance at 22% APR, paying $60 a month**, takes about 11½ years and costs about $5,200 in interest, more than the balance itself. At $150 a month it's about 26 months and $770.

Hi,

A hundred dollars a month doesn't sound like investing. It sounds like a phone bill.

But as a hypothetical, $100 a month for 40 years at a 7% average annual return grows to roughly **$262,000**, from $48,000 of contributions. Returns aren't guaranteed and markets go up and down, but starting early gives your money the one thing nobody can buy later: time.

The order that makes sense for most people starting out:

1. **Starter emergency fund** (your Day 4 account) so a car repair never forces you to sell investments.
2. **Get your full 401(k) match**, if you have one (Day 6). It's often an instant 50–100% on what you put in.
3. **Pay down high-interest debt.** Paying off a 22% card is a guaranteed 22% "return".
4. **Then invest more**, often in a Roth IRA with a low-cost index fund or a single target-date fund.

Two things beginners get wrong:

- **Depositing isn't investing.** Money you add to a brokerage account often lands as cash. You have to actually buy the fund. Check your holdings.
- **Fees compound too.** On a $10,000 investment over 30 years (hypothetical 7%), a 1% fee leaves you about $18,000 less than a 0.03% fee. [Here's how to check any fund's fee in a minute](/blog/expense-ratios-explained/).

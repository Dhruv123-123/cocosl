Your first bank account should be boring: free, insured, and impossible to accidentally overdraw. Here's what to look for and a few options worth comparing.

*Based on each bank's published terms, checked in November 2026. We haven't been paid for this guide. Check current terms before opening.*

## The four checks

1. **No monthly fee, with no conditions you'll miss.** Many "free" accounts are only free above a balance or with direct deposit. Over the summer, without a paycheck, that fee can kick in.
2. **No overdraft fees, or overdraft turned off.** Ask how the account handles a purchase you can't cover. The best answer is "it gets declined" or "a small buffer at no cost".
3. **A good app and free ATMs near campus.** You'll use both every week.
4. **Insured.** Member FDIC (banks) or NCUA (credit unions). If it's an app, [check who holds the money](/blog/is-my-savings-app-fdic-insured/).

## Mistakes that cost students money

- **Keeping the account your parents opened at 15.** Youth accounts can convert into fee-charging accounts when you turn 18 or 24. Check.
- **Opting in to overdraft "protection".** It can mean a fee each time. Decline it, or pick an account that doesn't charge one.
- **No savings account at all.** Even $20 a week into a separate [high-yield savings account](/blog/best-high-yield-savings-accounts/) builds a buffer for textbooks and flights home.
- **Signing up on campus for a card you don't need.** Your first credit card is a separate decision. [How credit scores work](/blog/how-credit-scores-work/).

## A simple setup

1. One fee-free checking account for spending.
2. One savings account, ideally at a different bank, with an automatic weekly transfer.
3. Low-balance alerts turned on, so you hear about problems before they cost money.

## Common questions

### Do I need a student account?
No. Any account with no monthly fee and no overdraft fees works. "Student" accounts are often just regular accounts with fees waived until graduation.

### Is Chime a bank?
No. Chime is a financial technology company; its accounts are provided by partner banks that are FDIC members.

### Should I use a credit union?
Credit unions are often a great fit for students: low fees, local branches and member-friendly policies. Check whether you're eligible through your school, job or family.

A high-yield savings account (HYSA) is a normal, insured savings account that pays a lot more interest than the account most big banks give you by default. Moving your emergency fund to one is one of the few money moves that takes ten minutes and keeps paying.

Here's the catch with every "best HYSA" list: **rates change all the time**, often weekly. A list sorted by today's top rate is out of date by next month. So instead of a ranking, here are the five checks that matter, and four well-known accounts worth comparing against them.

## The five checks

1. **Is it actually insured?** Look for "Member FDIC" (or NCUA for credit unions) and find the bank's name in the [FDIC's BankFind tool](https://banks.data.fdic.gov/bankfind-suite/bankfind). If the account is through an app, [check who really holds the money](/blog/is-my-savings-app-fdic-insured/).
2. **What's the rate, and what are the conditions?** Some accounts only pay the top rate above a minimum balance, or with direct deposit, or for an introductory period. The headline APY is only useful if you'll meet the condition.
3. **Are there fees or minimums?** A monthly fee can wipe out the extra interest on a small balance. Look for no monthly fee and a low or zero minimum to open.
4. **How fast can you move money out?** Transfers to your checking account usually take 1–3 business days. That's fine for an emergency fund, but know the number before you need it.
5. **Will you leave it alone?** Some people do better with a savings account at a *different* bank from their checking, so the money is out of sight.

Compare any account's rate with the FDIC's published national average savings rate. A good HYSA pays several times that average.

## Traps that cost you interest

- **The teaser rate.** A high rate for three months, then a drop. Fine if you know, annoying if you don't.
- **The balance tier.** "Up to X% APY" can mean "on balances over $5,000". Read the rate table, not the banner.
- **Chasing every rate change.** Moving accounts for 0.1% isn't worth your time. Re-check twice a year.
- **Leaving it at your main bank.** Many big banks still pay close to nothing on standard savings. That gap is real money on an emergency fund.

## Common questions

### Are high-yield savings accounts safe?
At an FDIC-insured bank, deposits are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category, if the bank fails. Check the bank's name in BankFind before you open the account.

### Is the interest taxed?
Yes. Interest is taxable income, and banks send a 1099-INT when you earn $10 or more in a year.

### Should my whole emergency fund go in a HYSA?
For most people, yes: it's insured, it's easy to reach in a few days, and it earns interest. Keep a small buffer in checking for true same-day emergencies. [How much to save](/blog/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund/).

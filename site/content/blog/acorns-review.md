Acorns is an investing app built around round-ups: every time you buy something, it rounds up to the nearest dollar and invests the spare change. It's a clever way to start a habit. The cost is a **flat monthly subscription**, which is expensive as a percentage on a small balance.

*How we review: based on Acorns' published pricing and disclosures, checked in November 2026. We haven't been paid for this review. General education, not investment advice. Investing involves risk, including loss of principal.*

## The short version

| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | Investing app with a managed ETF portfolio and round-ups |
| Price | Monthly subscription tiers, which have been about $3, $6 and $12 a month (check current) |
| Higher tiers add | Retirement (IRA) account, checking account, kids' accounts |
| Minimum | Low |
| Standout | Automatic round-ups and recurring investing |

**Best for:** people who've never invested and need an automatic nudge, as long as they'll build a balance quickly.
**Not for:** anyone keeping a small balance for a long time, where the fee eats the growth.

## The fee maths

A flat fee hits small balances hardest. Using the $3/month tier as an example ($36 a year):

| Balance | $36 a year as a % of balance |
|---|---|
| $300 | 12% |
| $1,000 | 3.6% |
| $5,000 | 0.72% |
| $20,000 | 0.18% |

Compare that with a typical low-cost index fund, whose yearly cost is often well under 0.1%. Round-ups add up slowly, often $20–$50 a month, so it can take a while to reach a balance where the fee is reasonable.

## How to make Acorns work for you

1. **Add a recurring deposit** on top of round-ups, so the balance grows faster than the fee.
2. **Check the tier.** Only pay for the higher tiers if you'll use the IRA or checking features.
3. **Review it yearly.** Once you have the habit, you might move to a cheaper option.

## Cheaper ways to automate investing

- A **target-date index fund** at a large brokerage, with an automatic monthly transfer.
- A **robo-adviser** with a percentage fee instead of a flat one. [Compare robo-advisers](/blog/best-robo-advisors-for-beginners/).

## Our verdict

[Acorns](/go/acorns/) is a good habit-builder for complete beginners who'll also add regular deposits. If you'll only invest spare change, the flat fee makes it an expensive habit. Run the maths with your expected balance first.

## Common questions

### Is Acorns worth it?
It can be for beginners who'll build a balance quickly. On small balances, the flat fee is a high percentage.

### Is Acorns safe?
Investments are held at a brokerage that's SIPC-protected against firm failure, but they can lose value with the market.

### Can I withdraw from Acorns anytime?
From a regular investment account, yes, though selling can take a few business days and may have tax consequences. IRA withdrawals follow retirement-account rules.

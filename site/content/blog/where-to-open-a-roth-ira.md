Decided a [Roth IRA is right for you](/blog/roth-vs-traditional-ira/)? Opening one takes about 20 minutes. The main decision is *where*: a brokerage, where you pick the investments, or a robo-adviser, which picks them for you.

*General education, not investment advice. Investing involves risk, including loss of principal. Limits and rules are from IRS.gov, checked in November 2026.*

## Step 1: Check you can contribute

- You need **earned income** (wages, self-employment) at least equal to what you put in.
- For **2026**, the IRA limit is **$7,500** if you're under 50 (more with the catch-up if you're 50 or older). That's the total across all your IRAs. The IRS announces each year's limit in the autumn.
- Roth eligibility **phases out at higher incomes**. Check the IRS income table for your filing status.

## Step 2: Choose where to open it

| | Brokerage | Robo-adviser |
|---|---|---|
| Who picks investments | You | The service |
| Typical cost | No account fee; you pay the funds' expense ratios | A yearly advisory fee (often 0.25%) plus fund costs |
| Best for | People happy to choose one or two index funds | People who want it fully done for them |
| Examples | [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/retirement-ira/roth-ira), [Charles Schwab](aff:schwab), Vanguard | [Betterment](aff:betterment), [Wealthfront](aff:wealthfront) |

What to look for at either: no account or inactivity fees, no minimum to open, a wide choice of low-cost index funds, and the ability to set up automatic monthly contributions. Most large brokerages tick all four. Some of the links above are affiliate links and some aren't; it doesn't change the list.

## Step 3: Open it

You'll need your Social Security number, your employer's name and address, and your bank details for funding. Pick **Roth IRA** as the account type (not "individual brokerage").

## Step 4: Fund it, then invest it

This is the step people miss. **Moving money into the IRA doesn't invest it.** At a brokerage, your contribution usually lands as cash. You then need to buy something with it.

A common simple choice is a single **target-date index fund** for the year near when you'd retire, or a total-market index fund. A robo-adviser invests it for you automatically. [Why low fees matter](/blog/expense-ratios-explained/).

## Step 5: Automate it

Set a monthly transfer you can keep up, even $50 or $100, and, at a brokerage, an automatic investment into your chosen fund. $7,500 a year is $625 a month, but any amount counts. [Starting with $100 a month](/blog/how-to-start-investing-with-100-a-month/).

## Before an IRA: the workplace match

If your employer offers a 401(k) match, many people contribute enough to get the full match first, then fund a Roth IRA. [How the match works](/blog/401k-match-explained/).

## Common questions

### Can I open a Roth IRA with $100?
Yes. Many brokerages have no minimum to open, and many funds can be bought in small amounts.

### What's the deadline to contribute for a tax year?
Usually the tax-filing deadline the following April. Contributions for 2026 can generally be made until mid-April 2027.

### Is my Roth IRA insured?
Investments aren't FDIC-insured and can lose value. SIPC protects brokerage customers if the brokerage itself fails, up to its limits; it doesn't protect against market losses.

### Can I have a Roth IRA and a 401(k)?
Yes. Income limits for Roth IRA contributions still apply.

Betterment is a robo-adviser: you answer a few questions, and it invests your money in a diversified portfolio of low-cost index funds, then rebalances it for you. It's one of the oldest and largest in the US. The main thing to understand is the fee, especially on small balances.

*How we review: based on Betterment's published pricing and Form CRS, checked in November 2026. We haven't been paid for this review. General education, not investment advice. Investing involves risk, including loss of principal.*

## The short version

| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | Registered investment adviser that manages an ETF portfolio for you |
| Digital plan fee | 0.25% a year of your balance, or $4 a month for smaller balances unless you meet the recurring-deposit condition (check current terms) |
| Fund costs | You also pay the funds' own expense ratios |
| Minimum to start | Low or none for the basic plan |
| Accounts | Taxable, Roth and Traditional IRA, rollovers, cash account |
| Human advice | Available on higher tiers |

**Best for:** people who want to invest regularly and never think about rebalancing.
**Not for:** people with very small balances who won't set up recurring deposits, or DIY investors happy with one target-date fund.

## How the fee works on a small balance

The small-balance fee matters most when you're starting out. Example with assumed numbers:

| Balance | $4/month fee as a yearly % | 0.25% fee |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 4.8% a year | $2.50 a year |
| $5,000 | about 1% a year | $12.50 a year |
| $20,000 | 0.24% a year | $50 a year |

A 4.8% yearly fee would eat most of a typical year's expected return. Betterment has waived the $4 fee if you make recurring deposits above a monthly amount; if you'll do that anyway, the cost is the 0.25%.

## What you get for the fee

- **Automatic diversification and rebalancing.**
- **Tax tools,** like tax-loss harvesting in taxable accounts (useful mainly for larger balances).
- **Goal-based planning** and a clean app that makes recurring investing easy.

## The do-it-yourself alternative

A single low-cost target-date or total-market index fund at a large brokerage does much of the same job for no advisory fee. If you're comfortable picking one fund and leaving it, that's cheaper. [How to start investing with $100 a month](/blog/how-to-start-investing-with-100-a-month/).

## Our verdict

[Betterment](/go/betterment/) is a solid, low-cost way to invest hands-off, as long as you avoid the small-balance trap: either set up recurring deposits that meet its condition, or wait until your balance makes the fee small.

*Betterment is an SEC-registered investment adviser. Check its Form CRS and look it up at adviserinfo.sec.gov. Past performance doesn't predict future results.*

## Common questions

### Is Betterment safe?
Investments aren't FDIC-insured and can lose value. Brokerage accounts are covered by SIPC against the failure of the brokerage (not against market losses), up to its limits.

### Can I lose money with Betterment?
Yes. Like any investment, the portfolio can fall in value, especially in the short term.

### Is Betterment worth it?
It can be for hands-off investors who'll invest regularly. For a small balance without recurring deposits, the fee is high.

Avalanche vs snowball gets argued online like a sport. Here's the actual maths on the same three debts, so you can pick the method you'll actually stick with.

## The two methods in one sentence each

Both methods pay the **minimum on every debt**, then put **all extra money on one target debt**. When that debt is gone, its payment rolls onto the next target.

- **Avalanche:** target the **highest interest rate** first. Usually saves the most interest.
- **Snowball:** target the **smallest balance** first. Gets you quick wins.

## A worked example

Three hypothetical debts, $200 a month extra on top of the minimums:

| Debt | Balance | APR | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store card | $900 | 18% | $30 |
| Credit card | $4,200 | 27% | $110 |
| Personal loan | $6,500 | 9% | $210 |
| **Total** | **$11,600** | | **$350** |

- **Avalanche order:** credit card (27%) → store card (18%) → personal loan (9%).
- **Snowball order:** store card ($900) → credit card ($4,200) → personal loan ($6,500).

## The results

| | Debt-free in | Total interest | First debt paid off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avalanche** | about 25 months | about **$1,850** | month 17 |
| **Snowball** | about 25 months | about **$1,950** | **month 5** |
| Minimums only (no extra) | about 89 months | about $6,700 | |

*Simulation with monthly compounding, minimums held constant, $200 extra a month. Hypothetical numbers for illustration.*

What jumps out:

1. **The interest gap is about $106.** Real, but smaller than the internet makes it sound.
2. **Snowball's first win comes a full year earlier.** Month 5 vs month 17 is a long time to wait for any visible progress.
3. **Either method beats minimums by a mile**: roughly 64 months (over five years) sooner and about $4,800 less interest.

## So which should you pick?

- **Pick avalanche** if the rate gaps are big (say a 27% card next to a 6% loan) or the high-rate balance is large, and you're the type who stays motivated by a spreadsheet.
- **Pick snowball** if you've started payoff plans before and quit, or you have several small balances you could knock out quickly. The extra cost buys momentum, which matters if it keeps you going.
- **Hybrid:** clear one tiny balance first for a quick win, then switch to avalanche.

The best method is the one you're still following in month 12.

## Why minimum payments take so long

Credit card minimums are designed to keep you paying for years. Take a **$3,000 balance at 22% APR**:

| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Total interest |
|---|---|---|
| $60 | about 11½ years | about **$5,200** |
| $150 | about 26 months | about **$770** |

At $60 a month you'd pay more in interest than the original balance. An extra $90 a month saves about $4,400. (Hypothetical; assumes no new charges.)

## Make it faster

- **Stop adding to the debt.** Take the cards out of your phone's wallet while you pay them down.
- **Call and ask for a lower rate.** It doesn't always work, but it's a five-minute call.
- **Consider a balance transfer or consolidation loan** only if the fees and the rate after any promotional period actually save money, and you won't run the cards back up.
- **Find the extra $200.** Your [weekly spending number](/blog/how-to-make-a-budget-you-will-keep/), [cancelled subscriptions](/blog/find-and-cancel-subscriptions/) and any windfalls are the usual sources.
- **Struggling with minimums?** A nonprofit credit counselling agency (for example, through the NFCC) can talk through options like a debt management plan. Be wary of companies that charge large upfront fees or promise to "erase" debt.

## Common questions

### Should I save or pay off debt first?
Many people keep a small [starter emergency fund](/blog/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund/) first, so new surprises don't go back on the card, then go all-in on high-interest debt.

### Does paying off debt raise my credit score?
Lower card balances reduce your utilization, which usually helps. [How credit scores work](/blog/how-credit-scores-work/).

### Should I close cards as I pay them off?
Often not, if they have no annual fee. Closing them can raise utilization.

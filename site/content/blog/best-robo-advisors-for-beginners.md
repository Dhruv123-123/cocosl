A robo-adviser is an online service that builds a portfolio of index funds for you, based on your goals and comfort with risk, then keeps it balanced. It's a good fit for people who want to invest regularly without making decisions. It isn't the only way, and not always the cheapest.

*Based on each firm's published pricing, checked in November 2026. Commissions never change our picks. General education, not investment advice. Investing involves risk, including loss of principal.*

## How to compare robo-advisers

1. **The advisory fee:** a yearly percentage of your balance. 0.25% on $10,000 is $25 a year.
2. **Flat or minimum fees:** some charge a flat monthly fee on small balances, which can be a very high percentage.
3. **Fund costs:** the funds inside your portfolio have their own [expense ratios](/blog/expense-ratios-explained/).
4. **Cash allocation:** some "no-fee" robos keep part of your portfolio in cash, which is how they earn money.
5. **Minimum to start** and the **account types** you need (Roth IRA, taxable).

## When you don't need a robo-adviser

If you're happy to pick one fund and keep adding to it, a **target-date index fund** at a large brokerage does much the same job (diversified, automatically rebalanced, more conservative as you age) for no advisory fee. That's often the simplest choice inside a [Roth IRA](/blog/where-to-open-a-roth-ira/).

A robo-adviser earns its fee when:

- you'd otherwise not invest at all because choosing feels overwhelming,
- you want tax features in a taxable account with a larger balance, or
- you value the app nudging you to invest automatically.

## Before you sign up

- Set up an automatic monthly deposit. It's the habit, not the platform, that matters most.
- Check the firm on the SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure site.
- Read the Form CRS: a two-page summary of fees and conflicts that every adviser must provide.

## Common questions

### Are robo-advisers safe?
They invest in regular funds held at a brokerage, which is typically SIPC-protected against the firm failing. Your investments can still lose value.

### How much do I need to start?
Some robos start at $0, others require $500–$5,000. A low minimum doesn't help if a flat fee makes small balances expensive.

### Robo-adviser or target-date fund?
If you'll only ever buy one fund, the target-date fund is usually cheaper. If you want automation and extra features, a robo may be worth the fee.

Anyone with your Social Security number and a few personal details can try to open a credit card or loan in your name. A **credit freeze** makes that much harder, it's **free by federal law**, and it takes about 15 minutes for all three bureaus.

## What a credit freeze does

A freeze (also called a security freeze) stops lenders from seeing your credit report when someone applies for new credit in your name. Because most lenders won't approve credit without checking a report, the application usually fails.

What a freeze **doesn't** do:

- It doesn't affect your credit score.
- It doesn't stop you using your existing cards and loans.
- It doesn't stop your current lenders checking your report, or stop you checking your own.
- It doesn't prevent every kind of fraud (e.g. misuse of an existing card), so keep reviewing statements.

## How to freeze your credit at all three bureaus

You need to freeze separately at each of the three nationwide bureaus. Go directly to each one's official site:

1. **Equifax**: equifax.com, search "credit freeze", or call 1-888-298-0045.
2. **Experian**: experian.com/freeze, or call 1-888-397-3742.
3. **TransUnion**: transunion.com/credit-freeze, or call 1-888-909-8872.

At each one you'll create an account (or use a PIN), confirm your identity, and place the freeze. It's free to place and free to lift. Online or phone requests must be handled quickly (within one business day for placing a freeze, and within an hour for lifting one).

Save your login details somewhere safe, like a password manager. You'll need them to unfreeze.

## How to "thaw" when you need credit

Applying for a credit card, car loan, apartment or a new phone plan? Temporarily lift the freeze first, at the bureau the lender uses (ask them), or at all three. You can usually set it to refreeze automatically after a set number of days.

## Freeze vs fraud alert vs credit lock

| | Credit freeze | Fraud alert | Credit lock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free by law | Free | Sometimes part of a paid product |
| What it does | Blocks access to your report for new credit | Tells lenders to verify your identity first | Similar effect to a freeze |
| How long | Until you lift it | 1 year (extended: 7 years for identity theft victims) | Depends on the product's terms |
| Bureaus | Place at each of the three | Place at one; it notifies the others | Per bureau |

For most people, a free freeze at all three bureaus is the strongest, simplest option.

## Check your credit report like an auditor

Your credit report can contain mistakes, and you're the one who has to catch them. Get all three reports free, weekly, at **[AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/)** (the official site; you don't need to pay or sign up for a trial anywhere else).

Seven things to check:

1. **Your personal details**: name spellings, addresses, employers. An address you've never lived at can be a sign of fraud or mixed files.
2. **Every account**: do you recognise each one? An unfamiliar account is the biggest red flag.
3. **Account status**: open accounts showing as closed, or vice versa.
4. **Balances and credit limits**: a missing or wrong limit can inflate your utilization.
5. **Payment history**: any late payment you believe you made on time.
6. **Duplicate accounts**: the same debt listed twice, especially collections.
7. **Hard inquiries**: applications you didn't make.

### If you find an error

Dispute it with the bureau reporting it (online is easiest) and with the company that supplied the information. Include copies (not originals) of any proof. Bureaus generally must investigate within 30 days. The CFPB has a step-by-step guide and sample letters.

### If you find fraud

Go to **[IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/)**, the FTC's official site. It builds a recovery plan and the letters you need, and an identity theft report lets you get fraudulent information blocked from your report.

## Common questions

### Does a credit freeze hurt my credit score?
No. It has no effect on your score.

### Should I freeze my credit if I'm young or don't use credit?
It's a good idea for many people, including young adults, since identity thieves often target people who aren't checking their reports. Parents can also freeze a minor's credit.

### Do I need to pay for credit monitoring?
Not necessarily. Free weekly reports plus a freeze plus reviewing your statements covers a lot. Paid monitoring alerts you to problems; a freeze helps prevent them.

### Will a freeze stop a landlord or employer check?
It can block new checks that need your report. Lift it temporarily when you expect one.

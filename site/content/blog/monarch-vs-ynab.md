Monarch Money and YNAB are the two budgeting apps people most often pay for. They cost about the same and both sync with your bank. The difference is philosophy: **Monarch shows you the whole picture. YNAB makes you plan every dollar.**

## Side by side

| | Monarch Money | YNAB |
|---|---|---|
| Price | About $14.99/month or $99.99/year | $14.99/month or $109/year |
| Free trial | Usually offered | 34 days |
| Student offer | Check current offers | Free for a year for verified college students |
| Method | Flexible budgets, or one "flex" spending number | Zero-based: give every dollar a job |
| Net worth and investments | Built in, a strength | Basic account tracking only |
| Couples | Separate logins to one household | Share one budget with up to six people |
| Effort | Lower: more automatic | Higher: hands-on, especially month one |

## The one question that decides it

**Do you need to *see* your money, or *control* it?**

- If you're broadly fine but scattered across accounts, and you want a clear view of spending, savings and net worth: **Monarch**.
- If you run out before payday, carry card debt, or feel anxious every time a bill hits: **YNAB**. The method is built to change exactly that.

## Other tie-breakers

- **Irregular income:** YNAB. Budgeting only money you already have works well with uneven paychecks.
- **Lots of investment accounts:** Monarch.
- **Couples who argue about money:** both work. Monarch is easier to share passively; YNAB forces the "what is this dollar for" conversation, which can be the point.
- **You hate apps:** neither. Use a [spreadsheet or notebook](/blog/budgeting-app-vs-spreadsheet/).

## Try both, cheaply

Both offer trials. Start one, give it two honest weeks with a weekly check-in, and only then decide. Don't run both at once; you'll compare features instead of building a habit.

## Common questions

### Is Monarch easier than YNAB?
For most people, yes. YNAB has a steeper learning curve because of its method; Monarch works more like a dashboard out of the box.

### Can I use the YNAB method in Monarch?
Partly. You can budget every category in Monarch, but YNAB's "assign only money you have" rule is built into YNAB's design in a way it isn't in Monarch.

### Which is cheaper?
They're within about $10 a year of each other on annual plans. YNAB's student offer makes it free for eligible students for the first year.

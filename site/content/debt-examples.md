## 5000

**The situation:** three cards, $5,000 in total, and $152 a month of minimums. Paying just those (and rolling each one into the next as cards clear) takes 4 years 7 months and costs $3,352 in interest: two-thirds of the original debt again.

Say this person cancels two subscriptions and moves to a cheaper phone plan, which frees $150 a month. Paying $302 a month instead, the debt is gone in 21 months with $1,079 of interest. The extra $150 a month saves about $2,270 and nearly three years.

The method barely matters here. The store card is both the smallest balance and the highest rate (29.99%), so avalanche and snowball both start with it, and it's cleared by month 5. After that, avalanche saves only about $58.

## 15000

**The situation:** $8,250 on three cards plus a $6,750 personal loan at 12.99%, with $431 a month of minimums. Minimums alone take 4 years 5 months and cost $7,454 in interest.

Adding $300 a month ($731 in total) clears it in 25 months for $2,931 of interest. Avalanche and snowball are only $74 apart, because the personal loan is both the biggest balance and the lowest rate, so both methods leave it until last.

The bigger lever here is the cards' rates. $8,250 at 22% to 30% is where nearly all the interest comes from. A 0% balance transfer for even 12 to 15 months (after a typical 3% to 5% fee) would cut the interest on those cards for most of the payoff. Asking each card company for a lower rate is a free first step.

## 30000

**The situation:** $12,000 on two cards, a $6,000 personal loan at 12.99% and a $12,000 car loan at 7.49%, with $761 a month of minimums. This is the example where the method makes a real difference.

Paying $1,261 a month ($500 extra), both methods finish in 28 months, but avalanche costs $4,735 in interest and snowball $5,257: $522 more. Snowball's first win comes in month 8 (Card B) against month 12 for avalanche (Card A). If that early win is what keeps you paying, it can be worth the $522. If you'll stick with it either way, avalanche is cheaper.

One thing not to do: put the extra $500 on the car loan. At 7.49% it's the cheapest debt here. Every extra dollar does three times as much work on a 24.99% card.

The best budgeting app is the one you'll still open in three months. The second-best is a free spreadsheet you actually use. Before you pay for anything, read [app vs spreadsheet vs notebook](/blog/budgeting-app-vs-spreadsheet/): for a lot of people the answer is not an app.

If you do want an app, here's how the main options differ. Prices change and most run regular promotions, so we describe the price *model* and you check the current price.

## Side by side

| App | Price model | Budgeting style | Bank syncing | Best for | Not great for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [YNAB](aff:ynab) | Paid subscription after a free trial | Zero-based: give every dollar a job | Yes (or manual) | People who want a strict method and will learn it | Anyone who wants a hands-off app |
| [Monarch Money](aff:monarch) | Paid subscription after a free trial | Flexible: categories or a simple "flex" budget | Yes | Couples and households, net worth tracking | People who want a free tool |
| [Rocket Money](aff:rocket_money) | Free tier, paid premium | Spending tracking and category budgets | Yes | Finding and cancelling subscriptions | Detailed zero-based budgeting |
| [Copilot Money](aff:copilot) | Paid subscription | Category budgets with automatic categorising | Yes | Apple users who want a polished app | Android users (check current platform support) |
| [EveryDollar](aff:everydollar) | Free manual version, paid version with syncing | Zero-based | Paid version | People following a Ramsey-style plan | People who disagree with that plan's approach |
| [Goodbudget](aff:goodbudget) | Free tier, paid upgrade | Digital envelopes | No, manual entry | Envelope budgeting, sharing with a partner | People who want automatic transactions |

*Price models and features as listed by each company at the "checked as of" date above. Confirm current pricing and platform support before subscribing.*

Mint, once the default free app, shut down in 2024. If a "best apps" list still recommends it, the list is out of date.

## How to choose in two minutes

1. **Will you pay?** If not: Rocket Money's free tier, Goodbudget's free tier, EveryDollar's free version, or a spreadsheet.
2. **Do you want a method or a mirror?** YNAB and EveryDollar teach a method (zero-based). Monarch, Copilot and Rocket Money mostly show you where money went.
3. **Do you share money with someone?** Monarch and Goodbudget are built with shared use in mind; check how each handles a second user.
4. **Are you comfortable linking your bank?** If not, Goodbudget or a spreadsheet.

Whatever you pick, use it with [one weekly spending number](/blog/how-to-make-a-budget-you-will-keep/): it works inside any app and stops the app becoming a guilt dashboard.

## Before you link your bank

Most apps connect through a data aggregator using read-only access. Before linking, check the privacy policy for whether your data is sold or used for advertising, and whether you can delete it. US rules on personal financial data rights (the CFPB's open banking rule) are still being phased in and contested, so read each app's own terms rather than assuming.

## Common questions

### Is a paid budgeting app worth it?
If it gets you to check your money weekly and saves you more than it costs, yes. If you'd stop opening it after a month, no.

### What's the best free budgeting app?
It depends on your style: Rocket Money's free tier for tracking, Goodbudget's free tier for envelopes, EveryDollar's free version for zero-based. Free tiers change; check what's included.

### Are budgeting apps safe?
Reputable apps use read-only connections and encryption, but you're still sharing financial data with another company. Use a strong unique password and two-factor authentication.

A robo-advisor is an investment service that builds and manages a diversified portfolio for you, usually of low-cost index funds. You answer questions about your goals and risk tolerance; it picks a mix, invests new money automatically and rebalances.

The honest comparison point isn't another robo-advisor. It's **one target-date index fund** at a brokerage, which also diversifies and rebalances automatically, often for less. A robo-advisor is worth paying for if its extras (tax-loss harvesting, goal planning, automatic everything) are things you'll actually use.

## What you pay

Robo-advisors usually charge one or both of:

1. **An advisory fee**, typically a percentage of your balance per year, or a flat monthly fee on smaller balances.
2. **Fund expense ratios** on the funds inside your portfolio.

On a $10,000 balance, a 0.25% advisory fee is about $25 a year, on top of fund fees. That's small, but it compounds like any fee: [the fee that can cost you $18,000](/blog/expense-ratios-explained/).

## The flat-fee trap on small balances

A $3 monthly fee is $36 a year. On a $1,000 balance that's **3.6% a year**, far more than a typical percentage-based robo fee or an index fund. Flat fees make more sense as the balance grows. If you're starting small, compare the fee in dollars on *your* balance.

## Robo-advisor vs one target-date fund

| | Robo-advisor | Target-date index fund (DIY) |
|---|---|---|
| Diversified | Yes | Yes |
| Automatic rebalancing | Yes | Yes, inside the fund |
| Tax-loss harvesting | Often, in taxable accounts | No |
| Goal planning tools | Yes | No |
| Cost | Advisory fee + fund fees | Fund fee only |
| Effort | Lowest | Choose one fund, set up automatic investing once |

If you're investing inside an IRA (where tax-loss harvesting doesn't apply), a single low-cost target-date fund gets most people most of the benefit. Walkthrough: [how to start investing with $100 a month](/blog/how-to-start-investing-with-100-a-month/).

## Before you pick

- **Get the 401(k) match first**, if your job offers one: [how it works](/blog/401k-match-explained/).
- **Check the adviser's record** on the SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure site (adviserinfo.sec.gov), and read its Form CRS, a short plain-English summary of fees and conflicts.
- **Investing involves risk**, including loss of principal. A robo-advisor doesn't remove market risk; it manages a portfolio for you.

## Common questions

### Is a robo-advisor good for beginners?
It can be, especially if you want everything automated and might otherwise not invest. Compare the fee in dollars on your balance with a single target-date fund.

### Are robo-advisors safe?
Brokerage accounts are typically protected by SIPC if the firm fails (not against market losses). Robo-advisors are registered investment advisers; check their registration on adviserinfo.sec.gov.

### Can I use a robo-advisor for a Roth IRA?
Most offer Roth IRAs. [Roth vs Traditional](/blog/roth-vs-traditional-ira/) explains which type to choose.

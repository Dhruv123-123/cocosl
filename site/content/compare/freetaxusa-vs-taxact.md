FreeTaxUSA and TaxAct are two of the most common picks for people who want to file online without paying big-brand prices. They take different approaches. **FreeTaxUSA** makes the federal return free for almost everyone and charges for the state return and optional extras. **TaxAct** has a free tier for simple returns and paid tiers that unlock more forms, plus optional live help from tax experts.

Short answer:

- **Simple W-2 return:** both can be free for federal. Compare the state price.
- **Freelance, gig, rental or investment income:** FreeTaxUSA usually costs much less, because its federal return covers those forms for free.
- **You want a tax professional on call while you file:** TaxAct's paid Xpert Assist add-on is the more developed option.

## How they compare

| | [FreeTaxUSA](aff:freetaxusa) | [TaxAct](aff:taxact) |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal return** | Free, including most common forms | Free tier for simple returns; paid tiers for more |
| **Paid tiers** | One optional Deluxe add-on | Deluxe, Premier and Self-Employed |
| **State return** | A fee per state (about $15 to $16 last season) | Extra on each tier; check the current state price |
| **Freelance and gig income (Schedule C)** | Included in the free federal return | Self-Employed tier |
| **Investment income, home sales, rentals** | Included in the free federal return | Premier tier or higher |
| **Itemized deductions, HSAs** | Included | Not in the free tier |
| **Live expert help** | Limited; priority and chat support with Deluxe | Optional paid Xpert Assist add-on on any tier |
| **Prepares it for you** | No | Xpert Full Service, a separate paid service |

*Prices and features as described by each company at the "checked as of" date above, from their published pricing for the most recent filing season. Prices for the next season may change. Confirm on each site before you start.*

TaxAct's published prices for the last season were **$0** for the free tier, **$59.99** for Deluxe, **$99.99** for Premier and **$109.99** for Self-Employed, each plus the state return. TaxAct says its free tier covers W-2 income, unemployment, retirement income, the earned income tax credit, the child tax credit, dependents, students and student loan interest. It doesn't cover investment income, self-employment, rentals, itemized deductions or HSAs.

## Three worked returns

These are fictional examples, using last season's published prices for illustration.

**1. A W-2 and student loan interest.** One job, a 1098-E for student loan interest, a state return.

- FreeTaxUSA: federal $0, plus the state fee.
- TaxAct: free tier covers this, plus its state price.

*Verdict:* close. Compare the state prices, and also check IRS Free File, which may make both returns free if your income is under the limit.

**2. A W-2 plus freelance income.** A day job, plus a 1099-NEC for design work, plus a state return.

- FreeTaxUSA: federal $0 (Schedule C included), plus the state fee.
- TaxAct: Self-Employed tier at $109.99, plus state.

*Verdict:* FreeTaxUSA, by about $100 or more. That's the biggest difference between them.

**3. A first return with lots of questions.** A simple return, but the filer wants to ask a professional about education credits and whether their parents can claim them.

- FreeTaxUSA: free federal and the state fee; the help is mostly email support, or chat with Deluxe.
- TaxAct: free tier plus the Xpert Assist add-on for live sessions with a credentialed tax expert.

*Verdict:* TaxAct with Xpert Assist, if live help is worth the add-on price to you. Free in-person help from **VITA** volunteers is also worth considering. ([Filing your first return, step by step](/blog/how-to-file-taxes-for-the-first-time/).)

## FreeTaxUSA

**Good at:** a free federal return that genuinely covers most situations, including self-employment, rentals, investments and itemizing; a low state fee; few upsells.

**Watch out for:** less hand-holding. Support is limited compared with pricier software, so you need to be comfortable answering the interview questions yourself.

**Skip it if:** you want to talk to a tax professional while you file.

More detail: [our FreeTaxUSA overview](/blog/freetaxusa-review/).

## TaxAct

**Good at:** a clear tier structure, a free tier for simple returns, and paid access to live tax experts for people who want reassurance.

**Watch out for:** the jump in price once you have investment or self-employment income, and the state fee on top. Check which tier you need before you start, so the upgrade doesn't appear halfway through.

**Skip it if:** you have freelance or investment income and want to pay as little as possible.

## Before you pay for either

- **Check IRS Free File first.** If your income is under the limit, guided software from IRS partners may make your federal return free, and some partners include a free state return.
- **Check VITA.** Volunteer Income Tax Assistance prepares returns for free for people with low to moderate incomes (many sites help those earning about $70,000 or less), people with disabilities and people who speak limited English.
- **Gather every form first.** Starting a return before all your W-2s and 1099s arrive is the most common reason people end up amending.

## Common questions

### Is FreeTaxUSA really free?
The federal return is free for most situations, including freelance income, rentals and itemized deductions. You pay a fee for each state return and for optional extras like the Deluxe add-on. Check the current price list before you start.

### Is TaxAct free?
TaxAct has a free tier for simple returns, such as W-2 income, unemployment, student loan interest and common credits. Investment income, self-employment income, rentals, itemized deductions and HSAs need a paid tier, and state returns cost extra.

### Can I switch tax software from one year to the next?
Yes. Most programs can import last year's return as a PDF or from certain other providers, which saves time on personal details and carryovers. Keep a PDF of every return you file to make switching easy.

### Which is better for self-employed people?
On price, FreeTaxUSA, because Schedule C is included in its free federal return. If you want live help with business deductions, TaxAct's Self-Employed tier with Xpert Assist offers more support, at a higher price.

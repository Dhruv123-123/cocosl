If you have no credit history (or a damaged one), you're stuck in a loop: you need credit to get credit. Credit-builder products break the loop by limiting the lender's risk, usually with your own money as a deposit.

They only help if you use them right. The habits matter more than the product: [how credit scores work](/blog/how-credit-scores-work/).

## The three types

| Type | How it works | Typical upfront money | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Secured credit card** | You put down a refundable deposit that usually sets your limit; it reports like a normal card | A deposit (often a few hundred dollars) | Annual fees on some cards; high APR if you carry a balance |
| **Credit-builder card tied to a bank account** | You move money into a linked account and spend only what's there; reports as a card | Whatever you move in | Rules vary on what's reported; check it reports to all three bureaus |
| **Credit-builder loan** | The lender holds a small loan in savings; you make monthly payments, then get the money at the end | Monthly payments, sometimes a fee | Interest and fees mean you pay to save; missed payments hurt you |

**Before applying anywhere:** check whether a **student card** or a starter card from your own bank or credit union is available. If a family member with a long, clean card history will add you as an **authorised user**, that can help too, at no cost.

## How to use one so it actually builds credit

1. **Put one small recurring bill on it**, like a streaming service or phone bill.
2. **Turn on autopay for the full statement balance.** You never pay interest and never miss a payment.
3. **Keep the reported balance low.** Pay before the statement closing date if you've spent a lot; utilization matters.
4. **Don't apply for several cards at once.** Each application can mean a hard inquiry.
5. **After 6–12 months of on-time payments,** ask about upgrading to an unsecured card and getting your deposit back. Keep the account open if it has no annual fee: its age helps.

## Red flags

- Big upfront "processing" or "application" fees, or monthly maintenance fees.
- Anyone promising a specific score increase or to "repair" your credit for a fee. You can dispute errors yourself, free: [how to check your credit report](/blog/how-to-freeze-your-credit/).
- A product that doesn't report to all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian and TransUnion).

## Common questions

### How long does it take to build credit from scratch?
Scoring models generally need at least one account reported for several months before they can produce a score. Six to twelve months of on-time payments usually gets you to a point where better cards are possible.

### Do I get my deposit back?
On a secured card, yes, when you close the account in good standing or upgrade to an unsecured card. Check the card's terms for how and when.

### Will a credit-builder product hurt my score?
Applying can cause a small, temporary dip if there's a hard inquiry. Late payments hurt a lot. Used with autopay and a low balance, they're designed to help.

Bill negotiation services call your internet, phone, cable or insurance provider and ask for a lower price on your behalf. If they get one, they keep a share of the savings. If they don't, you usually pay nothing.

It's a real service with a real trade-off: convenience vs a cut of your savings. Here's how to decide, and how to do it yourself for free.

## How the fee works

Most services charge a **percentage of the first year's savings**, often paid upfront in one go, even though you save month by month.

Example (hypothetical): your internet bill drops by $20 a month. That's $240 over a year. At a 40% fee, you'd pay **$96** for the negotiation, and keep $144 of first-year savings. If you switch providers or the promo ends after six months, you may have paid for savings you didn't fully get.

## What can (and can't) be negotiated

| Usually negotiable | Rarely negotiable |
|---|---|
| Internet and cable | Rent (sometimes at renewal) |
| Mobile phone plans | Utilities with regulated rates (electric, water, in many places) |
| Car and renter's insurance (by shopping around) | Student loan interest rates (federal) |
| Some subscriptions and memberships | Taxes |
| Medical bills (ask about financial assistance and itemised bills) | |

## Do it yourself: the 15-minute script

**Before you call (5 minutes):** find your current monthly price, how long you've been a customer, and a competitor's current new-customer price for a similar plan.

**On the call:**

1. "Hi, I've been a customer for [X years] and my bill has gone up to $[amount]. I'm looking at [competitor], which is offering $[price] for similar service. I'd like to stay, but I need a better price. What can you do?"
2. If they say no: "Could you transfer me to the retention or loyalty department?" (Often reached by saying you're thinking of cancelling.)
3. If they offer something: "Is that the best you can do? Are there any current promotions for existing customers?"
4. Before you hang up: "Can you confirm the new monthly price, how long it lasts, and whether there's a contract or any fee?" Write it down, and ask for it in writing.

**Set a calendar reminder** for a month before any promo ends, and repeat.

## Medical bills are different

For medical bills, ask for an **itemised bill**, check it for errors and duplicates, and ask about the hospital's **financial assistance** policy. Many nonprofit hospitals have one. Some services specialise in medical bill review; understand their fees before signing anything.

## Common questions

### Is bill negotiation worth it?
Doing it yourself almost always is: 15 minutes for potential savings every month. Paying a service makes sense if you genuinely won't make the call.

### Will negotiating affect my credit score?
No. Asking for a lower price doesn't touch your credit.

### Is it safe to give a service my account details?
You're giving a third party access to your provider accounts. Read what they'll do with the information, and change passwords afterwards if you prefer.

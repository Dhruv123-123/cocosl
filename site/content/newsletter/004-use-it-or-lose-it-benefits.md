Hi there,

This week's money move: **find any money that expires at the end of the year, and use it on purpose.**

## 1. A flexible spending account (FSA)

If you put money into a health or dependent-care FSA through work this year, log in to the FSA site and check the balance. FSAs are "use it or lose it": money left at the end of the plan year can be forfeited. Some plans give a grace period into the new year or let you carry over a limited amount, so check your plan's rules on the same screen.

Things an FSA usually covers that people forget: prescription glasses and contact lenses, sunscreen, first-aid supplies, some over-the-counter medicines, and copays for appointments you were going to book anyway. Your plan's site lists what counts.

Not the same as an HSA. HSA money rolls over every year and stays yours, so there's no rush to spend it.

## 2. Benefits you're already paying for

Five minutes with your benefits portal and your credit card's benefits page:

- **Dental cleanings and eye exams.** Many plans cover one or two a year. Unused visits don't roll over.
- **Wellness or learning stipends.** Some employers reimburse gym memberships, courses or home-office gear, up to a yearly cap.
- **Card credits.** Some credit cards include yearly statement credits (travel, streaming, food delivery) that reset on January 1 or on your card anniversary.

## 3. Don't buy things just to "not waste" a credit

This only works for things you'd buy anyway. Spending $80 to use a $50 credit you didn't need isn't saving $50, it's spending $30.

Hi there,

This week's money move: **log in to your workplace retirement plan and raise your contribution by one percentage point.** If you put in 4%, make it 5%.

## Why now

Many plans take a pay cycle or two to apply a change, so doing it in November means it's in place for January's paychecks. And if a raise or cost-of-living increase is coming in the new year, the 1% disappears into it before you ever get used to the money.

## What it actually costs you

Less than you think. Traditional 401(k) contributions come out *before* income tax, so a 1% contribution shrinks your take-home pay by less than 1%. A hypothetical example: on a $50,000 salary, 1% is $500 a year, about $19 a paycheck if you're paid every two weeks. With taxes taken into account, your paycheck might drop by something closer to $15. (Your numbers will differ; your plan's website usually shows the paycheck effect before you confirm.)

## Check the match first

If your employer matches contributions and you're not putting in enough to get the full match, that's the first place your 1% should go. It's the only guaranteed return in personal finance. The [401(k) match explainer](/blog/401k-match-explained/) shows how to read your plan's match formula.

## If there's no workplace plan

Same move, different account: set up (or raise) an automatic monthly transfer into a Roth or traditional IRA. Not sure which? [Roth vs traditional IRA](/blog/roth-vs-traditional-ira/) covers the choice in plain English.

## Make it automatic

Many plans have an "automatic increase" setting that raises your contribution by 1% every year on a date you pick. Turn it on and this becomes the last time you have to remember.

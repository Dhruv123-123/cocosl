Hi there,

This week's money move is for anyone carrying a credit card balance: **call the number on the back of the card and ask for a lower interest rate.**

It doesn't always work. It costs nothing to ask, it doesn't affect your credit score, and if it does work, every future payment goes further.

## The script

> "Hi, I've been a customer since [year] and I pay on time. I'm working on paying down my balance, and my rate is [your APR]. I'd like to ask whether you can lower it. I'm also looking at balance transfer offers from other cards."

Then stop talking and let them answer.

## If they say no

- **Ask once more, politely:** "Is there anything you can do? Even a temporary reduction would help."
- **Ask about hardship programs** if money is genuinely tight. Many issuers have them, with lower rates or payment plans for a few months.
- **Say thank you and hang up.** You can try again in a few months, especially after your score improves.

## The backup plan: a balance transfer

A balance transfer card can let you pay 0% interest for an intro period, usually for a one-time fee of a few percent of the balance. It's worth it when you can pay off most of the balance before the intro period ends, and when you won't keep using the old card. We compare them, fees and catches included, in [balance transfer cards](/compare/best-balance-transfer-cards/).

## Whatever happens, point the savings at the debt

A lower rate only helps if the payment stays the same. Keep paying what you were paying, and the extra goes straight to the balance. [Avalanche vs snowball](/blog/debt-avalanche-vs-snowball/) helps you pick which card gets it first.

Hi there,

This week's money move: **spend 15 minutes planning January before December is over.**

January catches people out for three reasons that all arrive together: the holiday card statement, yearly renewals (subscriptions, memberships, insurance, car registration), and, for many people, a long stretch between the last December paycheck and the first one in January.

## 1. Count your January paychecks

Open your calendar and count how many paydays land in January, and when. If the first one comes late in the month, December's last paycheck has to cover more than usual. Leave enough of it in your account.

## 2. List what renews

Search your email for "renew", "annual" and "membership", and check last January's bank statement. Write down every yearly charge and its amount. If something renews that you don't use, cancel it now, before the charge. [Find and cancel subscriptions](/blog/find-and-cancel-subscriptions/) walks through it.

## 3. Write down the holiday total

If you set a holiday number back in issue 1, check how close you came. If it's on a credit card, plan to pay the statement in full when it arrives. If you can't, that's useful to know now: it becomes January's first goal.

## 4. Set a January weekly number

Take January's take-home pay, subtract bills, renewals and the holiday balance, and divide what's left by the number of weeks. That's your January weekly number. It might be smaller than usual. Knowing that on January 1 is much better than finding out on January 20.

Hi there,

This week's money move: **pull your credit reports from all three bureaus and skim them for anything that isn't yours.**

## Where to get them

Go to **AnnualCreditReport.com**. It's the official free site set up under federal law, and you can get reports from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion there. You don't need a credit card, and you don't need to sign up for anything. If a site asks you to pay or start a "free trial", you're on the wrong site.

Checking your own reports is a "soft" inquiry. It does not lower your score.

## What to look for (ten minutes)

1. **Accounts you don't recognise.** Cards or loans you never opened are the big red flag for identity theft.
2. **Wrong personal details.** Addresses you never lived at, names that aren't yours.
3. **Late payments that weren't late.** Mistakes happen, and they cost points.
4. **Hard inquiries you didn't make.** Those are applications for credit in your name.

## If something's wrong

You can dispute errors directly with the bureau that shows them, online, for free. The bureau generally has to investigate, and it must remove or fix information it can't verify. If you find an account that's clearly fraud, freeze your credit at all three bureaus first (also free); [how to freeze your credit](/blog/how-to-freeze-your-credit/) has the steps.

## While you're there

Your report isn't your score, but it's what your score is built from. If you want to understand what moves the number, [how credit scores work](/blog/how-credit-scores-work/) explains it without the jargon.

## What's in the seven emails

### Day 1: Your real take-home pay
Not your salary: what actually lands in your account each month, and why paid-every-two-weeks means 26 paychecks, not 24. Task: work out your monthly number from one payslip. Guide: [How to make a budget you will keep](/blog/how-to-make-a-budget-you-will-keep/).

### Day 2: The subscription hunt
Search your statements for renewals and ask one question of each charge: would I sign up for this today, at this price? Task: cancel one. Guide: [How to find and cancel forgotten subscriptions](/blog/find-and-cancel-subscriptions/).

### Day 3: The bills that aren't on your list yet
Car registration, renewals and gifts, each divided by 12 so they stop being surprises. Task: list last year's non-monthly bills. Guide: [Sinking funds, explained](/blog/sinking-funds-explained/).

### Day 4: A starter emergency fund
Why a small buffer comes before almost everything else, and where to keep it. Task: open or name a separate savings pot and set up one automatic transfer. Guide: [How to build an emergency fund](/blog/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund/).

### Day 5: Pick your debt plan
Avalanche (highest interest first) or snowball (smallest balance first), with the trade-off in dollars. Task: list your debts and choose one. Guide: [Debt avalanche vs snowball](/blog/debt-avalanche-vs-snowball/).

### Day 6: Your credit, checked and protected
What actually moves a credit score, how to pull your free reports, and when a free credit freeze makes sense. Task: request your reports. Guide: [How credit scores work](/blog/how-credit-scores-work/).

### Day 7: Your first step into investing
The employer match first, then small, regular amounts into low-cost funds. Task: check whether you're getting your full match. Guide: [The 401(k) match, explained](/blog/401k-match-explained/).

## How it works

You get one email a day for seven days, starting the day you sign up. Each one is a single idea with the numbers shown, a guide to read if you want the detail, and one task that takes less than 20 minutes. After Day 7 you'll get the regular weekly Money Made Simple email, and you can unsubscribe in one click at any time.

You don't need an app or a bank login: a notes app or a spreadsheet is enough. Our [free tools](/tools/) help with some of the tasks if you want them.

## Common questions

### Is Money Basics in 7 days free?
Yes. It's seven emails, and you can unsubscribe in one click. After the course you'll get the weekly Money Made Simple email, which you can also stop at any time.

### Who is the course for?
Anyone starting out with money or starting over: a first job, a first apartment, or a fresh start after a hard year. If you already have a budget, an emergency fund and a debt plan, our [Start page](/start/) may be a better fit.

### How long does each day take?
About 20 minutes or less. Reading the email takes a couple of minutes, and each task is one small step you can finish the same day.

### Is this financial advice?
No. It's general education with the numbers shown. For decisions about your own situation, talk to a qualified professional.

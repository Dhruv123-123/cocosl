Money Made Simple is for students and early-career workers who feel behind on money. Every guide here is one idea you can use this week, with the numbers shown.

**Pick the goal that sounds most like you right now.** Each one has three first steps and a short reading list, in order. If more than one fits, start with debt or savings: they make everything else easier.

{{start:goals}}

Not sure? Take the 1-minute [money personality quiz](/quiz/), or start the free [7-Day Money Reset](/free/): seven 15-minute tasks that get your money organised in a week.

## Pay off debt

For credit card balances, loans, or a bill you're behind on.

1. **List every debt** with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment. Day 1 of the [free reset](/free/) walks you through it.
2. **Pick an order.** The [avalanche or snowball method](/blog/debt-avalanche-vs-snowball/) decides which debt gets your extra money, and the [debt payoff calculator](/debt-payoff/) shows how long each takes.
3. **Pay a fixed amount, not the minimum,** and stop adding to the card you're paying off. Then see whether a lower rate would help: [balance transfer vs personal loan](/compare/balance-transfer-vs-personal-loan/).

{{start:debt}}

## Build savings

For anyone with no cushion, or whose plans keep getting wrecked by surprise bills.

1. **Build a $1,000 starter emergency fund**, then grow it toward a few months of expenses: [the 8-week plan](/blog/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund/).
2. **Turn predictable costs into sinking funds** (car repairs, gifts, annual bills), so they stop being emergencies: [sinking funds](/blog/sinking-funds-explained/).
3. **Keep it in an insured high-yield account**, separate from checking: [best high-yield savings accounts](/compare/best-high-yield-savings-accounts/).

{{start:saving}}

## Start your first job

For your first full-time paycheck, or your first job with benefits.

1. **Check your first paycheck** against your salary. [Budget by salary](/budget/) shows typical take-home pay and a monthly plan for eight salary levels.
2. **Get the full 401(k) match** if your employer offers one. It's the best return available to most people: [the match, explained](/blog/401k-match-explained/).
3. **Set up a budget you'll keep**, built on one weekly spending number: [the four-line budget](/blog/how-to-make-a-budget-you-will-keep/).

{{start:first-job}}

## Freelance or side income

For freelancers, gig workers, and anyone whose pay changes from month to month.

1. **Separate the money.** Open a savings account just for income and another just for tax. Any [high-yield savings account](/compare/best-high-yield-savings-accounts/) with free extra accounts works.
2. **Set aside a share of every payment for tax**, from the day it arrives. Many freelancers start with 25% to 30%.
3. **Pay yourself a steady amount** each month from the income account, and build a buffer from the good months. [Sinking funds](/blog/sinking-funds-explained/) handle the irregular bills.

{{start:freelance}}

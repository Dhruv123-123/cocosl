Before and after you send it

  • Timing: the collector's first letter (the validation notice) gives a date. Dispute in writing by then: the collector must stop collecting until it verifies the debt. You can still dispute after that date, but those protections may not apply.
  • Mail it by certified mail with a return receipt, keep a copy, and note the date.
  • Don't pay or promise to pay until you've checked the debt is yours and the amount is right. In some states, a payment on an old debt can restart the time limit for being sued.
  • Check your credit reports for free at AnnualCreditReport.com and dispute anything that's wrong with the bureau.
  • If you're sued, respond to the court by the deadline on the papers, even if you think the debt is wrong. Ignoring it usually means losing automatically.
  • Problems with a collector? You can submit a complaint to the CFPB at consumerfinance.gov/complaint or to your state attorney general.

Questions

What is a debt validation letter?

A letter telling a debt collector you dispute the debt and asking them to show who the original creditor was, how the amount was worked out, and that they can collect it. If you dispute in writing within the 30-day period in the collector's validation notice, the collector must stop collecting until it sends you verification.

Does a stop-contact letter make the debt go away?

No. The collector must stop contacting you, with limited exceptions, but the debt still exists. The collector or creditor can still sue you or report the debt to credit bureaus. Many people send the validation letter first.

Is it a medical bill?

Ask the hospital or provider for an itemized bill and about financial assistance too: medical bill letters.

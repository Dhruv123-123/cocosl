Stop. Check. Call.

Put this page on the fridge.

STOPAny message that rushes you, scares you, or excites you gets a pause. Real problems can wait 10 minutes.
CHECKDon't use the link or number in the message. Open the official app, or type the website yourself.
CALLCall someone you trust before you pay or share anything. Use the number on your card or bill.

12 red flags

  • Pay with gift cards, crypto, wire or Zelle. No real company or agency asks for this.
  • "Read me the code we just sent you." That code unlocks your account. Never share it.
  • A deadline in hours. "Pay within 24 hours or…" is pressure, not policy.
  • Threats. Arrest, deportation, a frozen account, a suspended Social Security number.
  • "Keep this between us." Being told not to tell your bank or family.
  • A prize you never entered. Especially one with a fee to claim it.
  • A link that's almost right. usps-help.top, paypa1.com, amazon.account-check.xyz.
  • Move money to "keep it safe". Banks never ask you to move money to protect it.
  • Install AnyDesk or TeamViewer. Remote access lets them into your bank.
  • A friendly wrong number. That turns into talk of crypto or investing.
  • A job that pays for "likes" or tasks. And later asks you to deposit money.
  • A family emergency, by phone or text. "Grandma, I'm in jail, don't tell Mom." Voices can be faked with AI.

What to say when a scammer calls

You don't owe a caller anything, including politeness. These lines end the call without arguing.

"This is your bank's fraud department."

"We've seen suspicious activity. To protect your money we need you to move it to a secure account / read us the code we sent."

"I'll call the number on the back of my card." Then hang up and do it.

"This is the IRS / Social Security / the police."

"There's a warrant for your arrest. Pay today with gift cards to resolve it."

"Send me a letter." Then hang up. Agencies contact you by mail first and never demand gift cards or crypto.

"Grandma, it's me. I'm in trouble."

"I was in an accident / I'm in jail. Please don't tell Mom. My lawyer will tell you how to send bail."

"What's our family safe word?" Then hang up and call your grandchild or their parents on the number you already have.

"Microsoft / Apple / Geek Squad here. Your computer is infected."

"Install this app so we can fix it / process your refund."

"No thanks." Hang up. Never install remote-access apps for someone who contacted you.

"I'm a buyer from Marketplace. I sent you a code to prove you're real."

"Can you send me the code?"

"No, let's keep it on Marketplace." Never forward a code.

A simple rule for everyone: if they called you, you call them back on a number you find yourself.

Your family safe word

AI tools can copy a voice from a few seconds of video. The FBI suggests families agree on a secret word or phrase to check a caller is really who they say. Set yours up together, today.

  • Pick a word or short phrase that isn't online anywhere: not a pet's name, street, team, or anything in your social media.
  • Tell it only in person. Don't text it, email it, or write it on the wallet card.
  • Agree on the rule: no word, no money, however real the voice sounds or however urgent it is.
  • Agree who to call to double-check (for example, the parent of the grandchild).
  • Practise once: one person makes a pretend "emergency" call, the other asks for the word.
  • Change it if it's ever used in front of someone outside the family.

Talk it through

Ten minutes at the next family dinner covers it. Questions to ask each other:

  • Have you had a strange call or text this year? What did it want?
  • Which payment apps do you use (Zelle, Venmo, Cash App)? Who can see them?
  • Who would you call before sending money in a hurry?
  • Do you know how to forward a scam text to 7726?

The 30-minute account lockdown

Do this once for each adult in the family. Tick as you go.

Credit (10 min)

  • Freeze credit at Equifax (equifax.com)
  • Freeze credit at Experian (experian.com)
  • Freeze credit at TransUnion (transunion.com)
  • Freezes are free and you can lift them in minutes when you need credit.
  • Check your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com

Phone (5 min)

  • Add a number-transfer (port-out) PIN or lock with your mobile carrier
  • Turn on your phone's built-in spam call and text filtering
  • Keep the phone's software up to date

Email and banking (15 min)

  • Turn on two-step login for email first (it resets everything else)
  • Turn on two-step login for your bank and payment apps
  • Turn on transaction alerts for every card and account
  • Use a password manager so every password is different
  • Set payment apps (Zelle, Venmo, Cash App) to private and only pay people you know

Optional

  • Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN at irs.gov/ippin
  • Create your my Social Security account at ssa.gov so no one else can

"I think I've been scammed"

Don't be embarrassed. Act fast. Scammers are professionals and they target smart people. Money is easier to recover in the first hours.

If you…Do this now
Paid by cardCall the number on the back of the card. Ask them to dispute the charge and replace the card.
Sent a bank transfer, wire or ZelleCall your bank's fraud line immediately and ask them to recall or reverse the payment.
Paid with gift cardsCall the gift card company (number on the back of the card) and tell them it was a scam. Keep the cards and receipts.
Sent cryptoContact the exchange or crypto ATM operator you used and report it. Save the wallet address and transaction ID.
Gave a password or codeChange that password now, sign out other devices, and turn on two-step login.
Gave your Social Security numberFreeze your credit at all three bureaus and follow the plan at IdentityTheft.gov.
Let someone into your computerDisconnect it from the internet. Change passwords from a different device. Have the computer checked.

Then report it

ReportFraud.ftc.gov · IC3.gov (internet crime) · forward texts to 7726 · your local police (you may need a report for your bank)

Write it down while it's fresh

Date and time
How they contacted me
Names and numbers they used
What I paid, how, and to where
Who I've called (and case numbers)

Beware "recovery" scams: anyone who contacts you offering to get your money back for a fee is another scammer.

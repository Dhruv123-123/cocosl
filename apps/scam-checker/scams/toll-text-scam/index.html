Is this unpaid toll text a scam?

Texts claiming you owe an unpaid toll are one of the most common scams right now. How to spot them and check your real toll account.

What it looks like

E-ZPass: You have an outstanding toll balance of $6.99. To avoid a late fee of $50.00 and suspension of your driving privileges, pay now: https://ezpass-tollpay.vip/us

Reply Y and reopen this message to activate the link.

An illustration based on reported messages. Names, amounts and links are made up.

Signs it's a scam

  • A link that is not your toll agency's official site
  • A late-fee or license-suspension threat with a short deadline
  • A toll agency for a state you haven't driven in
  • "Reply Y" to make the link clickable

What the real one does

Toll agencies bill through your account or mail an invoice to the registered owner. The FBI and FTC have warned about mass text campaigns pretending to be toll services since 2024.

How to check safely

Log in to your toll account through the agency's own app or a web address you type yourself, or call the number on your transponder statement.

If you already clicked or replied

You only opened the messageYou're fine. Delete it and block the sender.
You clicked the link but typed nothingClose the page. Don't download anything it offers. Keep your phone updated.
You typed a passwordChange that password now, plus anywhere you reused it, and turn on two-factor login.
You entered card or bank detailsCall your bank or card issuer using the number on your card and ask them to block it.
You paid by gift card, wire, Zelle or cryptoContact the company you paid through right away (gift card company, bank, or exchange) and report it. Speed matters.
You gave your Social Security numberFreeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion (free) and see IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan.

Report it

Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM). Report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and internet crime or money lost at IC3.gov.

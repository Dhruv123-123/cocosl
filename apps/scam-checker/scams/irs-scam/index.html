Is this IRS message a scam?

The IRS does not text, email or call demanding immediate payment. Here are the signs of an IRS impersonation scam and how to check what you really owe.

What it looks like

IRS NOTICE: You owe $2,840 in back taxes. A warrant has been issued for your arrest. Pay immediately with Apple gift cards or Bitcoin to stop legal action. Do not tell your bank.

Signs it's a scam

  • Threats of arrest, deportation or license loss
  • Payment by gift card, crypto, wire or payment app
  • A text, email or social media message as first contact
  • Being told not to talk to anyone

What the real one does

The IRS usually contacts you first by mail. It does not demand immediate payment by a specific method, ask for gift cards or crypto, or threaten to have you arrested.

How to check safely

Sign in to your IRS Online Account at irs.gov (typed yourself) to see any balance, or call the IRS at the number on irs.gov.

If you already clicked or replied

You only opened the messageYou're fine. Delete it and block the sender.
You clicked the link but typed nothingClose the page. Don't download anything it offers. Keep your phone updated.
You typed a passwordChange that password now, plus anywhere you reused it, and turn on two-factor login.
You entered card or bank detailsCall your bank or card issuer using the number on your card and ask them to block it.
You paid by gift card, wire, Zelle or cryptoContact the company you paid through right away (gift card company, bank, or exchange) and report it. Speed matters.
You gave your Social Security numberFreeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion (free) and see IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan.

Report it

Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM). Report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and internet crime or money lost at IC3.gov.

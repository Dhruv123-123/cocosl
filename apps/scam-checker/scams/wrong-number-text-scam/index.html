Is this "wrong number" text a scam?

A friendly "wrong number" text can be the start of a long investment scam. Here is how it unfolds and why you should not reply.

What it looks like

Hi, is this David? My assistant saved your number for our golf meeting on Saturday. Sorry to bother you! By the way, I've been doing really well with a crypto trading platform, guaranteed returns of 20% a month.

An illustration based on reported messages. Names, amounts and links are made up.

Signs it's a scam

  • A chatty message meant for "someone else"
  • An attractive, successful stranger who wants to keep talking
  • Talk of a trading app, crypto, or a mentor with guaranteed returns
  • Small early "profits" that you can see but not withdraw

What the real one does

Nobody can guarantee investment returns. These scams build trust over weeks, then steer you to a fake trading site that shows gains you can't withdraw.

How to check safely

Don't reply, and block the number. If you're already investing, try to withdraw a small amount: if you're told to pay a "tax" or "fee" first, stop and call your bank.

If you already clicked or replied

You only opened the messageYou're fine. Delete it and block the sender.
You clicked the link but typed nothingClose the page. Don't download anything it offers. Keep your phone updated.
You typed a passwordChange that password now, plus anywhere you reused it, and turn on two-factor login.
You entered card or bank detailsCall your bank or card issuer using the number on your card and ask them to block it.
You paid by gift card, wire, Zelle or cryptoContact the company you paid through right away (gift card company, bank, or exchange) and report it. Speed matters.
You gave your Social Security numberFreeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion (free) and see IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan.

Report it

Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM). Report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and internet crime or money lost at IC3.gov.

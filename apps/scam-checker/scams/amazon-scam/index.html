Is this Amazon message a scam?

Fake Amazon messages about suspicious orders, locked accounts or refunds are common. Here is how to check, using only your Amazon app.

What it looks like

Amazon: We detected a suspicious purchase of an iPhone 15 Pro ($1,149.00) on your account. If this was not you, verify your account immediately at http://amaz0n-orders-help.com or call our support line.

An illustration based on reported messages. Names, amounts and links are made up.

Signs it's a scam

  • An order you don't recognise, with a link or phone number to "cancel" it
  • A web address that only looks like Amazon (here a zero replaces the "o")
  • Pressure to act now or your account will be locked
  • Requests to install an app, buy gift cards, or pay outside amazon.com

What the real one does

Amazon says it will never ask you to pay outside its website or app, ask for remote access to your device, or ask for gift cards as payment. Real orders and messages show up in Your Orders and your Message Center.

How to check safely

Open the Amazon app or type amazon.com, then check Your Orders and Message Center. If the order isn't there, the message is fake.

If you already clicked or replied

You only opened the messageYou're fine. Delete it and block the sender.
You clicked the link but typed nothingClose the page. Don't download anything it offers. Keep your phone updated.
You typed a passwordChange that password now, plus anywhere you reused it, and turn on two-factor login.
You entered card or bank detailsCall your bank or card issuer using the number on your card and ask them to block it.
You paid by gift card, wire, Zelle or cryptoContact the company you paid through right away (gift card company, bank, or exchange) and report it. Speed matters.
You gave your Social Security numberFreeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion (free) and see IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan.

Report it

Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM). Report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and internet crime or money lost at IC3.gov.

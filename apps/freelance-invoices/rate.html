    Questions

    How do I work out my freelance hourly rate?

    Start from the take-home pay you want, add the taxes an employer would otherwise pay part of (self-employment tax is 15.3% on 92.35% of profit), health insurance, retirement savings and business costs, then divide by the hours you can actually bill. Most freelancers bill 50% to 70% of the hours they work.

    Why is my rate so much higher than my old hourly wage?

    An employer pays half of Social Security and Medicare, your health insurance, paid time off and your equipment. As a freelancer you pay all of that from your rate, and only some of your working hours are billable, so a rate around double your old hourly wage is common.

    What counts as billable hours?

    Time a client pays for. Finding work, proposals, invoicing, admin, learning and unpaid revisions are not billable. If you are not sure, track a normal week: the share you could put on an invoice is your billable percentage.

    Should I charge by the hour or by the project?

    Use this hourly rate to price projects: estimate the hours, add 20% for surprises, and multiply. Quoting a project price instead of hours lets you keep the gain when you work faster.

    How is this worked out?

    Self-employment tax is 15.3% on 92.35% of profit (the 12.4% Social Security part stops at the 2026 wage base of $184,500). Income tax is at your bracket on profit after half of self-employment tax, health insurance, pre-tax retirement savings and the 20% qualified business income deduction; state tax is a flat rate on the same base. Real returns have credits, other income and several brackets, so treat it as a planning number. The rate is rounded up to the next $5.

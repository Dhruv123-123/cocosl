Business banking for freelancers

A separate account for client money is the cheapest tax tool there is. It makes your profit number obvious and keeps the tax you owe out of reach. Here is how the popular freelancer accounts compare.

How much to move into the tax account

Take the set-aside rate from the calculator and move that share of every client payment the day it lands. On $60,000 of profit a single filer owes about 20% in 2026 federal tax, before state tax, which is why 25–30% is a common rule of thumb.

FDIC insurance and fintech apps

Most freelancer accounts are run by fintech companies, and the deposits sit at a partner bank. FDIC insurance only protects you if that bank fails, and only when the app keeps accurate records of who owns what. The 2024 Synapse collapse froze money at several apps for months. Before you move your tax money, find the partner bank's name on the app's site, check it on the FDIC's BankFind, and keep balances under the insured limit.

Do I legally need a business account?

A sole proprietor isn't required to have one, but mixing personal and business money makes taxes, audits and your own profit number much harder. An LLC or S corp should always keep its money separate.

Questions

What is the best bank account for a freelancer?

One with no monthly fee, a separate bucket or account for taxes, and clear FDIC coverage. Found and Lili build the tax set-aside in; Relay and Novo let you build it yourself; Bluevine pays interest on larger balances.

Should my tax savings earn interest?

It helps, but safety and access come first: you need the money on four fixed dates a year. A high-yield savings account at an FDIC-insured bank is a fine place for it.

Can I use a personal account for freelance income?

Yes, as a sole proprietor. A separate account just makes tracking and taxes far easier.

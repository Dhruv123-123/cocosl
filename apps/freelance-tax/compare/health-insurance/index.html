The tax side

Premiums you pay as a self-employed person are usually deductible on Schedule 1 (the self-employed health insurance deduction), as long as you weren't eligible for a spouse's or employer's subsidized plan that month. It lowers income tax, not self-employment tax. Your marketplace subsidy is settled on Form 8962 when you file, so estimate income carefully: freelance income swings, and a low guess means paying some back.

HSAs

A health savings account with an HSA-eligible plan is a triple tax break: deductible in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical costs. 2026 limits are $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family. From 2026, marketplace bronze and catastrophic plans count as HSA-eligible; check the plan details before you open one.

Other routes

A spouse's or partner's job-based plan is often cheapest. COBRA keeps your old employer plan for up to 18 months, usually at full price. Short-term plans are cheaper because they can refuse pre-existing conditions and skip key benefits; read the exclusions before you rely on one.

Questions

How do freelancers get health insurance?

Most use an ACA marketplace plan through HealthCare.gov or their state's exchange, with a premium tax credit based on expected income. Others join a spouse's plan or use COBRA for a while.

Are health insurance premiums tax deductible for the self-employed?

Usually yes, through the self-employed health insurance deduction on Schedule 1, if you had a profit and weren't eligible for an employer-subsidized plan that month.

Is HealthSherpa legit?

Yes. It is an approved web broker that enrolls you in the same marketplace plans with the same subsidies.

S corp questions, answered

Where does the saving come from?

As a sole proprietor, all of your profit carries self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of it, up to the Social Security wage base ($184,500 for 2026), then 2.9%. As an S corp, only the salary carries payroll tax (7.65% from you plus 7.65% from the business). Profit above the salary comes out as a distribution without it. Income tax still applies to all of it.

What does it cost?

Running payroll for yourself, a separate business tax return (Form 1120-S) every year, federal unemployment tax on the first $7,000 of salary ($42 after the usual state credit), state unemployment tax in most states, and in some states an annual S corp tax or fee. Two more costs don't show up as a bill: the qualified business income (QBI) deduction only applies to the profit after salary, and a lower salary means lower Social Security earnings on your record and, with a Solo 401(k), a lower employer contribution limit.

What's a reasonable salary?

The IRS says S corporation officers who work in the business must be paid reasonable compensation before taking distributions, and it can reclassify distributions as wages if the salary is too low. There's no set percentage. A common approach is what you'd have to pay someone else to do your job. This calculator uses whatever salary you enter; it doesn't tell you what's reasonable.

When can I elect?

You form an LLC or corporation under state law, then file Form 2553. For the election to apply to the current year, it's generally due within 2 months and 15 days of the start of the tax year (March 15 for a calendar year), with relief available for late elections in some cases. See the S corp services comparison for providers that run payroll and the return for you.

What does this leave out?

State income tax (often similar either way) and state S corp taxes beyond what you enter as extra cost, health insurance (for a more-than-2% shareholder it goes through payroll), retirement contributions, credits and other household income. It's built for profits up to $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (joint), below the Additional Medicare Tax thresholds; above that, the result is flagged. Talk to a CPA or enrolled agent before electing.

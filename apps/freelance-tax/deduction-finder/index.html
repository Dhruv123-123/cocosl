    Questions

    What makes something deductible?

    It has to be ordinary and necessary for your business: common in your line of work and helpful for it. Anything you also use personally counts only for the business share. And you need a record: a receipt, a statement line or a log.

    How much does a deduction actually save?

    Less than people think, and more than nothing. Business expenses on Schedule C lower both income tax and self-employment tax: at $60,000 of profit, about $23 of 2026 federal tax for every $100. Adjustments such as health insurance, an IRA or an HSA lower income tax only, about $10 per $100 at that income. A deduction is a discount, never a reason to buy something.

    Are some deductions audit red flags?

    Deductions aren't risky; unsupported ones are. Round numbers, a home office that isn't exclusive, 100% business use of a car you also drive personally, and personal costs labeled as business are what cause trouble. Keep the records and they're routine.

    What isn't on this list?

    Commuting, everyday clothes, gym memberships, personal meals, and your own pay (an owner's draw isn't an expense). Also beyond this checklist: depreciation on a home you own, the actual-expense car method, inventory and cost of goods sold. For those, see IRS Publications 334 and 946 or a tax professional.

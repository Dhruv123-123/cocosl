About the January payment

Why is the fourth payment in January?

The IRS splits the year unevenly: April 15 covers January to March, June 15 covers April and May, Sept 15 covers June to August, and January 15 covers the last four months, September to December. So the January payment is often the biggest one for people whose income picked up late in the year.

How do I pay it?

Free at irs.gov/payments with IRS Direct Pay or your IRS online account. Choose "Estimated Tax", Form 1040-ES, and tax year 2026. That last choice is the one people get wrong in January, because it feels like a 2027 payment. Save the confirmation number.

Can I skip it?

Yes, if you file your 2026 return and pay everything you owe by February 1, 2027 (January 31 is a Sunday). That removes any penalty for the fourth quarter. It doesn't undo a penalty for earlier quarters you underpaid, and it means having every 1099 and your books closed in two weeks, so most freelancers just pay on the 15th.

I missed a payment earlier in the year. Now what?

The calculator shows the catch-up part separately. The underpayment penalty works like interest on each late amount for each day it's late, so a missed June payment has already cost a little, and paying it now stops it growing. Don't wait for April: the cost keeps running until the shortfall is paid.

What if my income was much higher than last year?

Use the safe harbor: if your payments for 2026 add up to 100% of your 2025 total tax (110% if your 2025 AGI was over $150,000), you generally owe no penalty, even if 2026's bill is much bigger. Enter your 2025 numbers above and the calculator picks the smaller requirement. You still owe the rest in April.

And my state?

Most states with an income tax also have a January payment for the prior year, usually on or near January 15. Check your state tax agency's estimated tax page.

Freelance invoices that get paid

What does a freelance invoice need?

Your name or business and an email, the client's name, a unique invoice number, the invoice date, a due date, a line for each piece of work with the quantity and rate, the total due, and how to pay you. If your contract has a late fee, repeat it on the invoice. The checklist next to the form tells you what's still missing.

Why does the due date show as a date?

"Net 30" makes someone count from a date they may not have in front of them. "Due July 1, 2027" doesn't. The template works out the date from your terms and prints both.

How should I number invoices?

Pick a pattern that never repeats, such as the year and a running number: 2027-001, 2027-002. "Start the next invoice" keeps your details and moves to the next number.

Can I add a late fee?

Only if the client agreed to it before the work, usually in your contract or proposal. Putting it on the invoice reminds them; it doesn't create the agreement. Some states limit how much you can charge, so check yours. This isn't legal advice.

Do I charge sales tax?

Most states don't tax most freelance services, but some tax many of them, and digital products have their own rules. Leave it at 0 unless your state's revenue department says your service is taxable and you're registered to collect it.

What happens to my data?

Nothing is sent anywhere. Your details are kept in this browser so the form is filled in next time; clear your browser data to remove them. Use a private window on a shared computer.

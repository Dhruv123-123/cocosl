Hi,

The quiet week between the holidays is the best time to work out the **January 15, 2027** payment, the 4th and last one for your **2026** income. Ten minutes now beats an hour on January 14.

**1. Write down three numbers**

- Your 2026 profit: income received minus business expenses, January to December. A close estimate is fine.
- What you've paid in estimated tax for 2026 so far (your IRS online account lists every payment).
- Your 2025 total tax, from the "total tax" line of last year's Form 1040.

**2. Work out the payment**

Your 2026 payments need to reach the *smaller* of:
- 100% of your 2025 total tax (110% if your 2025 AGI was over $150,000), or
- 90% of your 2026 tax.

Whatever gets you there is the January 15 amount. If you missed an earlier payment, it includes the catch-up, and paying it now stops the penalty on it from growing. The calculator does all of this and splits out the catch-up: {{q4_url}}

**3. Or skip January 15, if your books are done**

You can skip the payment if you file your 2026 return and pay everything by **Monday, February 1, 2027**. It only covers the fourth quarter, though, and your 1099s may not all be in by then. Most people just pay on the 15th.

**What arrives in January**

- **1099-NEC** from clients who paid you **$2,000 or more** in 2026 (the threshold was $600 until this year). Box 1 is what they say they paid you. Check it against your own records.
- **1099-K** from payment apps and marketplaces, required above $20,000 and 200 payments but often sent below that. It shows the *gross*, before fees and refunds. Report the gross, then deduct the fees.
- **Income with no form is still income.** Your own log is the source of truth; the forms are what the IRS compares it with.

When you pay, choose **tax year 2026**, not 2027. We'll send a reminder a week before.

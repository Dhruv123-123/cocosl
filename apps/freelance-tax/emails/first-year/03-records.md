Hi,

Good records do two jobs: they make filing quick, and they prove your numbers if the IRS ever asks. Here's the minimum.

**Keep:**
- **Income:** every invoice, and a record of when and how it was paid. Report all of it, whether or not you get a 1099.
- **Expenses:** the receipt or invoice, plus a few words on the business purpose for meals, travel and gifts. A bank statement shows you paid; it usually doesn't show what you bought or why.
- **Car:** a mileage log kept as you go: date, where, why, miles. The 2026 standard rate is **72.5 cents a mile** to June 30 and **76 cents** from July 1. Commuting to a regular workplace doesn't count.
- **Equipment:** what you paid and when you started using it.

Photos and scans are fine. A folder per year with a subfolder per month works.

**Startup costs.** Money you spent getting ready before your first client, like a course, a logo, a website or market research, is generally deductible up to **$5,000** in your first year, with the rest spread over 15 years. Go back through your bank statements from before your first invoice and collect them.

**The 5-minute weekly habit:** every Friday, photograph any new receipts and give each business transaction a category that matches a Schedule C line (advertising, car, contract labor, office expense, supplies, travel, meals).

**Do this today:** start your mileage log, and make a "startup costs" note listing anything you bought before your first client.

Sources: irs.gov recordkeeping pages, Publication 463 (travel, car, meals), Publication 334 (startup costs).

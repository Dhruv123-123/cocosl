Hi,

Two things your employer used to arrange are now yours.

**Health insurance**
- **Marketplace plans** (healthcare.gov or your state's site): open enrollment for the next year usually runs from November 1 to January 15. Outside that window, events like losing other coverage, marriage, a birth or a move generally give you **60 days** to enroll.
- **Estimate your income carefully.** Your premium tax credit depends on it, and it's settled on your tax return.
- **The deduction:** if you weren't eligible for an employer-subsidized plan (including a spouse's), premiums you pay for yourself and your family are generally deductible, up to your business profit. It lowers income tax, not self-employment tax.
- **HSA:** with an HSA-eligible plan, contributions are deductible too.

**Retirement**
- **IRA:** up to **$7,500** for 2026 ($8,600 if you're 50 or older).
- **SEP IRA:** about **20% of your profit** after half of self-employment tax. At $60,000 of profit that's about **$11,150**. You can open and fund it up to your filing deadline, extensions included.
- **Solo 401(k):** adds an employee deferral of up to **$24,500** for 2026 on top of the same 20%. At $60,000 of profit, about **$35,650** in total. Check the deadlines with the provider before you rely on a late start.
- Traditional contributions lower income tax, not self-employment tax.

You don't need to decide amounts today. Decide which account, and start with a percentage you'll actually keep.

**Do this today:** put November 1 in your calendar for open enrollment, and pick the retirement account you'll open this year.

Sources: healthcare.gov, irs.gov (Form 7206, SEP and one-participant 401(k) pages, 2026 limits).

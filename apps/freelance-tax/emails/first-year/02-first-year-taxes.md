Hi,

At a job, tax came out of every paycheck and you settled up in April. Freelancing changes three things.

**1. Nobody withholds anything.** A client pays you the full invoice. All of the tax is yours to set aside and pay.

**2. There's a second tax.** Self-employment tax is Social Security and Medicare, both halves: 15.3% on 92.35% of your profit, once your net earnings reach $400. It's on top of income tax. At $60,000 of profit, single, with 2026 figures, it's about **$8,478** of the **$12,037** total federal tax.

**3. You pay during the year.** If you'll owe $1,000 or more after any withholding, the IRS expects four estimated payments. For 2027 income they're due **Apr 15, Jun 15 and Sep 15, 2027, and Jan 18, 2028**.

**The first-year rule that helps.** You generally avoid the underpayment penalty if your payments cover the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or **100% of last year's total tax** (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000).

In your first year, "last year" is often a year you had a job. Example (fictional): last year your total tax on your W-2 return was $5,500. Pay $1,375 by each deadline and there's generally no penalty, even if your freelance year turns out bigger. You'd still owe the rest in April, which is what your tax savings account is for.

And if you had **no tax liability at all last year** (and were a US citizen or resident the whole year), there's generally no estimated-tax penalty for this year. You still owe the tax, just without the penalty.

**Do this today:** find last year's total tax on your Form 1040 (line 24) and divide it by four. That's your safe-harbor payment.

Sources: irs.gov/estimated-taxes, Form 2210 instructions.

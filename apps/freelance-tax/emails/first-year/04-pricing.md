Hi,

Most new freelancers copy someone else's rate. That rate carries their costs, their hours and their life, not yours. Work backwards instead.

**Example (fictional), with 2026 federal figures:**
1. Take-home you want: **$55,000**.
2. What an employer used to cover: health insurance **$6,000** and retirement **$5,000**. You need **$66,000** after tax.
3. Gross it up for self-employment and income tax: about **$84,000** of profit.
4. Add business costs of **$6,000**: **$90,000** of revenue.
5. Billable hours, honestly: 25 a week for 48 weeks is **1,200**, not 2,080. Email, proposals and admin aren't billable.
6. **$90,000 ÷ 1,200 = $75 an hour.** That's your floor.

Then check the market. If people in your niche pay more, charge more. If they pay less than your floor, the fix is usually a narrower niche or packaged projects, not a rate you can't live on.

**Two rules for year one:**
- **Trade scope, not rate.** "I can do it for $8,000 if we drop this part."
- **Raise rates on a schedule.** Every January, with 30 to 60 days' notice to existing clients. New clients get the new rate straight away.

Hi,

The difference between a good freelance year and a stressful one is often not the rate. It's when the money arrives.

**Before you start:**
- **A short written agreement:** what you'll deliver, by when, how many revisions, the price, and the payment terms. Even an email the client replies "agreed" to is better than nothing. (Not legal advice: have a lawyer look at a big contract.)
- **A deposit:** 30 to 50% up front for projects is common. It filters out clients who won't pay and gives you cash from day one.
- **Terms you choose:** "due on receipt" or net 14 rather than net 30 or 60, and a late fee written in.

**When you invoice:** your name and business name, the client's details, an invoice number, the date, line items, the total, the due date written out, and how to pay. The free invoice template does this and lists anything missing: {{invoice_template_url}}

**The forms:**
- A client may ask you for a **Form W-9**. It gives them your taxpayer ID. If you'd rather not hand out your Social Security number, a free EIN from irs.gov works for a sole proprietor.
- If a business pays you **$2,000 or more** in 2026 for services, it generally sends you a **1099-NEC** by early the next year. Payment apps and card processors may send a **1099-K** instead, at $20,000 and 200 transactions.
- **You report all your income either way**, form or no form.

**Do this today:** add a deposit line and your late-fee terms to your next proposal.

Hi,

The IRS charges an underpayment penalty if you don't pay enough tax during the year. But there's a rule that takes most of the guesswork out. It's called the **safe harbor**.

If you pay at least **100% of last year's total tax** (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000), in four on-time installments, you generally owe no penalty, even if this year's bill turns out bigger.

Example: last year's total tax was $6,500. Pay $1,625 by each deadline and you're covered. If this year's real bill is $12,000, you pay the difference when you file in April, without a penalty. (Which is why the set-aside habit from email 1 still matters.)

The other option is paying 90% of *this* year's tax. That's better when your income drops, but you only know this year's number if you track it.

So before every deadline, the real question is: **which is smaller right now, and how much have I already paid?**

You can do that by hand with Form 1040-ES. Or I'll show you the spreadsheet that does it for you in the next email.

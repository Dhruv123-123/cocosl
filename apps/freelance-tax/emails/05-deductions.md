Hi,

Every business expense lowers both your income tax *and* your self-employment tax. At $60,000 of profit (single), $1,000 of deductible expenses saves you about **$231** in federal tax. At $120,000, about $305.

The ones freelancers most often miss:

- **Business miles.** 72.5¢ a mile to June 30, 2026, 76¢ from July 1. 2,000 miles of client visits and supply runs is $1,450 off your profit. You need a log: date, where, why, miles.
- **Software and subscriptions** you use for work: design tools, cloud storage, scheduling, a share of your phone plan.
- **Home office**, if a space is used regularly and only for work. The simplified method is $5 a square foot, up to 300 square feet.
- **Fees.** Payment processor and platform fees are expenses. If a client paid $1,000 and you received $970, the $30 counts.
- **Health insurance premiums**, if you're not eligible for an employer plan (yours or a spouse's).

The rule for all of them: **ordinary and necessary for your business, and you have a record.** The record is what gets skipped. A bank statement line isn't enough on its own; add a note on what it was for.

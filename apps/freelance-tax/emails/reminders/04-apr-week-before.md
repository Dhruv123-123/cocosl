Hi,

**Thursday, April 15, 2027** is the big one. Three things are due the same day:

1. **Your 2026 return**, or an extension (Form 4868). An extension gives you until October 15 to *file*, not to *pay*. Any 2026 balance is still due April 15.
2. **Any balance you owe for 2026.**
3. **The 1st estimated payment for 2027**, covering January through March. When you pay it, choose **tax year 2027**.

That's why April feels expensive: you might be paying the end of last year and the start of this year at once.

**The good news for the safe harbor:** once your 2026 return is done, you know last year's total tax. A quarter of it (or of 110% of it, if your 2026 AGI was over $150,000) is a safe 2027 payment for most people.

**Last chance for 2026 savings:** April 15 is also the last day to make 2026 contributions to an IRA or HSA. A SEP IRA for 2026 can wait until you file, including an extension (up to October 15).

**The 15-minute routine:**

1. **Catch up your records.** Every client payment since last time in your income log, every business cost in your expense log.
2. **Pick your number.** The safe harbor (a quarter of last year's total tax, or 110% of it if last year's AGI was over $150,000), or 90% of this year's projected tax spread over the year. Paying the smaller one on time generally avoids the underpayment penalty. The calculator does the math: {{calculator_url}}
3. **Pay.** irs.gov/payments → Direct Pay (free) or your IRS Online Account → Estimated tax → 1040-ES. Pick the right **tax year**.
4. **Save the confirmation** and log the amount, so next time you know what you've already paid.

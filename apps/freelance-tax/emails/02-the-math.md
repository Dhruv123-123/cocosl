Hi,

If you came from a W-2 job, freelance tax feels unfair. Here's why, with a real example: $60,000 of profit, single, 2026 figures, no other income.

**1. Self-employment tax: $8,478.** That's Social Security and Medicare. An employer used to pay half; now you pay both halves. It's 15.3% on 92.35% of your profit.

**2. Income tax: $3,559.** Before income tax, you subtract half the SE tax ($4,239), the standard deduction ($16,100) and the 20% qualified business income deduction ($7,932). That leaves $31,729 of taxable income, taxed at 10% and 12%.

**Total: $12,037.** About 20% of your profit.

Two things follow from this:

- **Most of your bill is SE tax, not income tax.** That's why "I'm in the 12% bracket" leads people to save far too little.
- **Nobody withholds it.** So the IRS expects it four times a year, not once in April.

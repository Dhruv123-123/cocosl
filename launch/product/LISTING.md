## Full description

> Most budgets fail because they ask you to make 50 tiny decisions a day. This one asks for one decision a month and a 20-minute check-in once a week.
>
> **The 20-Minute Budget** splits your take-home pay into four lines: **Bills**, **Funds** (for the irregular stuff that usually wrecks budgets), **Goals** (paid first), and one **weekly spending number**. Groceries, eating out, fun: it all comes out of that one weekly number. When it's gone, the week's done. When there's some left, you choose where it goes.
>
> You get a 14-page guide that walks you through setup step by step, with a fully worked example (real numbers, a fictional person), and a spreadsheet that does all the maths for you, including a debt payoff planner that compares the avalanche and snowball methods side by side.
>
> **What's inside**
>
> - **The guide (PDF, 14 pages):** the method, a 6-step setup, a worked example, how to automate it with your bank, the weekly 20-minute routine, the monthly reset, avalanche vs snowball explained with numbers, common pitfalls and fixes, variations for irregular income and couples, an FAQ, and a printable one-page worksheet.
> - **The spreadsheet (.xlsx):** works in Excel, Google Sheets and Apple Numbers. Yellow cells are your inputs; everything else calculates itself. Comes pre-filled with the guide's example so you can see how it works before you add your own numbers.
>   - **Setup:** converts weekly, biweekly or twice-monthly pay into a monthly figure
>   - **Monthly Plan:** bills, goals and your weekly number (warns you if you've planned more than you earn)
>   - **Weekly Check-in:** type one number a week; it handles overspend and rollover
>   - **Sinking Funds:** monthly amounts and progress for irregular costs
>   - **Debt Payoff:** up to 6 debts, avalanche vs snowball, payoff month for each debt, total interest, debt-free date
>
> **Who it's for:** anyone who's tried budgeting apps and stopped using them, people early in their career who want a simple system, and anyone paying off debt who wants to see a real plan.
>
> **Who it's not for:** if you want automatic bank syncing and transaction categories, a budgeting app is a better fit. This is a deliberately low-tech system.
>
> Instant download. Educational content only, not financial advice.

## FAQ (for the listing)

**What format is it?**
A PDF guide (14 pages) and an .xlsx spreadsheet. Download instantly after purchase.

**Do I need Excel?**
No. It works in Google Sheets (free: File > Import > Upload, or open it from Google Drive) and Apple Numbers.

**Is it a subscription?**
No. One payment, yours to keep, including future updates to this edition.

**Does it connect to my bank?**
No, on purpose. You type one number a week. Nothing to sync, nothing to break, no account access.

**I'm paid every two weeks / my income varies. Will it work?**
Yes. The Setup tab converts weekly, biweekly or twice-monthly pay, and the guide has a section on irregular income.

**Will it work outside the US?**
Yes. The method works in any currency; change the currency format in the spreadsheet if you'd like a different symbol. A few references (e.g. FDIC, NCUA, NFCC) are US-specific.

**Is this financial advice?**
No. It's an educational guide and a tool for organising your own money. For advice about your specific situation, speak to a qualified professional.

**This week's money lesson**

### The job that pays you first

*Example (made up):* A text: "Hi! Remote assistant job, $25/hr, no interview. We'll mail you a $1,200 check. Keep $300 for your first week, buy $900 in gift cards for office supplies and send us the codes."

Fake job offers target students because you're looking for flexible work. The "employer" sends a check, asks you to deposit it and send part of the money somewhere else: gift cards, a payment app, crypto.

The check bounces days later, sometimes after the bank has made the money available. You owe the bank the whole amount, and the money you sent is gone.

**The big idea**

**A real job never pays you before you start and then asks you to send money back.**

**Class activity (10 minutes)**

1. **Spot the red flags** in the example as a class. (Hint: there are at least four.)
2. **Look up how real jobs hire:** an application, an interview, tax forms like the W-4. Compare that to the text.
3. **Write a reply** you could send, or decide not to reply at all and say why.

**Talk about it**

- Why do scammers like gift cards and payment apps?
- Who could you ask if you weren't sure a job offer was real?

**Exit ticket**

List two red flags of a fake job offer.

## Teacher notes

- **Standard:** Managing Risk (2021 National Standards, fraud and identity protection).
- **Timing:** 2 min slide, 6 min red flags and reply, 2 min exit ticket.
- **Answer key:** Red flags: unsolicited text, no interview, pay before work, a check for more than owed, gift cards, send codes.
- **Watch for:** A student who may already have deposited a check like this. Tell them privately to talk to a parent or guardian and their bank right away, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

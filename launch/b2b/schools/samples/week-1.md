**This week's money lesson**

### Where did my paycheck go?

*Example (made up):* Jordan works 20 hours at $15 an hour. Gross pay: $300. Social Security: $18.60. Medicare: $4.35. Federal income tax withheld: $9.00. State tax: $6.00. Net pay (what lands in the bank): $261.95.

**Gross pay** is what you earned before anything is taken out. **Net pay** is what you actually take home. The difference surprises almost everyone the first time.

Almost every paycheck has **Social Security (6.2%)** and **Medicare (1.45%)** taken out. These pay for benefits you can collect later. **Income tax** is also withheld, based on the W-4 form you fill in when you're hired. Many states take state income tax too.

**The big idea**

**Plan your spending around net pay, not gross pay. The number on the job ad isn't the number in your bank.**

**Class activity (10 minutes)**

1. **Work out Jordan's take-home pay** as a class: $300 minus each line in the example. Check that you get $261.95.
2. **Now your turn:** if you earned $12 an hour for 15 hours, what would Social Security and Medicare be? (Answer in the teacher notes.)
3. **Find the W-4** on IRS.gov and look at Step 1. That's the form you'll fill in at your first job.

**Talk about it**

- Why do you think Social Security and Medicare are taken out of every paycheck, even for teenagers?
- If your take-home pay is about 87% of your gross pay, how much would you actually have from a $500 paycheck?

**Exit ticket**

Name two things taken out of a paycheck, and say what each one pays for.

## Teacher notes

- **Standard:** Earning Income (2021 National Standards for Personal Financial Education, grade 12 benchmarks on taxes and payroll deductions).
- **Timing:** 2 min slide, 6 min activity, 2 min exit ticket.
- **Answer key:** $12 × 15 = $180 gross. Social Security: $180 × 6.2% = $11.16. Medicare: $180 × 1.45% = $2.61. Income tax depends on the W-4 and other income, so the example uses made-up amounts.
- **Watch for:** Students asking what to put on their own W-4. Point them to the IRS instructions and a parent or guardian; don't advise on individual forms.

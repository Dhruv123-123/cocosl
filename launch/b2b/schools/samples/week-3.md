**This week's money lesson**

### What's in a credit score?

*Example (made up):* Sam gets a first credit card with a $500 limit, puts a $40 phone bill on it each month, and pays it in full on the due date with autopay. A year later Sam has a short but perfect payment record.

A **credit score** is a number lenders use to guess how likely you are to pay back a loan. It can affect renting an apartment, a car loan, and sometimes the cost of insurance.

The most widely used score, FICO, says it's made from five ingredients: **payment history (35%)**, **amounts owed (30%)**, **length of credit history (15%)**, **new credit (10%)** and **credit mix (10%)**.

**The big idea**

**Paying on time, every time, is the biggest part of your score. Autopay for at least the minimum is the easiest way to protect it.**

**Class activity (10 minutes)**

1. **Rank the five ingredients** from biggest to smallest without looking, then check.
2. **Read Sam's example** and say which ingredients Sam is building, and how.
3. **Find the official free credit report site** (AnnualCreditReport.com). Why might someone your age check it?

**Talk about it**

- Why would a landlord care about your credit score?
- What's one risk of having a credit card, and one way to avoid it?

**Exit ticket**

Name the biggest ingredient in a FICO score and one habit that builds it.

**This week's money lesson**

### Needs, wants and a 10-minute budget

*Example (made up):* Maya takes home $800 a month from a part-time job. Using 50/30/20 as a starting point: $400 for needs (phone, gas, her share of groceries), $240 for wants (food out, games, concerts), $160 for savings.

A **need** is something you must pay for to live, work or go to school: housing, food, transportation, a phone you need for work. A **want** is nice to have. Some things are both: you need shoes, you want the $180 ones.

One common starting point is **50/30/20**: about half of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings. It's a rule of thumb, not a law. When rent is high, needs take more, and that's normal.

**The big idea**

**A budget is a plan for your money before you spend it, not a list of what you already spent.**

> **A note from your school:** {{SCHOOL_NOTE}}

**Class activity (10 minutes)**

1. **Sort this list into needs and wants** with a partner: bus pass, streaming service, school lunch, new phone case, car insurance, concert ticket. Some might be both: say why.
2. **Use Maya's example** to work out 50/30/20 for $600 a month.
3. **Pick one savings goal** you'd want 20% to go toward, and how many months it would take.

**Talk about it**

- Is a phone a need or a want? Does it depend on the person?
- What would you cut first if your income dropped by $100 a month?

**Exit ticket**

Write one need, one want and one thing that could be either, and explain the last one.

## Teacher notes

- **Standard:** Spending and Saving (2021 National Standards, budgeting and saving goals).
- **Timing:** 2 min slide, 6 min sort and math, 2 min exit ticket.
- **Answer key:** $600 × 50% = $300 needs; 30% = $180 wants; 20% = $120 savings.
- **Watch for:** Students whose families are under financial stress. Keep examples about made-up people, and don't ask students to share their own family's income or bills.

**This month's scam**

### The "fraud department" that is the fraud

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "This is the fraud department at your bank. We see a $2,400 charge you didn't make. To stop it, read me the code we just texted you."

The caller says they're from your bank's fraud department. They know your name and maybe part of your card number. They say someone is stealing from your account.

To "protect" you, they ask for the code your bank just texted you, or tell you to move your money to a "safe account", withdraw cash for a courier, or buy gold.

**The warning sign**

**Your bank will never ask for a code it sent you, or tell you to move your money to keep it safe.**

**What to do**

1. **Hang up.** Then call the number on the back of your card.
2. **Never share a code** sent to your phone, even with someone who says they're your bank.
3. **Never hand cash or gold to a courier**, or move money because a caller told you to.

**Also this month**

- **Caller ID can show your bank's real name.** Hang up and call back anyway.
- **Lost money?** Call your bank right away and the Elder Fraud Hotline, 1-833-372-8311.

**One thing to do this month**

Put the number on the back of your bank card into your phone as "My bank (real)".

> Questions? {{HELP_LINE}} If you're 60 or older and lost money to a scam, call the free National Elder Fraud Hotline: **1-833-372-8311**.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Has anyone had a call from someone saying they were your bank?"
- **Show:** Show the back of a bank card and the phone number on it.
- **Practise:** Practise: "I'll call you back on the number on my card." Then hang up.
- **Close with:** "Your real bank will be glad you called them back."

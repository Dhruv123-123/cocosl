**This month's scam**

### The prize you have to pay for

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "Congratulations! You've won $850,000 and a new car in the National Sweepstakes. To release your prize, send $450 for taxes and delivery today."

The call, letter or text says you've won a lottery, a sweepstakes or a car. To collect, you just need to pay taxes, fees or shipping, often by gift card, wire or cash in the mail.

Some say they're from a well-known company or a government "sweepstakes office". Once you pay, they call back with more fees.

**The warning sign**

**If you have to pay to get a prize, it isn't a prize. You can't win a contest you never entered.**

**What to do**

1. **Don't pay anything** to collect a prize, and don't give out bank or card numbers.
2. **Hang up or throw it out.** You don't need to argue or explain.
3. **Already paid?** Call your bank right away, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

**Also this month**

- **Foreign lotteries:** it's illegal for US citizens to play them. Any message about one is a scam.
- **They call back?** Scammers keep lists of people who answered. Hang up again.

**One thing to do this month**

Tell one friend this month: "You never pay to collect a real prize."

> Questions? {{HELP_LINE}} If you're 60 or older and lost money to a scam, call the free National Elder Fraud Hotline: **1-833-372-8311**.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Has anyone ever called to tell you that you won something?"
- **Show:** Show the flyer. Point to the line: real prizes are free to collect.
- **Practise:** Practise one sentence: "No thank you, I don't pay for prizes." Then hang up.
- **Close with:** "If you ever get one of these, bring it to us before you do anything."

**This month's scam**

### The pop-up that says "Call this number"

*What it looks like (made-up example):* A loud alarm, and a red box: "WARNING! Your computer has been infected. Do not turn it off. Call Microsoft Support now at 1-8XX-XXX-XXXX."

The "support agent" asks to connect to your computer to fix it. Once they're in, they may show you fake problems, charge you for a repair, or say they refunded you too much and need you to send money back.

Some versions start with a phone call instead: "This is Microsoft. Your computer is sending us errors."

**The warning sign**

**Microsoft and Apple don't put phone numbers in pop-up warnings, and they don't call you. Never let a caller on to your computer.**

**What to do**

1. **Don't call the number.** Close the browser. If it won't close, turn the computer off and back on.
2. **Never let anyone connect** to your computer unless you contacted them first, using a number you looked up yourself.
3. **Already let them in or paid?** Turn off the computer, call your bank, change your passwords from a different device, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

**Also this month**

- **"We refunded you too much"** is part of the same scam. Real companies don't ask you to send back money by gift card or wire.
- **Free help is close:** many libraries offer help with computers and phones. Ask before you call any number on your screen.

**One thing to do this month**

Ask a family member or library staff to show you how to close a stuck web page on your own computer or tablet, so you know what to do if it happens.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Has your computer ever suddenly shown a scary warning with a phone number?"
- **Show:** If you have a screen, show the flyer. Explain that the warning is just a web page, not a real virus check.
- **Practise:** Show how to close a browser tab and how to restart a computer.
- **Close with:** "If you ever see one, come and ask us before you call anyone."

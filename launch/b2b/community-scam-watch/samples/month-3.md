**This month's scam**

### "Your Social Security number has been suspended."

*What it sounds like (made-up example):* "This is an officer with the Social Security Administration. Your number was used in a crime and has been suspended. Press 1 now, or a warrant will be issued for your arrest."

Scammers pretend to be from Social Security, Medicare or the IRS. They say your number is suspended, your benefits will stop, or you'll be arrested. Then they ask you to confirm your number, or to pay a "fine".

A Medicare version offers a "new card", free medical supplies or a free test, and asks for your Medicare number.

**The warning sign**

**Real government agencies don't call to threaten you, and never ask you to pay with gift cards, wires, crypto or cash.**

**What to do**

1. **Hang up.** Don't press any buttons.
2. **Call the agency yourself** on its real number: Social Security 1-800-772-1213, Medicare 1-800-633-4227 (1-800-MEDICARE).
3. **Guard your Medicare number** like a credit card. Only give it to your own doctor, pharmacy or insurer.

**Also this month**

- **Caller ID can lie.** It can show "Social Security" or a real government number. That means nothing.
- **Letters too:** a real letter won't ask you to pay with gift cards. If you're not sure, call the agency's number above.

**One thing to do this month**

Write the real Social Security and Medicare numbers on a card by your phone. When a call says it's from them, use your card, not the number they give you.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Raise your hand if you've had a call that said it was from Social Security or Medicare." (Most hands go up.)
- **Teach:** Read the made-up example. Point out the threat, the hurry and the button to press.
- **Practise:** Everyone writes the two real numbers on a card or on the back of their sheet.
- **Close with:** "When in doubt, hang up and call the number you wrote down."

**This month's scam**

### When an online sweetheart needs money

*What it sounds like (made-up example):* "I've never felt this way about anyone, my darling. I want to come and see you, but my bank account is frozen while I'm working overseas. Could you help with the plane ticket? I'll pay you back the day I land."

Romance scammers spend weeks or months building trust, often on Facebook, dating sites or games. They say they're working far away: on an oil rig, in the military, or as a doctor overseas.

Then there's a problem only money can fix: a ticket, a medical bill, customs fees, or an investment that's "sure" to pay off.

**The warning sign**

**Someone you've only met online who soon says they love you, can never meet or video chat, and then needs money.**

**What to do**

1. **Never send money or gift cards** to someone you haven't met in person.
2. **Talk to someone you trust** before you send anything. Scammers try to keep you to themselves.
3. **Report the profile** to the site or app, and report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov or by calling 1-877-382-4357.

**Also this month**

- **Search their photo.** A family member or librarian can help you check whether the pictures belong to someone else.
- **It's not your fault.** These are professional criminals. Feeling embarrassed is normal, and telling someone is how it stops.

**One thing to do this month**

If you know someone who is lonely or newly widowed, call them this week. Scammers look for people no one else is talking to.

> Questions? {{HELP_LINE}} If you're 60 or older and lost money to a scam, call the free National Elder Fraud Hotline: **1-833-372-8311**.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Valentine's Day is this month. Today we'll talk about a scam that targets kind, caring people."
- **Teach:** Read the made-up message. Ask the group to spot the 3 warning signs.
- **Discuss:** "What would you say to a friend who told you about a new online sweetheart who needs money?" Stress kindness, not blame.
- **Close with:** Hand out the sheet. Remind everyone that the Elder Fraud Hotline (1-833-372-8311) is free.

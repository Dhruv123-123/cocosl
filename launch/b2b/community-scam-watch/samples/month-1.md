**This month's scam**

### "Grandma, it's me. I'm in trouble."

*What it sounds like (made-up example):* "Grandma, it's me. I was in a car accident and I'm at the police station. Please don't tell Mom. A lawyer is going to call you about bail."

The caller sounds upset and in a hurry. Then a "lawyer" or "officer" asks for money for bail or fees, often in cash, gift cards or a wire. Sometimes they send a courier to pick up cash at your door.

Scammers can now copy a voice from a short video online. So even if it sounds exactly like your grandchild, it may not be them.

**The warning sign**

**Hurry + secret + an unusual way to pay = a scam. Real family emergencies can wait 5 minutes while you call back.**

**What to do**

1. **Hang up.** Call your grandchild, or their parent, on the number you already have.
2. **Never pay** with gift cards, a wire transfer, cryptocurrency, or cash handed to a courier. Only scammers ask for these.
3. **Already paid?** Call your bank right away, then call the Elder Fraud Hotline at 1-833-372-8311.

**Also this month**

- **Pick a family safe word** that only your family knows. If a caller can't say it, hang up.
- **"Don't tell Mom"** is the scammer's way to stop you checking. Always tell someone.

**One thing to do this month**

Choose a family safe word this month and tell it to your children and grandchildren. Write it somewhere private, not on your phone's lock screen.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Has anyone here had a call from someone saying they were family and needed money?" Let 2 or 3 people share.
- **Teach:** Read the made-up example out loud. Ask: what were the warning signs? (Hurry, secret, unusual payment.)
- **Practise:** Have pairs practise the hang-up line: "I'm going to call you back on the number I have."
- **Close with:** Hand out the large-print sheet and ask everyone to pick a family safe word this week.

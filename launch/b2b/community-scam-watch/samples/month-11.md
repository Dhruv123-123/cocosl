**This month's scam**

### Anyone who asks for a gift card is a scammer

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "This is the sheriff's office. You missed jury duty and there's a warrant. To avoid arrest, buy $500 in gift cards and read me the numbers."

Many scams end the same way: "Go to the store, buy gift cards, and read me the numbers on the back." The caller might say they're the government, a utility, tech support, a grandchild or a new friend.

Once you share the numbers, the money is gone, and it's very hard to get back.

**The warning sign**

**No real company or government office asks you to pay with a gift card. Gift cards are for gifts.**

**What to do**

1. **Never read gift card numbers** to anyone on the phone or send a photo of them.
2. **A store clerk asks if you're being told to buy them?** Tell them the truth. They're trying to help.
3. **Already shared the numbers?** Call the company on the back of the card right away, keep the receipt, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

**Also this month**

- **Buying gift cards as presents?** Buy them from behind the counter, and check that the package hasn't been opened.
- **Keep your receipts** for any gift card you buy.

**One thing to do this month**

Write "Gift cards are for gifts" on a sticky note and put it by your phone.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "Has anyone ever been asked to pay something with a gift card?"
- **Show:** Show the back of a gift card and point out the numbers scammers ask for.
- **Practise:** Everyone says the rule together: "Gift cards are for gifts."
- **Close with:** "If someone asks you to buy gift cards, hang up and call us."

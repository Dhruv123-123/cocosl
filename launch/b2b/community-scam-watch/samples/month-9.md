**This month's scam**

### The charity that appeared overnight

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "We're collecting for hurricane families. Can you help tonight? We take gift cards or Cash App so the money gets there fastest."

After a hurricane, fire or flood, fake charities pop up fast with sad stories and names that sound real. They call, text, post on social media or knock on doors.

They push you to give right now, by gift card, wire, payment app or cash.

**The warning sign**

**Real charities are happy to wait while you check. Pressure to give right now, in cash or gift cards, is a warning sign.**

**What to do**

1. **Give to charities you already know**, or look them up at the BBB Wise Giving Alliance or Charity Navigator.
2. **Give by check or credit card**, never by gift card, wire or cash to a stranger.
3. **Not sure?** Say "I'll look you up and give online." Then do it yourself.

**Also this month**

- **Thanks you for a gift you don't remember?** That's a trick to make you give again.
- **Crowdfunding pages:** give only if you know the person who started it.

**One thing to do this month**

Pick one charity you trust this month and write down its real website, so you know where to give when something happens.

## Group leader's 10-minute talk

- **Open with:** "After a disaster on the news, has anyone called asking you to give?"
- **Show:** Show how to look up a charity on Give.org or Charity Navigator.
- **Practise:** Practise saying: "Thank you, I'll look you up and give online."
- **Close with:** "A good cause can wait 10 minutes for you to check."

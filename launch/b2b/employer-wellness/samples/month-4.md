**This month's money topic**

### Your {{RETIREMENT_PLAN}} in 10 minutes

April is Financial Literacy Month, so here's the shortest useful guide to your retirement plan. You don't need to pick investments today. You just need to know three things.

**The Rule of 72:** divide 72 by a yearly growth rate to see roughly how many years it takes money to double. At 6%, about 12 years. At 8%, about 9. That's why starting early matters more than starting big.

**The one thing to remember**

**Time does most of the work. Start, keep going, and don't cash out early.**

**Do it this month**

1. **Log in to {{BENEFITS_PORTAL}}** and check three things: what you contribute, what the match is, and how your money is invested. {{MATCH_LINE}}
2. **Look at the fees.** Your plan sends a fee disclosure each year. Small percentages add up over decades, so it's worth a look.
3. **Changing jobs one day?** Ask for a direct rollover into your new plan or an IRA instead of cashing out. Cashing out early usually means taxes and, often, a penalty.

**Good to know**

- **Add 1% when you get a raise.** You won't miss money you never got used to.
- **Try a free calculator:** the SEC's [compound interest calculator](https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator) shows what steady saving can grow to.

**Your 10-minute task**

Find your plan's beneficiary page in {{BENEFITS_PORTAL}} and check that the right person is named. It's the most-skipped step in retirement planning.

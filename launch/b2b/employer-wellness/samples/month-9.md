### The scams that come to work

Scammers send emails that look like they're from HR or payroll: "Update your direct deposit before Friday's payroll." The link goes to a fake sign-in page, and the next paycheck goes to the scammer.

Others send past-due invoices for things nobody ordered, or texts offering a remote job that asks you to deposit a check and send part of it back.

**The one thing to remember**

**Any message that changes where money goes, and is in a hurry, gets checked with a person you already know.**

**Do it this month**

1. **Never change direct deposit from an email link.** Go to {{BENEFITS_PORTAL}} yourself, or ask {{BENEFITS_CONTACT}}.
2. **Match every invoice** to a real order before it's paid, and follow the approval steps even if it says "final notice".
3. **Report it** to your IT or security team, and at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).

**Good to know**

- **Job offer that sends you a check** and asks you to send some back? That's a fake check scam.
- **Clicked or shared a password?** Tell IT right away. Fast is better than perfect.

**Your 10-minute task**

Find out who to tell at work if you click a bad link, and save their contact in your phone.

## Manager talking points

- **Say in your team huddle:** "Payroll will never ask you to update direct deposit through an email link. If you see one, forward it to IT."
- **If someone asks whether an email is real:** "When in doubt, don't click. Go to the portal yourself or call HR on a number you already have."
- **Don't:** Embarrass anyone who clicked. Fast reporting matters more.

# Compliance note: Money Made Simple at Work

**This is not legal, tax or investment advice.** It lists what an employer's HR, benefits broker or
counsel is likely to ask about, what we already do, and what stays the employer's decision. Nothing
here says the newsletter satisfies any legal requirement.

## 1. Our content rules

| Rule | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Education, not advice** | We explain how things work in general (the match, fees, rollovers, withholding). We never recommend a fund, an allocation, an account, a provider or an amount for anyone. |
| **Plan facts come from you** | The match, vesting, HSA or FSA rules, deadlines and portal names are filled in from your own wording. We never guess at plan details. |
| **Sources cited** | Each issue lists its sources: IRS, Department of Labor, CFPB, FTC, SEC. Rules of thumb are labelled as rules of thumb. |
| **No dollar limits that can go stale** | Contribution limits change every year. When an issue needs one, it comes from IRS.gov that year, with the year stated. |
| **No selling** | No affiliate links, product mentions, ads or links to our own sites in employer editions. |
| **No employee data** | We never receive employee lists, pay data or anything else about employees. You send from your own systems. |
| **Corrections the same day** | If a rule changes after delivery, we tell every client and send corrected files. |

## 2. Issues that matter for employers

### ERISA: education vs advice
- The Department of Labor's Interpretive Bulletin 96-1 ([29 CFR 2509.96-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/2509.96-1))
  describes kinds of participant **investment education** (general plan information, general
  financial and investment information, asset-allocation models, interactive materials) that, on their
  own, aren't fiduciary investment advice. Our issues stay in the "general information" lane: no
  individual recommendations.
- The line between education and advice has been the subject of DOL rulemaking and litigation more
  than once. Your counsel or plan adviser decides how your plan treats this content.
- Manager talking points tell managers **not** to recommend funds, accounts or amounts and to send
  questions to HR.

### Plan documents come first
- Anything about your plan must match the summary plan description (SPD). If the newsletter and the
  SPD ever disagree, the SPD controls; that's why plan facts come only from HR's own wording.

### Tax
- We describe IRS tools and general rules (the withholding estimator, estimated tax, free filing).
  We don't tell anyone how to file. The disclaimer says so in every issue.

### Privacy
- We hold no employee information, so there's nothing to secure, share or breach. You choose who gets
  the newsletter and how.

### Payroll scams
- Issue 2 warns employees that payroll will never ask for bank details through an email link. **HR must
  confirm that's true for your own process** before it goes out; the "From HR" note is your own text.

### Wellness program rules
- This is information only. There are no health questions, incentives or rewards attached, so the
  rules for incentive-based wellness programs shouldn't come into play. Talk to counsel if you add
  incentives.

### Accessibility
- Real text (not images), a plain-text version, alt text on every image and readable contrast. Check
  your own intranet and email templates too.

### Spanish edition (Plus)
- Translations are reviewed by a fluent speaker before delivery. Plan facts in Spanish come from your
  Spanish benefits materials, if you have them.

### Licence
- You may use, edit and rebrand each issue in your own channels while subscribed, and keep archived
  copies. The content stays ours, and we reuse the research (not your brand) for other clients.

## 3. Fact-check list (before every issue ships)

1. Every number has a source, a year and a publish date.
2. No individual recommendation: no "you should invest in", "put X% in", or named funds or providers.
3. Plan-specific statements come only from brand-file tokens supplied by HR.
4. Every link goes to a .gov site or the employer's own site.
5. Tax-law references checked against IRS.gov that month.
6. No unfilled `{{TOKENS}}` (the build refuses).
7. Read it once out loud. If it sounds like a salesperson or a lawyer, rewrite it.

## AI disclosure

Every issue's footer says: "Drafted with AI help and fact-checked by a person." That's true only because
a person checks each issue against its sources before delivery. Your organization may reword the line
(`ai_disclosure` in your brand settings), but it can't be removed: if it's left empty, ours is used.

# Tax season lead-in: Freelancer Money Notes and 1099 Money's tax-season push

For the accounting firms you write to from **Tue 1 Dec 2026 to early January** (`../OUTREACH_DATES.md`). It
connects the pitch to 1099 Money's tax-season series: 20 posts from **Jan 19 to Apr 14, 2027**, in
`content-factory/1099-money/tax-season/POSTS.md`. **Nothing here has been sent.** Dhruv sends every email by hand.

## Why it helps

- A firm's freelance clients start asking the same questions in January: "Do I still get a 1099?", "What
  can I deduct?", "Two payments on April 15?". The tax-season series answers those in public. Freelancer
  Money Notes gives the firm the same plain-English answers under its own name, so the questions come
  to the firm instead of to a search engine.
- It gives you a true, specific reason to write in December, before firms close their inbox for tax season.
- It lets you lead with something free: the **2027 Freelancer Tax Calendar** PDF
  (`content-factory/1099-money/b2b/freelancer-tax-calendar-2027.pdf`), which firms can hand to clients.

**Be honest about the link.** Say plainly that you run 1099 Money. Only mention the series once 1099 Money
is posting publicly. Until then, use version B. Never say a firm's clients follow 1099 Money, and never
quote audience numbers you don't have.

## Email 1, tax-season version (replaces email 1 in `README.md` for Dec–Jan sends)

**Subject options:** `Your freelance clients' January questions, answered under your name` ·
`A client newsletter for tax season, ready to review`

**Version A (1099 Money is live)**

> Hi {First name},
>
> {Personal line: one true sentence from their site, e.g. they work with contractors or small businesses.}
>
> I run 1099 Money, a free tax channel for freelancers. From January 19 it runs a tax-season series on
> the questions your freelance clients are about to ask: why a missing 1099 doesn't mean missing income,
> home office, mileage, and the two payments due on April 15.
>
> I've also made a version for accounting firms: Freelancer Money Notes, a monthly client newsletter
> sent under your firm's name, with your phone number and booking link. Each issue reaches you 2 weeks
> early to review. January covers the Jan 15 payment and the new $2,000 threshold for 1099-NEC.
>
> Would a free sample be useful before the season starts? I can also send our 2027 freelancer tax
> calendar, a one-page PDF you're welcome to hand to clients.
>
> {Your name}
> {Your mailing address} · Reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.

**Version B (before 1099 Money is posting):** use version A without its second paragraph. Then start the
third paragraph with "I write a monthly newsletter that accounting firms send to their self-employed clients
under their own name:".

No link and no attachment in email 1. Send the calendar PDF and the sample when they reply.

## Follow-ups for this version

- **Follow-up 1 (day 4):** same as in `README.md`.
- **Follow-up 2 (day 10), tax-season version:** paste the 7-item "freelancer folder" checklist (post 1 in
  `POSTS.md`, the LinkedIn version, without the link line) and say: "Feel free to send that to your clients
  as it is, before they come in. If you'd like one like it every month with your branding, the first 3
  firms get 2 months free." Sign with the same two lines. Then stop until late April.
- **Not answered by Jan 15?** Stop. Firms are in tax season until Apr 15. `../OUTREACH_DATES.md` moves the
  remaining firms to after Apr 19. Pick them up then with the April issue ("an extension gives you more time
  to file, not to pay").

## When a firm says yes in December

Its first client issue is January's (send date Jan 5). If it signs after Dec 22, start with February's
"what to bring" issue, and send January's as a free extra they can still use before Jan 15. Render it with
`python3 deliver.py freelancer-client-letter --month 2027-01 --name "..." --logo ... --set FIRM_PHONE=...`.

**This month for freelancers**

### January: one payment, and fewer forms

The fourth estimated tax payment for 2026 is due **Friday, January 15, 2027**. If you've been paying quarterly, this one closes out the year.

You'll also start getting 1099 forms from clients and payment apps. For payments made in 2026, a client generally has to send a 1099-NEC only once they've paid you **$2,000** in the year (it used to be $600), and payment apps send a 1099-K only after **$20,000 and 200 transactions**. So many freelancers will get fewer forms than last year.

**The one thing to remember**

**All your business income is taxable, whether or not a form shows up. The forms are paperwork, not permission.**

**Do it this month**

1. **Make the Jan 15 payment** if you've been paying quarterly, through [IRS.gov/payments](https://www.irs.gov/payments). Not sure what you owe? Call us at {{FIRM_PHONE}} before the 15th, not after.
2. **Start an income list** for 2026 if you don't have one: every client, every payment, from your invoices or bank deposits. When forms arrive, check them against it.
3. **Upload forms as they arrive** to {{CLIENT_PORTAL}}, so nothing's missing when we prepare your return.

**Good to know**

- **Your state may have its own estimated payment due the same day.** We'll tell you if yours does.
- **The IRS doesn't text or email you out of the blue** about your account or a refund. Delete those messages and type IRS.gov yourself.

**Your 10-minute task**

Make a folder (paper or on your computer) called "2026 taxes" and put every 1099, receipt and statement in it as it comes.

## Notes for your team

- **Clients may ask:** "I didn't get a 1099 from one client. Do I still report it?"
- **What to say:** "Yes, all business income goes on the return. Send us your own list or bank deposits and we'll match it."

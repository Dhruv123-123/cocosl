**This month for freelancers**

### Your year-end checklist

A few minutes in December saves a lot of time in January. If you paid other contractors for your business, you may need to send them a Form 1099-NEC by **January 31**. For payments made in 2026 and later, that generally applies when you paid someone **$2,000 or more** in the year.

To fill in the form, you need their legal name, address and taxpayer ID from a Form W-9. It's much easier to ask now than in late January.

**The one thing to remember**

**Get a W-9 before you pay a contractor, and finish your records before the holidays.**

**Do it this month**

1. **Ask for a W-9** from any contractor you paid $2,000 or more this year, if you don't have one.
2. **Pull your records together:** statements, receipts, mileage log and 1099s you expect to receive.
3. **Your last 2027 estimated payment** is due January 15. We'll confirm the amount.

**Good to know**

- **Thinking of buying equipment** before year-end to lower your tax? Talk to us first. It's not always the best move.
- **Paid contractors through a card or payment app?** Those payments are usually reported by the payment company, not by you. Ask us.

## Notes for your team

- **Clients may ask:** "Do I need to send a 1099 to my web designer?"
- **What to say:** "If you paid them $2,000 or more this year for your business, and not by card or payment app, you probably do. Send us their W-9 and we'll check."
- **Watch for:** The 1099-NEC threshold changed to $2,000 for payments made from 2026 on. Older guidance online still says $600.

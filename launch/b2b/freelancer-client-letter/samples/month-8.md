**This month for freelancers**

### Keep a mileage log that holds up

If you drive for your business, you can usually deduct either the IRS standard mileage rate or your actual car costs. Either way, you need a record of your business miles.

A good log shows the **date, where you went, why, and how many miles**. Driving from home to a regular workplace is commuting, which generally isn't deductible. Trips to clients, supply runs and between job sites usually count.

**The one thing to remember**

**No log, no deduction. Write it down when you drive, not in April.**

**Do it this month**

1. **Pick one way to log**: a notebook in the car, a note on your phone, or a mileage app.
2. **Write down your odometer reading** now and on December 31.
3. **Keep gas, repair and insurance receipts** too. We'll compare the two methods for you.

**Good to know**

- **The IRS sets the standard mileage rate** each year. We'll use the right one for 2027.
- **Rideshare and delivery drivers:** the app's mileage figure may not match your full business miles. Your own log is better.

**Your 10-minute task**

Take a photo of your odometer today, and start logging this week's trips.

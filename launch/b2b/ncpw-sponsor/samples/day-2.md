**National Consumer Protection Week**

### A call from "the FTC" that wants money

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "This is Agent Morris, FTC badge 4471. Your Social Security number was used in a fraud case. To protect your funds, we need to verify your bank account and move your savings today."

During Consumer Protection Week, scammers like to pose as the agency that runs it. They say they're from the Federal Trade Commission, give a badge number, and claim you've won a prize, your account is at risk, or you owe money.

The FTC says it will never call you to demand money, will never threaten you with arrest, and will never promise you a prize. It won't ask you to move money, either.

**The tell**

**No government agency calls to demand payment, threaten arrest, or tell you to move your money.**

> **Our promise:** {{CU_NEVER_ASK}}

**What to do**

1. **Hang up.** Caller ID can show a real government name.
2. **Never move money or share a code** because a caller says so. Call us first at {{CU_PHONE}}.
3. **Report the call** at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).

**Quick hits**

- **Badge numbers and case numbers** are easy to make up.
- **Real agencies** contact you by mail first in most cases.

**Your 2-minute job today**

Tell one family member today: "The government never calls to make you pay or move money."

> Not sure about a call, text or email? Call us at **{{CU_PHONE}}** or the number on the back of your card.

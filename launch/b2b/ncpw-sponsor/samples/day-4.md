**National Consumer Protection Week**

### The collector who threatens jail

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "This is the legal department. You owe $1,740 on an old payday loan. If you don't pay today by debit card, a warrant will be issued for your arrest."

Real debt collectors have to follow the law. They can't threaten arrest or violence, use abusive language, or lie about who they are or what you owe. Fake collectors break all of those rules, and sometimes they're collecting a debt you don't owe at all.

Within 5 days of first contacting you, a real collector must tell you, in writing, the creditor and the amount you owe. You then have 30 days to dispute the debt.

**The tell**

**A collector who threatens arrest, refuses to give a mailing address or wants payment today by gift card or wire is a red flag.**

> **Our promise:** {{CU_NEVER_ASK}}

**What to do**

1. **Ask for the collector's name, company, address and phone.** Within 5 days a real collector must send you the creditor and the amount in writing. A fake one often won't.
2. **Don't pay or confirm bank details** on that first call.
3. **Report abusive or fake collectors** to the CFPB or at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Questions about a payment? Call us at {{CU_PHONE}}.

**Quick hits**

- **Don't recognize the debt?** You have 30 days from that notice to dispute it in writing.
- **Check your credit reports** for free at AnnualCreditReport.com to see what's really there.

**Your 2-minute job today**

Write down the questions to ask any collector: name, company, address, amount, and the original creditor.

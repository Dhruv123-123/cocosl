# Compliance note: Weekly Scam Alert for credit unions

**This is not legal advice.** It's a plain-language list of the issues a credit union's compliance
team is likely to raise, what we already do about each one, and what stays the credit union's
decision. Each credit union reviews every issue under its own policies and counsel. Nothing here
says the product makes a credit union compliant with anything.

## 1. Our content rules (what we promise)

| Rule | How it shows up in the pack |
|---|---|
| **Sources cited for every claim** | Each issue has a sources list, in the email footer and as a separate sources page. We use official sources first: FTC, FBI and IC3, IRS, CFPB, SEC, NCUA, SSA. A news story is used only for a statistic, with the date. |
| **Dated and reviewed** | Every issue records the date its facts were last checked. Statistics say which year they cover and when they were published. |
| **Never tells members to send money, codes or personal details anywhere** | Every "what to do" step sends members to the credit union's real number, the number on their card, or a .gov site they type in themselves. |
| **No promises about getting money back** | We say "the sooner we know, the more we can try to do". We never say "you're covered", "you'll be reimbursed" or "you're protected". |
| **Made-up examples, clearly labelled** | Every example message is marked "made-up example". We don't show real company logos or real phone numbers in example messages. Government agencies (like the IRS) are named because they're the ones being imitated. |
| **No ads, affiliate links or tracking** | Credit union editions never contain Scam Decoded products, affiliate links, tracking pixels or links to our site. Links go to the credit union's own site or to .gov pages. |
| **No member data** | We never receive member lists, emails or account data. The credit union sends from its own email platform. |
| **Corrections fast** | If a source changes or we find an error, we tell every client the same day and send a corrected file. |

## 2. Issues that matter for credit unions

### NCUA advertising rule (12 CFR Part 740)
- **Accuracy (§740.2):** a credit union can't use advertising that's inaccurate or misleading. Our
  sourcing and "no promises" rules are aimed at this.
- **Official advertising statement (§740.5):** some advertisements must include "Federally insured by
  NCUA" (or the short form). The rule lists exemptions, including advertisements that "do not relate
  to member accounts". Whether a scam-education email counts as an advertisement that needs the
  statement is the credit union's call. **The template has a footer slot for it**
  and the sample pack fills it in by default. State-chartered credit unions
  that aren't federally insured have different disclosure rules; they should tell us what to put there.
- Source: [12 CFR 740.5, exemptions in paragraph (c)](https://federal-regs.com/title/12/part-740/740.5/).

### "We will never ask" statements
- Week 1 says the credit union will never ask a member to read back a code, give a PIN or password, or
  move money to a "safe account". **The credit union must confirm this is true for all of its own
  channels** (including its fraud team, card processor and any outsourced call centre) before it
  sends. The credit union supplies this sentence itself during setup, in its own words.

### UDAAP (unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices)
- The risk in fraud education is over-promising. We avoid words like "guaranteed", "fully protected"
  and "you won't lose money", and never describe the credit union's fraud monitoring.

### Regulation E and error resolution
- We don't describe liability limits, reporting deadlines or how disputes work, because they depend
  on the account, the payment type and the credit union's own disclosures. Members are told to call
  promptly. If a credit union wants to add its own Reg E wording, the email has room for it.

### Email law (CAN-SPAM) and member preferences
- An education email from a credit union to its members may or may not be "commercial" under
  CAN-SPAM; that's the credit union's call. The template includes an unsubscribe slot and a postal
  address slot either way, filled by the credit union's email platform.

### Privacy (GLBA) and vendor management
- We're a content vendor with **no access to member information**: nothing to share, store or
  breach. That should keep vendor due diligence light, but the credit union decides how to review us.
  We'll complete your vendor questionnaire. Files are delivered by email or a shared folder.
- NCUA has published guidance on third-party relationships; the credit union's own vendor policy
  decides what it needs from us.

### Accessibility
- Emails are real text (not one big image), with a plain-text version, alt text for every image,
  good colour contrast and readable font sizes. Credit unions that hold themselves to WCAG 2.1 AA
  should still check their own email template and website.

### Social media
- The FFIEC's social media guidance (2013) expects credit unions to manage compliance risk on social
  channels, including comments. Posting and moderating is the credit union's job; we supply the post,
  caption and alt text only.

### Text messages
- We don't send texts. If a credit union turns an alert into an SMS, consent and opt-out rules (TCPA,
  10DLC registration) are the credit union's.

### Older members and staff training
- The staff huddle brief (Standard and Plus) is a conversation aid, not a training program. It always
  says "this follows your own procedures". Don't describe it as meeting any training requirement
  (for example, the Senior Safe Act's training conditions).

### Spanish edition (Plus)
- Translations are reviewed by a fluent speaker before delivery. A credit union that offers Spanish
  materials should check its own policy on matching disclosures across languages.

### Copyright and licence
- The credit union gets a licence to use, edit and rebrand each issue in its own channels while
  subscribed, and to keep archived copies. Content stays ours; we can reuse the research (not the
  credit union's brand) for other clients. Local exclusivity (Plus) means no other client in the same
  market gets the same issue under their brand.

## 3. Our fact-check list (run before every issue ships)

1. Every statistic has a source link, a year and a publish date.
2. Every link opens, and goes to a .gov site or the credit union's own site. (No link shorteners.)
3. Every "what to do" step sends money questions to the credit union's own number.
4. Example messages are labelled made up and contain no real company logo, real phone number or
   working link.
5. No "you're covered", "guaranteed", "always", or promises about refunds.
6. Reporting addresses checked against the agency's current page (ReportFraud.ftc.gov, IC3.gov,
   IdentityTheft.gov, 7726, IRS reporting page).
7. No brand slot is left unfilled (our build tool refuses to finish if one is).
8. Read it once out loud. If it sounds like a lawyer or a salesperson, rewrite it.

## 4. Common questions

- **"Who writes this?"** Scam Decoded, an independent scam-education publisher. We aren't lawyers and
  don't give legal advice.
- **"Can we edit it?"** Yes, anything. Send edits by the Friday before the send date and we return the
  updated files within 1 business day.
- **"What if a fact changes after we approve it?"** We email you the same day with the correction.
- **"Do you use AI?"** Drafting is AI-assisted. A person checks every issue against the sources
  listed before it ships.
- **"Does this satisfy our [exam, policy, training] requirement?"** We can't say that. It's member
  education content; how you use it is up to you.

**This week's scam**

### The fake fraud department call

*What it sounds like (made-up example):* "Hi, this is the fraud department at {{CU_SHORT}}. We just stopped a $482 charge at an electronics store. To block it, I'm sending a code to your phone. Can you read it back to me?"

The caller ID may even show our name. Caller ID is easy to fake.

That code isn't for blocking anything. It's the code that lets someone sign in to your account or approve a payment. Once you read it out, they can get in. Another version asks you to move your money to a "safe account" to protect it. There is no such thing.

**The tell**

**Anyone who asks you to read back a code, give your password or PIN, or move money to keep it safe is not us.**

> **Our promise:** {{CU_NEVER_ASK}}

**What to do**

1. **Hang up.** Then call us at {{CU_PHONE}} or the number on the back of your card. If it was really us, we'll still be there.
2. **Already shared a code or moved money?** Call us right away at {{CU_PHONE}}. The sooner we know, the more we can try to do.
3. **Report it** at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). It helps track who's behind these calls.

**Quick hits**

- **A text first, then a call:** a text asks "Did you make this purchase? Reply YES or NO", then a "fraud agent" calls. Don't trust the call because of the text. Hang up and call us.
- **"Don't tell the branch":** if a caller tells you to keep it secret from our staff, that's a scam every time.

**Your 5-minute job this week**

Save {{CU_PHONE}} in your phone as "{{CU_SHORT}} (real number)", and tell one family member the rule: we never ask you to read back a code.

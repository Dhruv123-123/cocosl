**This week's scam**

### The sweetheart who needs a favor

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "I'm stuck overseas and my card isn't working here. Could you pick up two $200 gift cards for me and send me a photo of the back? I'll pay you back next week, I promise. ❤️"

Romance scammers spend weeks building trust on social media, dating apps or games. They always have a reason they can't meet or video chat: working on an oil rig, deployed overseas, a doctor abroad.

Then comes a favor only money can fix. Gift cards are a favorite because once you share the numbers on the back, the money is gone.

**The tell**

**Someone you've only met online who can't meet in person and then needs money is running a scam. Nobody real asks for a photo of the back of a gift card.**

**What to do**

1. **Never send money, gift cards or crypto** to someone you haven't met in person.
2. **About to send a wire or buy big gift cards for someone you met online?** Talk to us first at {{CU_PHONE}}. We see these every week and we won't judge.
3. **Report the profile** to the app or site, and report the scam at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).

**Quick hits**

- **Search their photo:** a reverse image search often shows the pictures belong to someone else.
- **It's not your fault.** These are organized criminals. Telling someone is how it stops.

**Your 5-minute job this week**

If someone you love is newly single or widowed, call them this week. Scammers look for people no one else is talking to.

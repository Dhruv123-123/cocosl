**This week's scam**

### The IRS isn't texting you

*What it looks like (made-up example):* "IRS: Your online account has been suspended. Verify your identity within 24 hours to release your refund: [link]"

The link opens a copy of a real sign-in page. It takes your login, then asks for a photo of your driver's license "to confirm it's you". With both, someone can file a tax return in your name or get into your real account.

**The tell**

**The IRS doesn't text or email you out of the blue about your refund or your account. Any message like this is fake, however official it looks.**

**What to do**

1. **Don't tap the link.** To check your real account, type **IRS.gov** into your browser yourself.
2. **Already entered your details?** Change that password now and follow the steps at the IRS's [Identity Theft Central](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-central). If you shared card or account numbers, call us at {{CU_PHONE}}.
3. **Report it.** Forward the text to **7726** (SPAM), then report it to the IRS using [these steps](https://www.irs.gov/help/report-fraud/report-fake-irs-treasury-or-tax-related-emails-and-messages).

**Quick hits**

- **"We sent your refund by mistake":** if a refund you didn't expect lands in your account and someone asks you to send it back to them, don't. Call us first.
- **The arrest call:** the real IRS doesn't threaten arrest or demand gift cards or crypto. Hang up.

**Your 5-minute job this week**

Get a free IRS Identity Protection PIN. It stops anyone else filing a return with your Social Security number. Start at [IRS.gov's Identity Theft Central](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-central) and make sure the address is irs.gov.

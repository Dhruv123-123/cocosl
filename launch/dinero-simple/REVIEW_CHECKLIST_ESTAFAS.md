# Dinero Simple scam series: review checklist

Every piece needs two things before it is posted. First, a fluent Spanish speaker reads every word (on screen, caption, narration) and fixes anything that sounds translated. Second, every fact listed under the piece is checked against its source. Each folder's `caption.txt` has the same facts under CHECK BEFORE POSTING.

Check these first, because getting them wrong could hurt someone:
- Immigration pieces (notario, USCIS forms, visa lottery, bond, the 6 scams carousel, the who-can-help carousel). Confirm each official URL and rule is current. Keep the wording general. Never say what someone should file or whether their case is safe. The last slide of the who-can-help carousel says the FTC form doesn't ask for immigration status, so confirm that before posting.
- Virtual kidnapping and the fake bond. Keep the tone calm and not graphic, and use the current FBI and ICE pages.
- Expected guardrail warning: the brand rules avoid 'cripto', and two pieces (the task-job Short and the 6 preguntas carousel) mention crypto as a scam payment method. That's a warning, not a promotion, so it stays, but the reviewer should confirm.
- The money mule Short. It says 'casi siempre' (almost always), not 'siempre' (always). Keep that hedge.


## Shorts (videos/01–13)

### 01. Un 'notario' en EE. UU. no es abogado
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] US notaries public cannot give legal or immigration advice (USCIS 'Avoid Scams' page)
- [ ] Only licensed attorneys and DOJ-accredited representatives of recognized organizations may give immigration legal advice
- [ ] EOIR publishes the roster of recognized organizations and accredited representatives (check current URL)
- [ ] 'Can damage your case' wording stays general; do not promise outcomes

### 02. Los formularios de inmigración son gratis
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] USCIS forms are free to download from uscis.gov
- [ ] USCIS does not accept payment by phone, email, gift card or transfer to an individual (USCIS scam guidance)
- [ ] Case status can be checked online with the receipt number

### 03. La lotería de visas: gratis y sin correos de 'ganaste'
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] Diversity Visa entry is free and only at dvprogram.state.gov
- [ ] The State Department does not notify selectees by email, letter or phone; entrants check status online with their confirmation number
- [ ] Many nationalities (including Mexico) are not eligible in a given year; do not imply everyone can enter

### 04. 'Tu familiar está detenido, paga la fianza ya'
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] ICE has an Online Detainee Locator System (locator.ice.gov)
- [ ] ICE immigration bonds are paid through ICE's official channels (CeBONDS or an ERO office), not gift cards or person-to-person apps; confirm current process
- [ ] Scenario is a fictional composite

### 05. El secuestro virtual se rompe con una palabra
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] FBI has warned about virtual kidnapping ransom schemes, including ones aimed at Spanish speakers (check the current FBI page)
- [ ] FBI PSA (Dec 2024) recommends a secret word or phrase with family against AI voice cloning
- [ ] Scenario is a fictional composite; keep tone calm, not graphic

### 06. 'Tu envío está retenido, paga para liberarlo'
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] CFPB Remittance Transfer Rule: 180 days to report an error to the provider
- [ ] Impostor 'customer support' accounts on social media are a documented scam pattern (FTC)
- [ ] Do not name any real remittance company on screen

### 07. 'Te pago por recibir y reenviar dinero'
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] FBI money mule guidance: mules can face account closures and legal consequences
- [ ] Hedge: 'casi siempre' is used, not 'siempre'

### 08. Ningún banco te pide mover tu dinero para cuidarlo
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] Caller ID can be spoofed to show a real bank's number (FCC)
- [ ] Banks do not ask customers to move money to a 'safe account' to protect it (confirm with a bank or regulator statement)

### 09. 'Te mandé un código por error, ¿me lo pasas?'
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] WhatsApp offers two-step verification (a PIN) in Settings > Account (menu path may vary by version)
- [ ] Legitimate companies don't ask you to read back one-time codes
- [ ] Do not claim WhatsApp endorses this; no logo on screen

### 10. Ningún trabajo te pide pagar para pagarte
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] FTC consumer alert (2023) on gamified 'task' job scams (check the Spanish URL)
- [ ] Task scams typically require deposits, often crypto, to unlock earnings
- [ ] Example payout amounts are fictional

### 11. 7726: el número para reenviar mensajes de estafa
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] Forwarding to 7726 reports a text to most major US carriers at no cost
- [ ] Replying (even STOP) can confirm your number is active (FTC: don't reply)
- [ ] Forward menus vary by phone and OS

### 12. $0 y 10 minutos: congela tu crédito hoy
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] Credit freezes and lifts are free at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion under federal law (since Sept 2018)
- [ ] A freeze does not affect your credit score or existing accounts
- [ ] 'About 10 minutes' is an estimate for all three bureaus
- [ ] Bureau websites are mostly in English; note this if a viewer asks

### 13. El preparador de impuestos que no firma
- [ ] Native Spanish read (screen, caption, narration)
- [ ] Paid preparers must sign the return and include their PTIN (IRS)
- [ ] IRS warns about ghost preparers who refuse to sign (Dirty Dozen list; check year)
- [ ] Taxpayers are legally responsible for what is on their return

## Title

## Short description
Enter your debts, pick avalanche, snowball or your own order, and get the month you'll be debt-free. Plus balance transfer and consolidation calculators.

## Full description
> Debt feels endless when there's no finish line. This planner gives it one: a month and a year, worked out from your real balances, rates and payments.
>
> Type in up to ten debts and your extra monthly payment. Choose **avalanche** (highest rate first, least interest), **snowball** (smallest balance first, quickest wins) or your own **custom** order. The spreadsheet rolls each paid-off minimum into the next debt automatically and shows the payoff month for every debt, your debt-free date, total interest, and how much you save compared with paying minimums only.
>
> **What's inside**
> - **Planner (.xlsx):** Plan, Balance Transfer ("is the fee worth it?"), Consolidation ("would one loan be cheaper?"), Progress (log your real balance monthly, with a chart) and a month-by-month Schedule for up to 30 years. It flags any minimum payment that doesn't cover the interest.
> - **Guide (PDF, 10 pages):** a fully worked example, how to choose a method in ten seconds, what $100, $200 or $400 extra a month actually does, a script for asking for a lower APR, how balance transfers and consolidation loans really compare, how to automate it, honest guidance on when a plan isn't enough (credit counselling, debt management plans, and the risks of settlement), and a printable payoff tracker.
>
> Instant download. Works in Excel, Google Sheets and Numbers. Educational tool, not financial advice.

## FAQ
**I already have the 20-Minute Budget. Is this different?** Yes. The budget has a simple avalanche-vs-snowball comparison for up to 6 debts. This planner adds a custom order, up to 10 debts, a payoff date per debt, minimums-only comparison, balance transfer and consolidation calculators, a progress tracker and a much deeper guide. Budget buyers get $5 off with `BUDGET5`.
**Does it include my mortgage?** You can add it, but the guide suggests leaving it out so your other debts' progress is visible.
**How accurate is it?** It uses monthly interest (APR/12). Lenders calculate daily, so expect small differences.
**Format?** .xlsx + PDF.

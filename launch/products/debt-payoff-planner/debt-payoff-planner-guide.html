The four inputs that matter

  1. Each debt's balance, APR and minimum.
  2. Your extra monthly payment.
  3. Which plan: avalanche, snowball or custom.
  4. Optional: a one-time extra payment (tax refund, bonus).
Please read

This is general education and a calculator for organizing your own plan. It is not financial, tax, legal or credit advice, and it doesn't know your situation. The example debts are made up. The planner uses a simple monthly model (interest = balance × APR ÷ 12); lenders usually charge interest daily and card minimums shrink as balances fall, so real dates can land a month or two either side. If you're behind on payments or dealing with collections, a non-profit credit counselor (in the US, an NFCC member agency) is a good, low-cost first stop, before any paid debt-relief company.

Setup

Three steps on the My Debts tab

Grab your latest statements or open each lender's app. You need three numbers per debt.

1

List every debt

Credit cards, store cards, personal loans, car loans, student loans, buy-now-pay-later plans, money owed to family if you want to plan it. Up to eight. For each one:

ColumnWhere to find it
BalanceCurrent balance on the statement or app (not the credit limit).
APR %The annual rate, e.g. 24.49. Cards often list a purchase APR; use that. A 0% promo? Enter 0, but note when it ends.
Minimum / mo"Minimum payment due". For loans, your regular monthly payment.
Custom orderOnly if you'll use the Custom plan: 1 = pay first, 2 = next… Blanks go last.

The Check column flags missing numbers and any debt whose minimum doesn't cover its monthly interest (that balance grows if you only pay the minimum).

2

Set your extra payment

The amount you can pay on top of all your minimums, every month. This is the engine of the whole plan. Even $25 moves your date. Set it to something you can keep up for a year, not your most optimistic month. If you use The 20-Minute Budget, this is the "extra debt payment" goal.

Got a tax refund or bonus coming? Put it in One-time extra payment and choose the month it arrives.

3

Pick a plan, read your date

Choose Avalanche, Snowball or Custom from the dropdown. My Debts then shows each debt's payoff month and date, the interest you'll pay on it, your debt-free month and how much sooner (and cheaper) that is than paying minimums only. Not sure which plan? Look at the Compare tab first. The next page explains the difference.

How the plan works: every debt gets its minimum, every month. All the extra goes to one target debt. When the target is paid off, its minimum doesn't disappear: it rolls into the next target along with the extra, so your payments snowball (whichever order you choose) while your total monthly payment stays the same.

Avalanche: least interest

Extra goes to the highest interest rate first. Mathematically it costs the least interest (with fixed minimums, as modeled here). The catch: if your highest-rate debt is also big, the first win can take a while.

Snowball: fastest first wins

Extra goes to the smallest balance first. You usually pay a bit more interest, but you clear whole debts sooner, and each one frees up its minimum. If motivation is what's failed you before, that's worth something real.

Custom: when life isn't a formula

Some reasons to choose your own order:

  • A 0% promo ends soon and the rate will jump.
  • Clearing one loan frees a big minimum (or a co-signer).
  • One debt is with someone you'd rather not owe.
  • You want the car paid off before it needs repairs.

Type 1, 2, 3… in Custom order and compare the cost on the Compare tab.

Three things to notice

  1. The extra payment is what matters most. Adding $250 a month (and rolling minimums forward) gets this example debt-free in 43 months instead of 119, and saves about $7,658 in interest compared with paying minimums only.
  2. Avalanche vs snowball is a smaller gap. Here snowball costs about $255 more over 43 months, and both finish in the same month. In return, snowball clears the personal loan in month 11 instead of 19. Your numbers will differ, which is why the Compare tab exists.
  3. "Minimums only" is slow even with fixed payments. And real card minimums usually shrink as the balance falls, which makes the minimums-only path slower still than shown here.

Before paying extra on a loan: check the lender applies extra to principal (some hold it as an early "next payment"), and that there's no prepayment penalty. For federal student loans, check studentaid.gov first: income-driven plans and forgiveness programs can change which loan is worth paying early.

Making it stick

Habits, pitfalls and FAQ

Keep a small buffer first

Without some cash set aside, the first surprise bill goes back on a card. Many people keep a starter emergency fund (say, one month of bills) while they attack debt.

Stop adding new debt

The plan assumes balances only go down. If you're still using a card you're paying off, consider moving that spending to debit for now.

Update once a month

On the same day each month, type in the real balances. Five minutes. Seeing the date move closer is the motivation.

Windfalls

Tax refund, bonus, side income: put it in the one-time payment box and see what it does before you decide.

Lower rates can help (read the fine print)

Balance transfers and consolidation loans can lower your rate, but often charge fees, and promo rates end. Model the new rate and fee in the planner and compare before you sign anything.

Should I invest or pay off debt?

It depends on rates, any employer retirement match and your safety buffer. Paying off a 25% card is a guaranteed 25% "return"; a common approach is to clear high-rate debt before investing beyond any match. That's general education, not advice for your situation.

Why doesn't my lender's payoff date match?

Lenders compute interest daily and minimums change. Treat the planner as a close estimate and update balances monthly.

Can I use it in Google Sheets?

Yes. File → Import → Upload, or open it from Google Drive. Numbers works too.

It says "30+ years". What's wrong?

A minimum is smaller than that debt's monthly interest, or there's no extra payment and a debt never shrinks. The Check column shows which.

I have more than 8 debts.

Combine the smallest similar ones (say, two store cards) into one row with a blended APR, or plan the first eight and add the rest as they clear.

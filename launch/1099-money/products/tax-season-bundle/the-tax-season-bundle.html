Is an S corp worth it for you?

An S corp pays you a salary (with payroll taxes) and the rest as profit (without self-employment tax). It saves money only once profit is high enough to cover the salary rules and the extra costs.

From the site's calculator (lib/scorp.js): income tax, self-employment tax, both halves of payroll tax and FUTA, with the 20% QBI deduction. With salary at 40% of profit it starts to pay at about $35,000 of profit; at 60%, about $60,000.

The salary has to be reasonable for the work you do (IRS: S corporation compensation). A low salary is what makes the numbers look good and what the IRS checks. Ask a CPA what's reasonable for your field.

Single filers with profit over $200,000 (or married over $250,000) are outside this table: Additional Medicare Tax and QBI limits change the result.

Questions for your CPA

  • What salary is reasonable for my work, and how do you support it?
  • What does my state charge an S corp each year (franchise tax, fees)?
  • When would the election take effect, and what's the Form 2553 deadline for that?
  • What changes for my health insurance and retirement contributions?

If line F is small, or you aren't sure profit will stay at line A, staying a sole proprietor is usually simpler. The election is easier to make than to undo.

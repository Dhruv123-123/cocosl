## Full description

> Most freelancers set their rate by copying someone else's, then find out in April it didn't cover self-employment tax, health insurance or the weeks they couldn't bill.
>
> **The Rate & Contract Kit** starts from what you want to take home and works forward:
>
> - **Rate Calculator:** take-home, health, retirement and business costs in; profit needed after 2026 federal tax (plus a state rate you choose), revenue, hourly floor, day rate and your tax set-aside % out.
> - **Project Pricer:** list tasks, estimate hours, add a revision buffer and get a rounded project price with a deposit.
> - **Proposal template:** goal, deliverables, "not included", revisions, timeline, two options and one clear next step.
> - **Freelance Services Agreement:** 13 plain-English clauses: deposit, late fee, pausing work for overdue invoices, revisions, ownership on full payment, portfolio rights, cancellation and a liability cap.
> - **Change Order:** turn "can you also..." into paid work.
> - **Invoice + Invoice Log:** a clean invoice, and a log that flags overdue invoices and tracks how long clients take to pay.
> - **7-page guide** walking through all of it.
>
> **Who it's for:** US freelancers and solo service businesses: designers, writers, developers, marketers, photographers, consultants.
>
> **Not legal advice.** The agreement is a well-organized starting point; laws differ by state, so have a lawyer review it before you rely on it for big projects.
>

## FAQ

**Is the contract legally binding?** A signed agreement generally is, but whether every clause holds up depends on your state. Have a lawyer review it, especially the late fee and liability clauses.
**Outside the US?** The templates work as a starting point; the rate calculator's tax math is US federal only.
**Can I edit everything?** Yes. Every template is a normal .docx; the spreadsheet's yellow cells are yours.
**Does the rate calculator handle married filing jointly?** Yes, assuming no other household income.

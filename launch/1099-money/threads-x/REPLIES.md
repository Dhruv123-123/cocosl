# Reply templates for Threads/X

For replying to other people's posts about freelancing, pricing, clients and self-employed taxes. Rewrite
the first words every time so the reply answers *that* post, and fill every `[bracket]`.

**Rules**
- No links, no "check out our calculator", no mention of 1099 Money. A useful reply gets the profile click.
- Add one thing: a number, a step, a question or a different angle. One to three sentences.
- General answers only on tax questions. Say what it depends on; never tell someone what they owe.
- Reply early (within the first hour or two of a post) and to people in your audience, not other brands.
- Never reply-guy a stranger's bad news with advice they didn't ask for. Templates 16–19 are for when they ask.

## Wins

**1. First client**
> Congrats! Worth writing down now how they found you and why they said yes. That's your next five clients.

**2. Raised rates**
> Nice. The math usually holds even if a client leaves: +13% on the rate covers losing about 1 in 8 billable hours.

**3. Big month**
> Well earned. Quick reminder for future you: move the tax share out before celebrating. Roughly 20% federal at $60K profit, plus state.

## Questions people ask

**4. "What should I charge?"**
> Work backwards: take-home + health + retirement + business costs, gross up for tax, divide by hours clients actually pay for. That last number is usually 20–30 a week, not 40.

**5. "How much do I save for taxes?"**
> Depends on profit and state, but ~19–22% federal is common at $40K–$100K profit, since the 15.3% self-employment tax starts at the first dollar. Add state, round up.

**6. "Do I have to pay quarterly?"**
> If you'll owe $1,000+ for the year, generally yes: April, June, September and January. Next one is Jan 15, 2027.

**7. "Net 30 or deposit?"**
> For projects, 50% upfront is normal and nobody serious blinks. Net 30 after delivery means you finance the client for a month.

**8. "Client hasn't paid"**
> Ask for a date, not just "following up". And check whether the person you work with is the person who pays invoices. Often isn't.

**9. "Do I need an LLC?"**
> For liability, maybe. For tax, a single-member LLC is taxed like a sole proprietor by default, so it doesn't change the bill on its own.

**10. "Is this deductible?"**
> If it's ordinary and necessary for the business, generally yes, and only the business-use share if it's mixed use. Keep the receipt and a one-line note.

## When they ask for help with a struggle

**16. Slow month**
> Happens to almost everyone working solo. A couple of check-ins a week with past clients, even when busy, smooths it out over time.

**17. Overbooked**
> Might be a pricing signal. If you raised your rate enough to drop the lowest-paying client, would the numbers still work?

**18. Scope creep**
> "Happy to add that. Here's what it changes in time and price" works better than a no. Puts the decision with them.

**19. Tax shock**
> Rough, and really common in year one. A fixed % out of every payment plus the quarterly payments stops it happening twice.

## Full description

> Paying a little extra on every debt feels responsible, but it spreads your money thin. The faster way is to pay every minimum, aim all your extra at one debt, and roll its payment into the next one when it's gone. The hard part is knowing which order and when you'll be done.
>
> **The Debt Payoff Planner** does that math. Type in your debts (balance, APR, minimum) and what extra you can pay each month. It shows you:
>
> - the month each debt will be paid off, and your debt-free date
> - the total interest you'll pay, and how much you save compared with paying minimums only
> - **avalanche** (highest rate first), **snowball** (smallest balance first) and **your own custom order**, side by side
> - a month-by-month plan: exactly what to pay on each debt, every month
>
> **What's inside**
>
> - **The spreadsheet (.xlsx):** works in Excel, Google Sheets and Apple Numbers. Yellow cells are inputs; everything else calculates itself. Comes filled in with a fictional example so you can see it working first.
>   - **My Debts:** up to 8 debts, plan picker, extra monthly payment, optional one-time payment (tax refund, bonus), payoff month and interest per debt, a chart of your balances shrinking, and a check that flags any minimum too small to cover its interest
>   - **Compare:** all three orders plus "minimums only", with a balance-over-time chart
>   - **Payoff Plan:** a printable month-by-month schedule
>   - **Loan Amortization:** one fixed-payment loan, interest vs principal each month, and what an extra payment saves
> - **The guide (PDF, 8 pages):** how to set it up in 15 minutes, avalanche vs snowball vs custom explained with a worked example, how to follow the plan, habits and FAQ, and a printable debt-free tracker.
>
> **Who it's for:** anyone with two or more debts (cards, personal loans, car, student loans) who wants a clear plan and a date to aim for.
>
> **Who it's not for:** if you can't currently make your minimum payments, start with a non-profit credit counselor (in the US, an NFCC member agency) rather than a spreadsheet.
>

## FAQ (for the listing)

**What format is it?**
An .xlsx spreadsheet and an 8-page PDF guide. Instant download.

**Do I need Excel?**
No. It works in Google Sheets (File > Import > Upload, or open it from Google Drive) and Apple Numbers.

**How many debts can I enter?**
Up to 8. The guide explains how to handle more.

**Will the dates match my lender's exactly?**
Close, not exact. The planner uses a standard monthly model; lenders usually charge interest daily and card minimums shrink as balances fall. Expect real dates within a month or two. Update your balances monthly and it stays accurate.

**Which is better, avalanche or snowball?**
Avalanche usually costs less interest; snowball gives faster first wins. The planner shows the dollar difference for your debts so you can choose.

**Does it connect to my bank?**
No. You type in a few numbers. Nothing to sync, no account access.

**Is it a subscription?**
No. One payment, yours to keep.

**Is this financial advice?**
No. It's an educational tool for planning your own payments. For advice about your situation, talk to a qualified professional or a non-profit credit counselor.

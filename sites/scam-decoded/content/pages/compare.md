## First, the free stuff (do this even if you buy nothing)

1. **Freeze your credit at all three bureaus** (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). It's free by law, and it blocks most new-account fraud. You can do it for your kids too.
2. **Check your credit reports for free** at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/), the official site.
3. **Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN** at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin) so nobody can file taxes in your name.
4. **Turn on two-step sign-in** for your email and bank.
5. If it happens, [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) (from the FTC) gives you a free recovery plan.

A paid service can't freeze your credit better than you can, and it can't stop every scam. What it adds is **convenience**: alerts in one place, help with the paperwork if your identity is stolen, and insurance for some recovery costs.

## Password managers: Bitwarden vs 1Password

Most hacked accounts start with a reused password. A password manager makes a different strong password for every site and remembers them all. For most families this is the single best security purchase, and you can start for free.

| | Bitwarden | 1Password |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Open-source password manager with a free plan for unlimited passwords on all your devices. | Polished paid password manager with family plans and a free trial. |
| Good fit if | You want to start free today, or want the cheapest paid family plan. | You want the smoothest setup for a family that isn't techy. |
| Watch for | The free plan covers the basics; sharing with a whole family needs a paid plan. | No free-forever plan; set a reminder before the trial ends. |
| Free option | Yes, a free plan. | No, a free trial. |
| | {{aff:bitwarden|See Bitwarden plans}} | {{aff:1password|See 1Password plans}} |

**Our take:** start with Bitwarden's free plan today. If the family finds it fiddly, try the 1Password trial. Either is far better than reusing passwords.

## Identity theft protection: Aura vs LifeLock

These services watch for your details in places they shouldn't be (new credit, data breaches, the dark web), alert you, and help you recover if your identity is stolen. Both advertise identity theft insurance; check the limit and what it covers on the plan you're looking at.

| | Aura | LifeLock |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | An all-in-one plan: identity and credit monitoring plus device and privacy tools. | Identity and credit monitoring from Gen Digital, the company behind Norton. |
| Good fit if | You want one app that also covers antivirus, a VPN and passwords for the family. | You mainly want identity monitoring and restoration help, and may already use Norton. |
| Watch for | Plans, features and insurance limits change; check what the family plan covers for kids. | Cheaper tiers may monitor only one credit bureau; first-year prices often rise at renewal. |
| Free trial | Check the site: trials and money-back terms change. | Check the site: trials and money-back terms change. |
| | {{aff:aura|See Aura plans}} | {{aff:lifelock|See LifeLock plans}} |

**Our take:** freeze your credit first. If you then want someone watching and a team to call if it goes wrong, compare the family plans side by side, and check the renewal price, not just the first-year deal.

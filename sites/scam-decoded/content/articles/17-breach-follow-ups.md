## What it looks like

A company announces a data breach, and a few days later the follow-ups arrive. A call from your "bank's fraud team" says your account was in the breach and needs to be secured right now. A text says you're owed money from a breach settlement, after a small processing fee. An email says your details are "for sale on the dark web" and offers a scan. Each one uses the real news to sound believable.

## The tell

**The follow-up contacted you, and it wants something now: a code, a fee, a password, or a payment to "protect" you.** A real breach notice tells you what happened and points you to free steps. It doesn't ask you to pay, verify your password, or read back a code.

- **The "fraud team" call:** your bank won't ask for your password, PIN or a one-time code, and won't ask you to move money to "keep it safe".
- **The settlement text:** real class action settlements don't charge a fee to file a claim. Find the settlement through the court notice or a news story, not the link in a text.
- **The "dark web" email:** it's often a sales pitch, or a phishing link. You can check your email address for free at [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com/).
- **Paid data removal or "recovery":** you don't need to pay anyone to freeze your credit, get an IRS IP PIN, or report identity theft. Those are free.

## What to do

1. Hang up or don't reply. Contact the company yourself, using the number on your card, statement or its own website.
2. Work through our free [data breach checklist](/breached/): passwords, two-step sign-in, a credit freeze, and an IRS IP PIN if your Social Security number was exposed.
3. Follow the official steps for your type of breach at [IdentityTheft.gov/databreach](https://www.identitytheft.gov/databreach).

## Already paid or clicked?

If you read out a code or gave a password, change the password now and turn on two-step sign-in, then call the company on a number you found yourself. If you paid a "fee", call your bank or card company the same day. If someone used your information, report it at [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) for a recovery plan, and at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

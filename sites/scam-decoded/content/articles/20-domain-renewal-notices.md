<p class="kicker">Scam explainer · small business</p>

## What it looks like

A letter or email with your web address on it: *"DOMAIN NAME EXPIRATION NOTICE: yourbusiness.com. Annual website address listing and search submission, $89. Failure to pay may result in loss of your online presence."* It looks like a bill from the company that runs your domain.

The FTC has stopped senders of notices like these. It says they looked like they came from the business's real registrar, sometimes used a close variant of the name (.org instead of .com), and sold a "website address listing" with search optimization. Most people who paid got no domain registration at all.

## The tell

**It isn't from the company you pay for your domain.** Your real registrar is the one whose account you sign in to. A notice from anyone else, a different ending on your name, or words like "listing" and "search submission" in the small print are the giveaways.

## What to do

1. Sign in to your real registrar account (the one on your last receipt) and check the renewal date there.
2. Turn on auto-renew with your registrar, so a "you'll lose your domain" letter can't scare you.
3. Tell whoever opens the mail and pays bills: domain bills only get paid inside the registrar account.

## Already paid?

Call your card company or bank and dispute the payment. Report the notice at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

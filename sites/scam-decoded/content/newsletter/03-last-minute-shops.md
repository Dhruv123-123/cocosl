Hi,

Two weeks before the holidays, the ads in your feed get very good at guessing what you want. Some of those shops don't exist.

## This week's scam: the store that only exists in an ad

**Spotted this week (fictional example):** *"Last chance! Our warehouse is closing. Everything 70% off, today only. Free shipping on orders over $49. Shop now at the link."*

The site looks polished, the photos are lifted from real brands, and checkout works fine. What arrives is a cheap copy, or nothing, and the 'customer service' email never answers.

## The tell

**A shop you'd never heard of until an ad, a price that's too good, and a countdown.** Real closing-down sales don't need a timer on every page.

## What to do

1. Search the shop's name plus "scam" or "reviews" before you pay, and look for a real address and phone number.
2. Pay with a credit card, which lets you dispute the charge if nothing arrives. Avoid payment apps, wires and gift cards.
3. Already paid and nothing came? Call your card issuer and dispute it, then report the shop at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

## Quick hits

- **"Your order can't ship, confirm your card":** if the email doesn't come from the shop you actually used, ignore it and check your order in the shop's own app.
- **Gift card deals on resale sites:** cards sold at a big discount are often stolen or drained. Buy from the store or the brand.
- **Package texts are still peaking.** Track from the shop's own order email, never from a link in a text. Our [delivery text explainer](/scams/package-delivery-texts/) has the full list.

## Your 5-minute job this week

Before your next holiday order, check one thing: is the card saved in your shopping accounts a credit card, not a debit card? If something goes wrong, it's the bank's money on the line while they sort it out, not yours.

Hi,

This week's scam is backwards: it starts with someone sending you money.

## This week's scam: the "send it back" refund

**Spotted this week (fictional example):** *A $3,140 deposit labeled "IRS TREAS 310 TAX REF" lands in your account. Two days later a caller says: "That refund was issued in error. Return it to our collection partner today or you'll face penalties."*

Someone used your stolen details to file a fake return, and the refund went to your real bank account. If you "send it back" where they say, the money's gone and you still owe the IRS.

## The tell

**The IRS doesn't call to demand you send a refund to a third party.** A refund you didn't file for is a sign someone filed in your name.

## What to do

1. Don't send money anywhere a caller, email or letter tells you to.
2. Call your bank, and follow the IRS's own steps for returning an erroneous refund (IRS Tax Topic 161 on IRS.gov).
3. Report identity theft through IRS.gov, freeze your credit, and get an IP PIN for next year.

## Quick hits

- **"Settle your tax debt for pennies on the dollar":** the FTC warns some tax relief companies charge big fees up front. Check IRS payment plans and the free Taxpayer Advocate Service first.
- **Your tax software is stuck?** Don't call a number from a search result. Use the help button inside the software.
- **Real IRS letters** have a notice number in the corner. Look it up on IRS.gov's "understanding your notice" page.

## Your 5-minute job this week

Sign in to your IRS online account (type IRS.gov yourself) and look around. Knowing what your real account looks like makes the fakes easier to spot.

Forward this to whoever does the taxes in your family.

Today is Slam the Scam Day, run by Social Security's inspector general to fight government imposter scams. (Check ssa.gov for the confirmed date each year.) So this issue is a field guide.

## This week's scam: "your Social Security number has been suspended"

**Spotted this week (fictional example):** *"This is the Social Security Administration. Your Social Security number has been suspended due to suspicious activity in Texas. Press 1 to speak to an officer."*

The "officer" says your number was used in a crime, and the only way to protect your savings is to move them to a "safe account" today.

## The tell

**Social Security numbers don't get suspended.** And no real agency will ever tell you to move your money to protect it.

## What to do

1. Hang up. If you're worried, call Social Security on the number from its official website.
2. Never move money, buy gift cards or hand cash to a courier because of a call.
3. Report it at oig.ssa.gov.

## Quick hits: what the real agencies won't do

- **IRS:** won't threaten arrest or demand gift cards. It usually starts with a letter.
- **Medicare:** won't call asking for your Medicare number. Cards are free.
- **Courts:** don't call to collect fines for missed jury duty.
- **The FTC:** won't call to "protect your money" or say you're under investigation.
- **Your utility:** won't demand gift cards, crypto or a store barcode to stop a shutoff.

## Your 5-minute job this week

Agree on one family rule: **we never pay anyone who called, texted or emailed us first. We hang up and call the real number ourselves.** Then practice one line with your parents: "I don't do that on the phone. I'll call you back."

Hi,

Today is Data Privacy Day. Your information has almost certainly been in a data breach. That's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to lock the three doors that matter.

## This week's scam: the fake breach settlement

**Spotted this week (fictional example):** *"You may be owed up to $1,200 from a data breach class action settlement. Confirm your identity with your Social Security number to file your claim: breach-claims-●●●●.com"*

Real breach settlements exist, and some really do pay out. But the fake ones copy their style and ask for your Social Security number or bank details up front.

## The tell

**A settlement or breach notice that asks for your Social Security number or a fee.** Real claim forms come from a settlement administrator named in court papers, and you can find the case yourself.

## What to do

1. Search the settlement's name yourself, plus "settlement administrator", before you click anything.
2. Never pay a fee to claim money.
3. Already gave your SSN? Freeze your credit today and report it at IdentityTheft.gov.

## Quick hits

- **Real breach letter?** Change that account's password, turn on two-step sign-in, and watch for phishing that mentions the breach.
- **Tax season opens soon:** an IRS Identity Protection PIN stops others filing a return with your SSN. Get one at IRS.gov.
- **Data brokers** sell your address and phone number. Many let you opt out, one by one.

## Your 5-minute job this week

Lock three doors: (1) freeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion; (2) turn on two-step sign-in for your email; (3) put a PIN or passcode on your mobile carrier account, so nobody can move your number.

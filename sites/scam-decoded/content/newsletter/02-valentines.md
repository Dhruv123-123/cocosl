Hi,

Valentine's Day is Sunday, which makes this one of the busiest weeks of the year for romance scammers. And Tuesday was Safer Internet Day, so there's one more conversation at the end worth having.

## This week's scam: the gift card favor

**Spotted this week (fictional example):** *"Babe I'm so embarrassed to ask. My card isn't working out here and I wanted to send you something for Valentine's. Could you grab 3 gift cards and send me a photo of the backs? I'll pay you back the second I'm home 💕"*

After weeks of good-morning texts, it feels small. But that photo **is** the money: the number and PIN let them spend it in seconds, and it's almost impossible to get back.

## The tell

**Anyone you've only met online who asks for gift cards is a scammer.** The FTC is clear on this. No exceptions, however real they feel.

## What to do

1. Say it kindly, once: "I don't send money or gift cards to anyone I haven't met in person. Let's video chat this week."
2. Already sent one? Call the gift card company on the number on the card, right away, and keep the receipt.
3. Report it at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

## Quick hits

- **"Your Valentine's gift couldn't be delivered":** a $1.99 "redelivery fee" text is after your card number. Ask the sender, or check the carrier's own app.
- **The deployed soldier:** if an online partner "needs" $2,500 for leave papers, it's a scam. The US Army says soldiers don't pay to go on leave.
- **The package stuck in customs:** if they "shipped you something" and a courier wants fees, there is no package.

## Your 5-minute job this week

Send one message to a teen in your life: *"If anyone online ever threatens you with a photo, come to me. I won't be angry, and we'll fix it together."* Criminals who run sextortion count on kids being too scared to tell anyone. The FBI says don't pay, save the messages and report it. For under-18s, NCMEC's free Take It Down tool helps get images removed. If anyone feels unsafe, call or text 988.

Forward this to one person dating online right now.

Hi,

Save this email. I hope you never need it.

It happens to careful, smart people every day. These are professionals. Shame is what scammers count on to stop you acting, and **speed matters more than anything**: money can sometimes be stopped if you move fast (not always).

**In order:**

1. **Call your bank or card issuer** on the number on the back of your card. Say "I've been scammed." Ask them to stop or reverse the payment and lock the card.
2. **Call where the money went.** Gift card? Call the card company on the number on the card. Payment app or wire? Contact them directly.
3. **Change passwords, email first,** from a device the scammer never touched. If you gave someone remote access, disconnect that computer from the internet first.
4. **Freeze your credit** if you shared your Social Security number or ID.
5. **Report it** at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at IdentityTheft.gov if your identity was used (it builds you a recovery plan).
6. **Write everything down:** names, numbers, dates, amounts, how you paid.

And one warning: anyone who contacts you offering to **recover your money for a fee** is very likely the next scammer.

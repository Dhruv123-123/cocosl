It happens to careful, smart people every day. These are professionals. Shame is what scammers count on to stop you acting, and speed matters more than anything: money can sometimes be stopped if you move fast (not always).

In order:

- Call your bank or card issuer on the number on the back of your card. Say "I've been scammed." Ask them to stop or reverse the payment and lock the card.
- Call where the money went. Gift card? Call the card company on the number on the card. Payment app or wire? Contact them directly.
- Change passwords, email first, from a device the scammer never touched. If you gave someone remote access, disconnect that computer from the internet first.
- Freeze your credit if you shared your Social Security number or ID.
- Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at IdentityTheft.gov if your identity was used (it builds you a recovery plan).
- Write everything down: names, numbers, dates, amounts, how you paid.

And one warning: anyone who contacts you offering to recover your money for a fee is very likely the next scammer.

Voices can be cloned now. One word fixes it.

Hi,

The call comes late in the evening. It's your grandson's voice, crying. He's been in an accident, he's at the police station, please don't tell Mom. Then a "lawyer" takes the phone and asks for bail money, today, in cash or gift cards.

This scam is old. What's new is that scammers can clone a voice from a short clip posted online. So "it sounded exactly like him" isn't proof anymore. The FBI now recommends families agree on a secret word or phrase for exactly this.

Set it up this week:

  • Pick a word or phrase that isn't findable online (not a pet's name, street or school).
  • Say it out loud. Don't text it or post it.
  • The rule: anyone calling in a panic about money has to say it. No word, or they dodge the question? Hang up and call that person back on the number you already have.

The hardest part isn't picking the word. It's getting the whole family, especially parents and grandparents, to agree on the rules before a scary call happens. That's why I made a printable family plan. It's in the next email, if you want it.

A credit freeze is free, doesn't hurt your score, and blocks new-account fraud.

Hi,

The identity theft that costs real money is usually a thief opening a new account in your name: a credit card, a phone plan, a loan. A credit freeze stops that, because lenders can't pull your report.

Here's the whole thing:

  1. Go to Equifax, Experian and TransUnion directly. Type the addresses yourself; don't click ads.
  2. Make an account at each one and switch the freeze on.
  3. Save the three logins somewhere safe (a password manager is ideal).
  4. Applying for a loan or card later? Ask the lender which bureau it uses and lift only that one, for a day or two.

What it doesn't do: hurt your credit score, stop your existing cards working, or cost anything. Freezes and lifts are free by federal law.

Bonus round: parents can freeze a child's credit too (under 16), and two smaller agencies, Innovis and ChexSystems, offer free freezes as well.

If you do one thing this week, do this. Then do your parents'.

The Scam Decoded team

P.S. Some companies sell "identity protection". It can be useful for monitoring and recovery help, but the freeze itself is free, and it's the part that actually blocks new accounts.

How to read a link before you tap it

Scam explainer

What it looks like

Scam links borrow a real name and add to it: `usps-redelivery-help.com`, `amazon.account-verify.top`, `paypa1.com`. On a phone, you only see part of it, and a shortened link hides it completely.

The tell

Read the domain: the last two pieces before the first single slash. In `amazon.account-verify.top/login`, the real site is `account-verify.top`, not Amazon. Extra words, odd endings like .top or .xyz, numbers swapped for letters, and shortened links are all red flags.

What to do

  1. Don't tap links in texts or emails you didn't expect.
  2. Type the company's address yourself, or use its app.
  3. Paste a suspicious link into our checker to see its red flags (it never opens the link).

Already paid or clicked?

If you entered a password, change it now and turn on two-step sign-in. If you entered card details, call your card issuer. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Fake invoices: the bill for something your business never ordered

Scam explainer · small business

What it looks like

An invoice lands in the shared inbox or the mail: "FINAL NOTICE: Invoice #44871 for Search Optimization Services, $489.00, now 30 days past due. Pay today to avoid collections." The logo looks familiar, or the company sounds like one you might use. Nobody remembers ordering it, but nobody wants to be the person who let a bill go to collections either.

The FTC says these invoices are usually for things like tech support, domain registration or search engine optimization, and some come by email as phishing, to get into your accounts instead.

The tell

Nobody can point to the order. A real bill matches a purchase order, a contract or a person who asked for it. "Past due" on a first notice, an urgent deadline and a vendor nobody has used before are the giveaways.

What to do

  1. Match every invoice to a real order or contract before anyone pays it. No match, no payment.
  2. Write down who approves purchases, and tell staff that a scary "final notice" doesn't skip that step.
  3. Search the company's name with "scam", "complaint" or "review" before you pay a vendor you don't know.
  4. Got products you never ordered? The FTC says you can keep unordered merchandise for free.

Already paid?

Call your bank or card company right away and ask about stopping or disputing the payment. Report the invoice at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and forward phishing emails to reportphishing@apwg.org.

the half-price beach house

Hi,

Spring break and March Madness land in the same week, and so do two scams that look like great deals.

This week's scam: the fake vacation rental

Spotted this week (fictional example): "Hi! The beach house is still free for spring break 😊 If you book directly with me instead of through the site I can take off the fees, just send the $600 deposit by Zelle today, I have two other families asking."

The photos are often copied from a real listing. Once you pay off the platform, there's no protection, and the house might belong to someone else entirely.

The tell

A host who wants you off the booking site and paying by payment app, wire, gift card or crypto. The FTC flags all four.

What to do

  1. Reverse search the listing photos and look up the address on a map.
  2. Keep every message on the booking platform.
  3. Pay only through the platform or with a credit card, which gives you dispute rights if it turns out to be fake.

Quick hits

  • "Sure-win" March Madness picks: nobody can promise wins. If a betting app wants a fee before you can withdraw, the winnings were never real.
  • "Your power will be cut off in 30 minutes": hang up and call the number on your bill. Small businesses get this one a lot.
  • Spring clean your passwords: reused passwords are how one breach becomes five hacked accounts. A password manager fixes that.

Your 5-minute job this week

Turn on two-step sign-in for your email account. It's the key that resets every other password you have, so it's the one to lock first.

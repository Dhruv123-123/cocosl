Scam Alert · issue 19 · 2027-04-01

the IRS letter that wants a phone call

Hi,

Two weeks to Tax Day. Most people know the IRS doesn't text out of the blue, so scammers have gone back to paper.

This week's scam: the fake IRS letter

Spotted this week (fictional example): "NOTICE OF INTENT TO LEVY. Our records show a balance of $4,118.60 for tax year 2025. To avoid further action, call our Resolution Department at (202) 555-0147 within 10 days."

It looks official: an eagle, a case number, a deadline. But the phone number goes to the scammer, who "settles" the debt over the phone with a payment app, gift cards or a wire.

The tell

The only thing a fake letter can't copy is the IRS's real phone number and your real online account. If the balance isn't in your IRS Online Account, the letter isn't real.

What to do

  1. Look up the notice number (CP or LTR plus a number, usually in a corner) on IRS.gov, and sign in to your IRS Online Account to see any real balance.
  2. If you need to call, use the number on IRS.gov, not the one on the letter. Already paid? Call your bank or the gift card company today.
  3. Report fake IRS letters and messages through IRS.gov, and at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Quick hits

  • "I'll set up your IRS account for you": don't let a helper create your IRS Online Account. Set it up yourself at IRS.gov.
  • The IP PIN: a free 6-digit number that stops anyone else e-filing a federal return with your Social Security number. A new one shows up each January.
  • Free tax help is real: VITA and TCE volunteers prepare returns for free for people who qualify. Find them from IRS.gov, not a search ad.

Your 5-minute job this week

Sign in to your IRS Online Account (or create it, yourself, at IRS.gov) and look at the balance. Now you know what a real letter would say.

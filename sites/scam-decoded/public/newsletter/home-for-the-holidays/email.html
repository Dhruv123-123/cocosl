Going home for the holidays? A 30-minute plan to scam-proof your parents, and a canceled-flight text to ignore.

Hi,

If you're heading home next week, you'll be at the same table as the people scammers call most. That's the best chance all year for a short, useful conversation.

This week's scam: the canceled flight text

Spotted this week (fictional example): "Your flight has been canceled due to weather. Call our rebooking desk at 1-888-555-0161 to secure a new seat before they're gone."

The number doesn't go to the airline. A friendly 'agent' charges a rebooking fee and takes your card, and your new ticket never appears. Scammers also buy search ads for airline help lines and reply to people complaining on social media.

The tell

A rebooking number that came from a text, an ad or a stranger, not from the airline. Your real flight status is in the airline's own app.

What to do

  1. Check your flight in the airline's app or on the website you typed yourself.
  2. Need help? Use the number on your booking confirmation or in the app.
  3. Already paid a fake agent? Call your card issuer the same day and dispute the charge.

Quick hits

  • The scam talk with your parents: start with your own story ("I got the weirdest text this week…"), not a warning. Our free holiday conversation guide has the openers and a 30-minute plan.
  • Pick a family safe word while everyone's together. Anyone calling in a panic for money has to say it. No word, call them back.
  • Charity calls keep coming until December 31. Look the charity up yourself and give on its own website.

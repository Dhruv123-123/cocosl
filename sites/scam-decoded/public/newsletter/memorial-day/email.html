Before Memorial Day: pension poaching, veterans' charity calls and the free help that's real.

Hi,

Memorial Day is a week from Monday. It brings real tributes, and scams that borrow them: offers to "help" with veterans' benefits, and charity calls that sound patriotic.

This week's scam: pension poaching

Spotted this week (fictional example): "As a veteran, you may qualify for an extra $2,100 a month in VA pension. Our advisers can restructure your savings to qualify. Our one-time fee is $3,500."

The VA and its inspector general warn about this. "Advisers" move savings into annuities or trusts so a veteran appears to qualify, charge large fees, and can leave the family with locked-up money and benefits that don't come through.

The tell

Anyone charging a fee to help file a VA benefits claim, or telling you to move assets to qualify. VA-accredited representatives and veterans service organizations help for free.

What to do

  1. Get help from a VA-accredited representative or a veterans service organization. You can search for one on VA.gov.
  2. Already signed or moved money? Talk to an accredited representative before anything else changes.
  3. Report it to the VA Office of Inspector General.

Quick hits

  • Veterans' charity calls: look the charity up yourself before giving. Don't give to a caller who wants payment today.
  • "Deployed overseas, can't video call": a classic romance scam profile. Never send money to someone you haven't met.
  • A pledge isn't a payment: you don't owe anything you didn't agree to in writing.

Your 5-minute job this week

If you have a veteran in the family, send them the VA's page for finding an accredited representative. Free help is easier to say yes to when it's already in hand.

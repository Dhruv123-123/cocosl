Hi,

Data breach letters keep coming, and each big one brings a second wave: scammers who use the real news to sound believable.

This week's scam: the "fraud team" follow-up

Spotted this week (fictional example): "Hello, this is the fraud team at your bank. Your account was included in the recent data breach. To secure it, please read me the 6-digit code we just texted you."

The code is a sign-in code. Read it out, and the caller is in your account. Other versions: a breach settlement that wants a "processing fee", an email saying your data is "on the dark web, scan now", and paid offers to "remove your data".

The tell

A real breach notice tells you what happened and points to free steps. A follow-up that wants a code, a fee or a password is the scam.

What to do

- Hang up and call the number on your card or statement.
- Read out a code or a password? Change the password now, turn on two-step sign-in, and call the company. Paid a "fee"? Call your bank today.
- Follow the official steps for your type of breach at IdentityTheft.gov/databreach.

Quick hits

- Breach settlements: real ones don't charge a fee to file a claim. Find them through the court notice or a news story, not a text link.
- "Your info is on the dark web": the FTC warns about these emails. Check your email address for free at haveibeenpwned.com, typed in yourself.
- The two free locks: a credit freeze at all three bureaus, and an IRS IP PIN if your Social Security number leaked.

Your 5-minute job this week

Type haveibeenpwned.com into your browser and search your main email address. If it lists breaches you'd forgotten, change those passwords tonight.

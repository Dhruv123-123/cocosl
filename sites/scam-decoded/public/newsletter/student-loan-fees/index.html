Scam Alert · issue 17 · 2027-03-18

"your student loans qualify for forgiveness, $499 to apply"

Hi,

Student loan rules keep changing, and every change brings a wave of calls and texts offering to "handle it for you". The help is real. The fee isn't.

This week's scam: the forgiveness fee

Spotted this week (fictional example): "Final notice: you've been approved for student loan forgiveness under the new federal program. Call (877) 555-0162 today to lock in your $0 payment. One-time enrollment fee: $499."

The caller asks for an upfront fee, sometimes a monthly "maintenance" charge, and your FSA ID login so they can "manage your account". With the login, they can change your contact details so you stop hearing from your real servicer, while your loans fall behind.

The tell

A fee to apply for forgiveness or a repayment plan. You can apply for federal repayment plans and forgiveness yourself at StudentAid.gov, for free.

What to do

  1. Go to StudentAid.gov or call your loan servicer, using the number on its own website or your statement.
  2. Already shared your FSA ID? Change the password at StudentAid.gov, check your contact details, and call your servicer. Paid a fee? Call your bank or card company today.
  3. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Quick hits

  • "Receive packages, relabel, reship": a reshipping "job" moves stolen goods, and the paycheck never comes. No real job works like that.
  • A package you didn't order, with a QR code to "see who sent it": don't scan it. It's after your details.
  • Never share your FSA ID: not with a company, not with a "counselor". It's the key to your federal student aid account.

Your 5-minute job this week

Sign in to StudentAid.gov and check that the phone number and email on your account are yours. If a scammer ever gets in, that's the first thing they change.

Scam Alert · issue 25 · 2027-05-13

the job offer that comes with a check

Hi,

Graduation season means first jobs, first apartments and, for younger siblings, scholarship hunting. Scammers time it well. Forward this to a graduate.

This week's scam: the fake job with a check

Spotted this week (fictional example): "Congratulations! After reviewing your resume, we'd like to offer you a remote assistant role at $32/hour. Interview today on Telegram. We'll mail a check for your home office equipment; please send the balance to our approved vendor."

The job is fake. The check is fake too, but the bank makes the money available before it finds out. When it bounces, the graduate owes the bank everything they sent to the "vendor".

The tell

A job you didn't apply for, an interview by chat app only, and a check that comes with instructions to send money on. No real employer pays you so you can pay someone else.

What to do

  1. Look up the company yourself and contact it through its own website.
  2. Already deposited the check? Don't spend it or send any of it. Call your bank and say you think it's fake.
  3. Report the "employer" to the job site and at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Quick hits

  • Scholarships with fees: real scholarships don't charge to apply or to claim. The FAFSA is free.
  • "I'm out of the country, so I can't show you the apartment": never pay a deposit for a place nobody will let you see.
  • First paycheck: set up two-step sign-in on the new bank account and payroll login before the first deposit.

Tax Day edition: the texts that arrive after you file, and the one place to check your refund.

Hi,

It's Tax Day. If you've filed, well done. The scams don't stop today, they change: now they're about your refund.

This week's scam: the "refund adjustment" text

Spotted this week (fictional example): "IRS: Your 2026 refund of $1,284 requires an adjustment before release. Scan the code or visit irs-refund-adjust.com within 48 hours."

The link (or the QR code) leads to a lookalike site that asks for your Social Security number, date of birth and bank details. That's enough to redirect a refund, or file a fake return next year.

The tell

The IRS doesn't start contact about your refund by text or email, and refunds don't expire in 48 hours.

What to do

  1. Check your refund only at IRS.gov/refunds (or the IRS2Go app), typed in yourself.
  2. Typed your details into a fake site? Get an IP PIN now, and follow the steps at IdentityTheft.gov.
  3. Forward the text to 7726 (SPAM) and report it to phishing@irs.gov.

Quick hits

  • Need more time? An extension is free from IRS.gov. Nobody needs to charge you to file one.
  • "Tax relief, settle for pennies": high-fee "relief" companies often charge thousands up front. The IRS's own payment plans are on IRS.gov.
  • Your state refund: check it only on your state tax agency's own website.

Your 5-minute job this week

Write down the date you filed and how you'll receive your refund. If a message ever says otherwise, you'll spot it.

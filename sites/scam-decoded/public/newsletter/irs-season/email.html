This week's scam: "verify your IRS account"

Spotted this week (fictional example): "IRS: Your online account has been suspended. Verify your identity within 24 hours to release your refund: [link]"

The link opens a perfect copy of the IRS sign-in page. It takes your login, then asks for a photo of your driver's license "to confirm it's you." With both, someone can file a return in your name or get into your real account.

The tell

The IRS doesn't text or email you out of the blue about refunds or your account. So any message like this is fake, however official it looks.

What to do

  1. Delete the link. To check your real account, type IRS.gov yourself.
  2. Already entered your details? Change that password now, and follow the steps at IRS.gov's identity theft page.
  3. Forward the text to 7726 (the free spam-reporting number), then report it at IRS.gov.

Quick hits

  • The arrest call: "There's a warrant, pay today with gift cards." The real IRS doesn't threaten arrest or demand a payment method. Hang up and report it at tigta.gov.
  • State refund texts: scammers copy your state's tax department too. Check your refund on your state's official .gov site.
  • Free tool: an IRS Identity Protection PIN stops anyone else filing a return with your Social Security number.

Your 5-minute job this week

Get your IRS Identity Protection PIN. Search "IRS IP PIN" and make sure the address is irs.gov, or go there directly. It's free, and it's one of the best anti-fraud tools the IRS has.

Scam Alert · issue 6 · 2027-02-25

"Hi Mom, this is my new number"

Hi,

This one goes to parents more than anyone, so if you're the adult child, forward it up the family tree.

This week's scam: the new number text

Spotted this week (fictional example): "Hi Mom, dropped my phone in the sink 🙄 this is my new number, save it and delete the old one x" ... (small talk) ... "Are you free? I have a bill due today and I can't log into my banking on this phone. Could you pay it and I'll send it back tomorrow?"

Every step is believable on its own. Phones break, bills are due. And "delete the old number" stops you calling the real one.

The tell

A new number, plus an urgent payment, plus "I can't talk right now." That combination is the scam.

What to do

  1. Call the old number anyway. It almost always still works.
  2. Or ask a question only your child would know, or use your family safe word.
  3. If it really is them, they can wait five minutes. Real family always can.

Quick hits

  • "Unpaid tax on your payment app transactions": real 1099-K forms come from the app itself. Tax is paid through IRS.gov, never a text link.
  • AI voice clones: the same scam now comes as a phone call that sounds like your child. A safe word beats it too.
  • The QR code in a package you didn't order: don't scan it. You can keep the item.

Your 5-minute job this week

Pick a family safe word tonight: something silly and memorable, never posted online. Text it to your family group in person or by phone call, not in a group chat that could be hacked. Anyone asking for money in a hurry has to say it.

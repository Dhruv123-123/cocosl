## Money Made Simple

### r/povertyfinance: value post

**Title:** The "bill calendar" trick that stopped my surprise overdrafts (no app needed)

> Most of my overdrafts weren't from overspending. They were from bills landing in the wrong week.
>
> What fixed it:
> 1. List every bill with the **day of the month** it comes out, not just the amount.
> 2. Mark your paydays on the same list.
> 3. Group bills by which paycheck they come out of. If one paycheck carries 70% of the bills, call the
>    biggest one or two companies and ask to move the due date. Most utilities, phone and card companies
>    will do this for free; it took [me two calls].
> 4. Keep a small buffer, even $50, in checking that you pretend isn't there.
>
> The "surprise" bills (car registration, annual subscriptions) go on the same calendar with the month
> they hit. Divide by 12 and set that aside each month.
>
> Happy to help anyone lay theirs out in the comments. Just post bills and paydays, no amounts needed.

## Scam Shield / Scam Decoded

### r/Scams: answer to an "is this a scam?" toll-text post

> Yes, this is the toll-text scam that's been everywhere this year. The tells:
> - A real toll agency won't text you a link to pay a small "unpaid toll" under threat of a fine.
> - The link goes to a look-alike domain, not your state's official toll site.
> - The urgency ("pay within 24 hours") is there to stop you checking.
>
> What to do: don't tap the link. If you're worried, go to your toll account by typing the official site
> yourself, or call the number on your toll statement. If you already entered card details, call your
> card company now and ask for a new card number, and watch for follow-up "fraud department" calls,
> which are often the same scammers.

### r/freelance (on a Sunday, if Resource Sharing is running)

**Title:** How I set aside tax from every invoice so April isn't a shock

> [Rewrite with your real method.] The simple version: every time a client pays, move a fixed
> percentage to a separate savings account the same day. For a lot of US freelancers that's somewhere
> around 25-30%, but it depends on your bracket and state, so work out your own number once, from last
> year's return or an estimator.
>
> Then pay quarterly. The safe harbor rule says that if you pay at least 100% of last year's tax (110% if your
> income was over $150K), you avoid the underpayment penalty even if this year is bigger. [Confirm against
> IRS Form 1040-ES instructions for the current year.]
>
> What do others use for their percentage?

### r/doordash_drivers: answer to "do I have to pay taxes on DoorDash?"

> Yes. You'll get a 1099 if you earned over the reporting threshold, but the income is taxable either way.
> The good news is the mileage deduction. Track every delivery mile (the app's mileage isn't the full
> picture; track from when you start driving), because at the IRS standard rate [check the current-year
> rate] it often cancels out a big chunk of what you owe. Set aside a slice of each payout and pay
> quarterly if you'll owe $1,000 or more for the year.

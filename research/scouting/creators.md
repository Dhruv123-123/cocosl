## What the 20 have in common

1. **One clear identity per account.** The lawyer who reads the fine print, the Wall Street friend, the
   maths teacher, the scam-baiter. Money Made Simple's equivalent is "the friend who shows the real
   numbers". Every hook should sound like that.
2. **Hooks lean on five mechanics:** a hidden rule (fine print), a specific number, a dated restart
   ("if I started in 2027"), a mistake or myth, and a quick action ("do this tonight"). The top finance
   YouTube outliers in OutlierKit's sample use dated/restart frames, specific numbers, social comparison
   and urgent action verbs.
3. **Cadence is high and steady.** The creators with posting data post most days (Vivian Tu 12 of 14
   days; Michela Allocca and Haley Sacks most days). Our cadence caps (one or two posts a day per platform)
   fit that range without flooding.
4. **Views are a funnel, not the income.** Almost every finance creator here makes money from a book,
   course, templates, newsletter or an employer or sponsor deal. That matches our model: product + email + affiliate.
5. **Scam creators sell protection.** The biggest scam channel built its own anti-scam software. For us,
   scam-awareness content leads naturally to a free "scam checklist" lead magnet and affiliate links to
   real protection tools (credit freeze is free; identity-monitoring services are affiliate-friendly).
6. **Trust is the moat.** A 2026 study of 1,764 finance videos (692M views) found 29% contained
   misleading information, misleading videos averaged more views (556K vs 326K), and only 2.2% of
   creators held a CFP, CFA or CPA ([Yahoo Finance](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/only-2-2-financial-content-110000452.html)).
   Our fact-check gate and "boring wins" positioning are a real difference, not a handicap.

## What not to copy

- **Scam-baiting itself.** Calling scammers, accessing their systems or publishing their personal details
  carries legal risk and isn't something an automated pipeline should do. We explain the patterns
  instead and cite public reports.
- **Urgent sales wording** on course pages. The guardrails already warn on hype; keep it that way.
- **Trading tips.** The riskiest category in the 2026 study (40.6% misleading). Stay in budgeting,
  credit, basic investing and scam defence.
- **Personas that imply credentials or personal experience.** Our presenter is faceless and never says
  "I used this" (fake-testimonial guardrail).

## Watchlist

All 20 are now in [`watchlist.csv`](watchlist.csv) alongside the original accounts, with a `platform` column:

- **Instagram** rows: looked up with Instagram Business Discovery (needs `IG_USER_ID` and `IG_ACCESS_TOKEN`).
- **YouTube** rows: looked up with the YouTube Data API's `channels.list` (needs `YT_API_KEY`, 1 quota unit each).
- **TikTok** rows: skipped by the script. TikTok has no official lookup API for other accounts outside
  its academic Research API, so check those by hand.

Several handles are marked "unverified" in the CSV. If a lookup errors, fix the handle and re-run.

The hook patterns drawn from this research are in `src/contentfactory/hooks_library.json` (30 templates),
and the writer now uses them. See [`hooks.md`](hooks.md).

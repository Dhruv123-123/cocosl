# Spanish review and legal review on a bootstrap budget: what's actually available

Written 2026-11-07. **Docs only. Nobody was contacted.** Public pages only. The Dinero Simple thread puts
the native-review load at **116-175 hours**, and the coordinator has paused Dinero content and asked for a
lean launch set. This memo answers two questions. Who can review Spanish copy cheaply or for free? Who can
review immigrant-finance content (ITIN, credit without an SSN, remittances) for free?

## TL;DR

- **The free routes are mostly for nonprofits, and we're a for-profit business.** Translators without
  Borders (CLEAR Global) requires "non-governmental, non-profit legal status". University translation
  courses translate for nonprofits (University of Kentucky's SPA 423, Spring 2024). The US Department of
  Labor describes unpaid volunteer and intern exceptions for **non-profit and public** employers, not
  for-profit ones. A for-profit business with unpaid "interns" has to pass the seven-factor
  primary-beneficiary test. **So don't recruit unpaid volunteers to review a business's copy.**
- **The realistic cheap route is a paid student or entry-level reviewer, on a small lean set.** English to
  Spanish runs about **$0.06-0.09 per source word entry-level** and $0.09-0.14 mid-level for translation
  (Jobbers, Apr 2026, citing ATA, ProZ and Slator data). Editing and review run **$20-80+ an hour**. At
  $20-25 an hour, the full 116-175 hours would cost about **$2,300-4,400**, which is why the lean set
  matters. Reviewing **only the launch set** (welcome emails, the thank-you page, 10 Shorts scripts, 2
  product pages) is likely 10-20 hours, about **$200-500**. That's spending, so it's Dhruv's call.
- **Legal review: legal aid won't take this, but two cheap routes exist.**
  1. **Law school entrepreneurship and small-business clinics** give free legal help to eligible
     businesses. UW's Entrepreneurial Law Clinic, for example, has an owner income cap ($60,240 for one
     person), quarterly intake windows, and preference for low-income founders.
  2. **Bar association lawyer referral services** give a **30-minute consultation for $35** (Chicago Bar
     Association; New York State Bar), enough to check two scripts or one guide with a consumer or
     immigration lawyer.
- **The best way to cut both loads is to write less advice and link to official Spanish pages more.**
  The IRS, CFPB, FTC and USCIS publish Spanish material. Content that explains and links to them, instead
  of giving individual guidance, needs less review and carries less risk.

## 1. Spanish reviewers

| Route | Cost | Open to us? | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Translators without Borders / CLEAR Global | Free | **No**: non-profit legal status required; trade associations without charitable aims excluded | [TWB eligibility](https://translatorswithoutborders.org/partners/Eligibility/) | Only relevant to the B2B library product if a library partners with us, and even then it's the library's request, not ours |
| University translation courses (service learning) | Free to partners | **Usually no**: projects come from nonprofits and campus partners | [UK SPA 423, Sep 2024](https://uknow.uky.edu/campus-news/found-translation-students-uk-spanish-course-serve-nonprofits-through-translating) | Semester timing; students supervised by faculty |
| University translation certificate students, **paid** (via the campus job board or the program's coordinator) | About $15-25/hr (our estimate; set by the posting) | **Yes**, if paid | Programs exist at UT Arlington, CCSU, USC Upstate, Arizona and others (program pages found in search) | Good fit: they review real copy, we pay, and they get portfolio work. Ask the program coordinator, not individual students |
| Volunteer sites (Idealist, VolunteerMatch, Catchafire) | Free | **No**: built for nonprofits; Catchafire matches skilled volunteers with nonprofits | Catchafire, Idealist listings | — |
| Freelance translator or reviewer (ProZ, Upwork) | Translation $0.06-0.14/word; editing $20-80+/hr | Yes | [Jobbers rate guide, Apr 2026](https://www.jobbers.io/how-to-hire-a-freelance-translator-language-pair-rate-comparison/) (citing ATA, ProZ, Slator) | Ask for **review of existing copy by the hour**, not translation by the word. Our Spanish copy is already written. Ask for US-Latino Spanish (Mexican and Central American readers), not Spain Spanish |
| ATA-certified translator | Higher (ATA's survey is members-only) | Yes | [ATA survey summary](https://www.atanet.org/resources/summary-of-the-ata-translation-and-interpreting-services-survey/) | Only for legal-adjacent pages (the ITIN and credit guides), after the lawyer's read |

**A way to keep the cost honest:** pay for a **two-hour paid test** on the welcome series first (five
emails on PR #11). That shows the reviewer's speed per 1,000 words, and the rest of the lean set can be
priced from it.

## 2. Legal review for immigrant-finance content

| Route | Cost | Open to us? | Source | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legal aid organisations | Free | **No**: they serve low-income individuals with their own cases, not businesses' content | — | Point readers *to* them (e.g. the Immigration Advocates Network directory), don't ask them to review |
| **Law school entrepreneurship and small-business clinics** | Free | **Maybe**, if Dhruv meets the income and location rules | [UW Entrepreneurial Law Clinic eligibility](https://www.law.uw.edu/academics/experiential-learning/clinics/entrepreneurial-law/information-for-clients/client-application-and-eligibility) (income cap from $60,240 for one person; intake 1 Jul-1 Sep and 15 Oct-1 Dec); similar clinics at Texas, Idaho, Michigan and others | Good for business questions: terms of sale, disclaimers, affiliate disclosures, trademarks. **Less likely** to review immigration content line by line. Ask |
| **Bar association lawyer referral service** | **$35 for 30 minutes** | Yes | [Chicago Bar Association](https://lrs.chicagobar.org/pages/how-it-works); [New York State Bar](https://www.nysba.org/LawyerReferral/) | Ask for an immigration attorney. Bring **two specific pages** (the ITIN guide and the credit-without-SSN guide), not a whole pack |
| Law school immigration clinics | Free to clients | **No** for content review: they take individual cases | — | Same as legal aid: a referral destination for readers |

**What the reviewer should check:** that nothing reads as individual immigration advice (which only
attorneys and accredited representatives may give), that ITIN and credit statements match IRS and CFPB
pages, and that no page implies a status or benefit the reader may not qualify for.

## 3. Shrinking the review load (free)

1. **Explain and link, don't advise.** Every ITIN, tax or credit claim links to the IRS, CFPB or FTC page
   in Spanish, and the copy says "check with a lawyer or a free tax site (VITA)". Pages written this way
   need a lighter legal read.
2. **Review once, reuse many times.** Have the reviewer approve a **Spanish glossary** (presupuesto,
   fondo de emergencia, puntaje de crédito, ITIN…) and 10 standard sentences (disclaimers, CTAs,
   affiliate disclosure). The factory can reuse them across packs, so later reviews get faster.
3. **Launch lean.** That's what the Dinero thread is already doing: welcome emails, the thank-you page, a
   handful of Shorts, and the two product pages. Everything else waits for phase 2.

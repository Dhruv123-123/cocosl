# Dinero Simple: the Spanish-language money creator landscape, the gaps, and the formats that work

Written 2026-11-07 from public sources only: Pew Research, Nielsen (via its press release), press lists
of creators, the Debt.com FinTalk Awards release and public profile pages. **Docs only.** No account was
followed, messaged or scraped. Follower counts come from third-party profile pages and old articles, so
treat them as rough sizes, not facts. Where something is our judgment rather than data, it says so.

## TL;DR

1. **Who actually watches money content in Spanish in the US is mostly immigrants, especially recent
   ones.** Pew (Mar 2024): 21% of Hispanic adults get news mostly in Spanish, but that's **56% of
   immigrants who arrived in the past 10 years** and only **2% of US-born Latinos** (81% mostly English).
   Our brand file targets "jóvenes latinos de veintitantos y treinta y tantos". US-born young Latinos
   mostly watch in English (that's Money Made Simple's audience). **Aim Dinero Simple at Spanish-first
   adults, many of them newer arrivals, and their families.**
2. **The big Spanish money creators aren't about the US.** The largest accounts (Andrés Garza
   @capital_inteligente, about 8.5M on TikTok; Luis Miguel Altamirano @luisminegocios, 2M+; Gabriel García,
   1M+) are based in Mexico and Latin America and talk about CETES, AFORES and local banks. The best-known
   US-Latina money creators (Yanely Espinal, Jannese Torres, Delyanne Barros, Investing Latina) mostly
   post in **English or bilingually**, and lean towards coaching for women and investing.
3. **The gap is US systems, explained in calm Spanish, faceless and practical:** ITIN, building credit
   without a Social Security number, sending money home cheaply, taxes with an ITIN, US bank accounts, and
   scams aimed at immigrants (fake "notarios", fake IRS and immigration calls). Spanish-language news sites
   (El Cronista USA, Univision, Telemundo) write about these, but as one-off articles, not as a steady
   creator feed. That's our judgment from the search results, not a measured count.
4. **Our own content leans the other way.** Across Dinero Simple's packs on the project drive, "invertir /
   fondo / Roth / 401k" appears 326 times, and **"ITIN" 8 times**. Remittances (47), no-SSN (14) and
   scams (218, mostly the estafas pack) do better. Rebalance: one immigrant-systems post for every
   investing post.
5. **Formats that work in this space:** short vertical explainers with Spanish on-screen text; humour and
   trends (Juan Hun's style); "cuánto te cuesta" fee comparisons with real numbers; scam alerts; printables;
   and distribution through **WhatsApp Channels and Facebook groups** (we have the kits already). On
   YouTube, long videos matter more than usual: YouTube is almost **21% of Spanish speakers' TV time**
   (Nielsen), so people watch on the living-room TV, often with family.

## 1. Who the audience is (the numbers)

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hispanic adults who get news mostly in Spanish / both / mostly English | 21% / 23% / 54% | Pew, Mar 2024 |
| Immigrants who arrived in the past 10 years: mostly Spanish | **56%** (10% mostly English) | Pew |
| All Latino immigrants: mostly Spanish / both | 41% / 31% | Pew |
| US-born Latinos: mostly English / mostly Spanish | 81% / **2%** | Pew |
| Second-generation: both languages about equally | 22% | Pew |
| Streaming's share of TV time, Hispanic viewers vs the rest of the US | 55.8% vs 46% | Nielsen Diverse Intelligence Series, Dec 2025 release |
| YouTube's share of Spanish speakers' TV time | almost 21% | Nielsen |
| Spanish speakers who want more Hispanic representation in their feeds | 63% | Nielsen |
| Hispanics vs everyone else: CapCut / Linktree / AI tools use | +115% / +80% / +29% | Nielsen |

**What it means.** Spanish-language money content in the US has a real, under-served audience. But it's
older and more immigrant than "Gen Z Latino" suggests. The bilingual 23% (and 22% of the second
generation) is the bridge. They'll watch in either language, so **Spanish captions on Money Made Simple
videos, and English captions on Dinero Simple ones,** reach them cheaply.

### US-based, Spanish-first

- **Debt.com's 2026 FinTalk nominees for the best Spanish-language financial creator:** María Mercedes
  Velásquez (@soymmvelasquez, a CPA, investing for women over 40), Daniela Salazar (@neurodany), MoneyAcción
  (@moneyaccion, simple and humorous), Elizabeth Inciarte (@mamaperonoaburrida) and Xavier Serbiá
  (@xavierserbia, 20+ years on TV, formerly CNN en Español). Past winners: Juan Sánchez (2024) and Rey
  Martínez (2025). The list is short, and Debt.com sponsors the awards, so it isn't neutral. Still, it's
  the best public list of US Spanish-first money creators.
- **News sites** (El Cronista USA, El Diario NY, Univision, Telemundo) cover ITIN, credit for immigrants
  and remittances. They rank in search but don't post a steady short-video feed. **Our site and Shorts
  can answer the same questions, with sources cited.**
- **Official Spanish pages we should cite, not compete with:** CFPB en español (consumerfinance.gov/es),
  IRS en español (irs.gov/es), FTC en español (consumidor.ftc.gov).

**Nobody here is faceless and AI-voiced.** The successful accounts are personalities. That's both a risk
and an opening: a faceless brand has to win on usefulness (numbers, checklists, tools) and on looking
consistent, not on charm. Being faceless also makes the "original element" rule from the YouTube policy
review even more important for long videos.

## 4. Formats that work, and how we'd use them

| Format | Where it's proven | Our version |
|---|---|---|
| 30-45 second vertical explainer, Spanish text on screen | All the large accounts | Already the default. Keep captions big; many people watch muted |
| Humour skit or trend sound | Juan Hun, MoneyAcción | Faceless: text-message skit ("mi tía vs el banco") with a calm voiceover. Test 2 a month |
| Series (part 1 of 6) | Gabriel García's step-by-step lessons | The ITIN-to-credit series. Each part ends on the next part's question |
| "Cuánto cuesta" numbers post | Card reviews (Eduardo Rosas); fee comparisons | A remittance cost table on the 1st of each month (sourced, dated) |
| Scam alert ("si te llega este mensaje...") | FTC en español; news | Screenshot plus red callouts, the Scam Decoded signature visual |
| Long video for the TV (8-12 minutes) | YouTube is ~21% of Spanish speakers' TV time | One a fortnight on the gap topics, family-friendly, with a chart or screen recording as the original element |
| WhatsApp Channel and Facebook groups | `distribution.md` names Facebook as the strongest channel | Kits exist (`WHATSAPP_CHANNEL.md`). Share the printable and the calculator, not links to ads |
| Printables in Spanish | Our own Etsy and Gumroad catalog | Presupuesto familiar, registro de envíos: tie each series to one |

**Avoid:** "get rich" or "libertad financiera" framing (already banned in the brand file), crypto, and
**anything that reads like legal advice about immigration status.** Point to official sources and say
"consulta con un abogado de inmigración acreditado" when a topic touches status.

## 5. Suggested changes to the brand file (for the Dinero Simple thread)

1. **Audience:** "adultos latinos en EE. UU. que prefieren el español, muchos recién llegados, y sus
   familias", instead of "jóvenes de veintitantos".
2. **Pillars:** add "sistemas de EE. UU. sin rodeos (ITIN, crédito sin SSN, cuentas de banco)" and
   "mandar dinero sin perder en comisiones". Merge investing into one pillar.
3. **Seed topics:** add `itin paso a paso`, `crédito sin seguro social`, `cuánto cuesta mandar dinero`,
   `estafa del notario`, `vita impuestos gratis`.
4. **Voice:** Kokoro `em_alex` or `ef_dora` (see `pipeline-tools-round3.md`), checked by a fluent listener.
5. **Captions:** add English captions to long videos for the bilingual 23%.

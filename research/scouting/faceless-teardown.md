# Faceless money channels: a teardown of 5, and 10 changes for our brands

Researched 2026-11-07 from public tracker pages: SponsorRadar, OutlierKit, CreatorDB, vidIQ and qoruz,
plus each creator's own site where it says how they make money. Tracker numbers are **estimates**
scraped from public channel data, and sponsor lists are detected from video content, not confirmed by the
channels. Where two trackers disagree, both numbers are shown. `creators.md` already covers 20 creators who
show their faces. This file covers only **faceless** ones, which are closest to our format.

## How the 5 were picked

These are the largest money-focused channels and accounts whose videos are built around graphics and
narration rather than a presenter on camera, with numbers available from public trackers:

- **Big faceless money channels are YouTube channels.** Several are macro or business rather than strictly
  personal finance; they're included because they're the biggest and their format is ours.
- **Faceless TikTok finance couldn't be ranked from public sources.** The trackers list creators who
  show their faces. The largest faceless account found on TikTok was Personal Finance Club (156K),
  included below.
- **The Instagram faceless pages in `watchlist.csv`** (`@wealth`, `@entrepreneurshipquote`) are quote and
  meme pages with no numbers we could verify, so they're left out.

## 1. Economics Explained (2.89M)

- **Format:** macroeconomics and current events, told with charts, maps and one narrator.
- **Cadence:** about 5 a month.
- **Hooks (title style):** "How" and "Why" titles, or a question about a number. "How China Is Restarting
  Its Economy", "Can $55,000 Solve The Population Crisis?" The number in the title does the hooking.
- **Money:** ads plus **sponsors on 27 uploads in 12 months** (30 brands, 53 deals tracked). The top ones
  are Surfshark (7), Saily (5), Trading 212 (5), Ground News and Grammarly (3 each). Chime was the most
  recent, on 21 Oct 2026.
- **What we can learn:** tie a video to **this month's news**, and put a specific number in the title as a
  question. Sponsors here are mostly VPNs, eSIMs and brokers, not niche finance brands. A big audience
  attracts general consumer sponsors.
- **Don't copy:** the map-and-chart visual style or the narrator's voice. Topics are fine. The packaging
  must be ours.

## 2. How Money Works (1.8M)

- **Format:** ~15-minute narrated explainers over stock footage and simple graphics. They're about the
  money systems behind everyday life: jobs, housing, scams, countries.
- **Cadence:** about 1 a week. A second channel, "How Money Works Uncut", appears in SponsorRadar too.
- **Hooks (title style):** short and provocative, often an ellipsis or a question that sets up a paradox.
  "Your Job Achieves Nothing... (probably)" (6.1M views), "How The Wolf of Wall Street Scam Actually
  Worked" (4.8M), "If Nobody Can Afford A Home... Who's Going To Buy Them?" (4.0M). Some recent titles use
  mocking mixed case ("tHe pOpULaTiOn iS CoLlapSiNg!").
- **Money:** ads plus **40 sponsored uploads in 12 months** (52 brands, 117 deals tracked). The top ones are
  Brilliant (17), Odoo (15), **Monarch** (6, plus 3 as "Monarch Money"), MANSCAPED (5), Cape, and
  **DeleteMe** (3). The most recent was Monarch, on 24 Oct 2026.
- **What we can learn:**
  1. **Monarch and DeleteMe both sponsor faceless money channels.** Both are already on our sponsor
     target list (`youtube-monetization.md`), so this is evidence they buy this format, not just
     personal-brand creators.
  2. A "how it actually works" scam story ranks in their top 3. That's Scam Decoded's whole beat.
  3. One weekly ~15-minute video at high quality beats daily volume. They average ~850K views a video
     at about 4-5 uploads a month.
- **Don't copy:** the mixed-case mocking titles. They'd read as clickbait from a new brand, and our
  guardrails reject sensational packaging.

## 3. WhiteBoard Finance (1.03M)

- **Format:** hand-drawn whiteboard explainers voiced by the creator (Marko). The face is mostly off camera.
- **Cadence:** about 2-3 a month.
- **Hooks (title style):** a question or bold claim plus a parenthetical. "The 10-Year Treasury Just Hit 5%
  (Here's What Happens Next)", "How Much Should You REALLY Save for Your Kid's College?"
- **Money:** sponsors on dozens of videos. The trackers disagree (60 sponsored videos in one count, 32 in
  another). **Policygenius sponsors 24 of them**, then JM Bullion (7) and Gold Standard IRA (3). Recent
  ones include Seeking Alpha, Kikoff, Nexo and Perplexity.
- **The warning:** **about 1M subscribers but ~15.9K average views** a video, roughly 1.5 views per 100
  subscribers. Subscribers don't watch by default.
- **What we can learn:**
  1. **One recurring sponsor is a business.** Policygenius on 24 videos is one relationship renewed
     many times, not 24 pitches. Pitch our first sponsors as a series of slots, not one-offs.
  2. The parenthetical "(Here's What Happens Next)" hook works for dated, news-driven money topics.
  3. Measure views per video, not subscriber count.
- **Don't copy:** the whiteboard-and-hand look. It's this channel's signature (and VideoScribe-style tools
  make it common), and it would make us look like a clone.

## 4. The Swedish Investor (~1.0M)

- **Format:** animated summaries of investing books and famous investors' rules. vidIQ shows a 58-minute
  average length, which suggests long compilations alongside the regular videos.
- **Cadence:** slow, about one every 4 weeks in 2025, with the last tracked upload in June 2025.
- **Hooks (title style):** numbered lists and named authorities. "10 Money Habits Keeping You Poor",
  book-title-plus-author parentheticals, dated portfolio recaps.
- **Money:** **no sponsors detected** (SponsorRadar). vidIQ estimated **$1.8K-5.3K a month** in ads (May
  2025), which is small for 1M subscribers.
- **What we can learn:**
  1. **Evergreen book summaries keep earning views years later,** but alone they make a weak business
     at this size.
  2. The lack of sponsors or products is the cautionary part: **a big faceless channel with no offer of
     its own leaves most of its value unclaimed.** We already have products and email, so the job is
     making sure every long video points to one of them.
  3. Book summaries carry copyright and "reused content" risk if they just restate a book. Keep ours to
     official rules and our own worked numbers.
- **Don't copy:** book-summary channels as a format. Low sponsor interest, a crowded niche, and high
  reused-content risk under YouTube's rules.

## 5. Personal Finance Club (Instagram 696K, TikTok 156K)

- **Format:** clean infographic explainers and **myth-vs-math comparisons**: one chart, one claim, one
  calculation. The founder (Jeremy Schneider) appears in some videos, but the brand is built on the graphics.
- **Cadence:** low. About 1.4 posts a week on Instagram and 0.7 on TikTok (CreatorDB, Oct 2026).
  Engagement is above the category median: 2.3% Instagram, 4.2% TikTok, against a 1.5% median.
- **Hooks:** a myth stated plainly, then the numbers that break it. The top recent post has 5.9M views.
- **Money (from its own 2021 post):**
  - **Its own paid investing course** is the main income. About 2% of followers bought it, and 20% of
    course revenue goes to charity.
  - **No sponsored posts and no affiliate marketing.** It turns down "1-2 offers per day" to avoid a
    "conflict of interest".
  - Its site now shows courses, bundles and sales.

  That post is from 2021, so check whether the policy still holds before quoting it.
- **What we can learn:**
  1. **A public "how we make money" post builds trust,** and it fits our own disclosure rules.
  2. **Low volume, high quality** works on Instagram. 1.4 posts a week built a 696K account.
  3. A **myth-vs-math carousel** is the format our chart renderer is built for.
  4. **Own product first, sponsors later or never** keeps advice credible. We sell $5-49 products,
     so the model matches.
- **Don't copy:** its chart style, colours or exact myth list. Use our own brand kit and our own
  worked examples.

## What all 5 have in common

1. **Low cadence, high production.** The largest faceless money channels post **1-5 long videos a month**,
   not daily. Our 1-long-a-week cap is already in the right range, and these channels show there's no need
   to push past it.
2. **A signature visual system.** Charts and maps, whiteboard, stick-figure animation, clean
   infographics. The look is the brand. With no face, the visuals have to be recognisable.
3. **Titles built on a question, a paradox or a number,** often with a parenthetical or an ellipsis.
4. **Sponsors come from a few categories that repeat:** money apps (Monarch, Chime), privacy (DeleteMe,
   Surfshark), insurance (Policygenius), brokers (Trading 212, Seeking Alpha), learning (Brilliant). And the
   same sponsor comes back many times.
5. **The weakest earner has no product.** The Swedish Investor has neither sponsors nor an offer of its own.

## 10 changes for our brands

| 1 | **Long videos: 1 a week per channel, 12-15 minutes, with an original element.** That means a worked example with our own numbers, plus a real screen recording or a chart we draw.

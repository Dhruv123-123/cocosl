# Competitors: personal finance content (Nov 2026)

Who Money Made Simple is up against, what they do well, and where the openings are.
Follower counts come from third-party roundups (sources at the bottom) and move fast; treat them as
order-of-magnitude.

## The landscape in one paragraph

Mass-market personal finance is crowded at the top by on-camera personalities (Graham Stephan 5M+,
Humphrey Yang 3.4M TikTok / 2M YouTube, Nischa 2.2M, Mark Tilbury 8.6M). Almost none of the big winners
are pure tip-of-the-day accounts. They win with a **repeatable show format** (Caleb Hammer's audits),
a **strong persona** (Haley Sacks' pop-culture money humour), or a **specific audience** (Clever Girl
Finance for women, Delyanne for beginner investors). The faceless winners (Economics Explained 2.9M,
Alux 5.1M, Primate Economics 561K) do long-form explainers or essays, not 30-second tips, and lean on
newsletters and affiliates because a faceless brand is harder to sponsor.

## Gaps worth taking (from OutlierKit's finance-niche analysis plus our own read)

1. **Age-gated milestone content**: "what you should have by 25 / 30 / 35". Easy lane, high search.
2. **Roast / audit formats that take submissions**: "I rated your budget" without needing a guest on
   camera: viewers submit anonymised numbers, the factory renders the breakdown. This is Caleb Hammer's
   engine, made faceless and kinder (fits our calm voice).
3. **Life-stage credit card strategy** (students, new parents). Credit card content has the highest
   finance RPM ($16–26 average) but needs careful compliance.
4. **Immigrant / first-generation money guides** for US systems (credit history from zero, ITIN,
   sending money home). Medium difficulty, under-served.
5. **Spanish-language personal finance**: described as less saturated, smaller sponsor pool but higher
   funnel conversion. See `04-adjacent-niches.md`.
6. **Behavioural finance / money psychology**: growing, but weakest sponsor market. Good for reach, bad
   for revenue.

## What this means for Money Made Simple

- Keep the tip Shorts, but add **one recurring series** per week (proposed: "Rate My Budget", built from
  submissions on the link-in-bio page). Series give people a reason to follow, not just watch.
- Push harder on **long-form** (2 per month minimum). Shorts pay $0.04–0.32 RPM; a 10+ minute finance
  video pays $13–26. One long video earns what ~100x the Shorts views would.
- Our edge vs. the big names: **specific, checkable numbers** and a fact-check gate. Most viral finance
  content is vague; ours shows the math. Keep "real math" in hooks (it's also the best hook family; see
  `03-hooks-and-formats.md`).
